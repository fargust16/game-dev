# Purpose

This document defines the systemic laws governing the world itself, independent of lore. It is the design basis for world-state changes, faction behaviour, readable consequences, and world collapse. Future faction, settlement, economy, and consequence systems must depend on this document.

The document deliberately defines a lightweight, expandable model. It records owner-approved setting premises where they govern world-state design, while leaving detailed lore, named gods, spell lists, and belief/sanity mechanisms to future scope.

# First Law

> **The world seeks to preserve balance, while intelligent beings constantly disrupt it.**

This is the primary world law. The world responds to accumulated disruption, while intelligent actors pursue goals that change the conditions around them. Balance and disruption create the pressure from which the remaining systems emerge: faction activity, observable symptoms, escalating consequences, and eventually world collapse.

# Balance

Balance is a **dynamic equilibrium**, not a static or ideal state. It constantly shifts as the world and its actors change. A temporary stabilisation is possible; permanent equilibrium is not.

No Major Faction truly preserves Balance. Each pursues its own world-shaping goals and pushes the world toward possible terminal states. Minor Factions pursue bounded interests; they may support or obstruct Major Factions and change local conditions, but do not directly drive World Collapse. The player can shift, redirect, or temporarily stabilise conditions, but cannot freeze the world in place.

Balance is a hidden systemic condition. It is inferred through the world rather than displayed as a single player-facing meter.

## What changes Balance

Balance shifts primarily through **Faction Influence**.

Faction Influence for Major Factions rises when a Major Faction completes Main Goals and can also shift through Side Events and player Allegiance choices. Higher Influence makes a Major Faction harder to oppose and more able to reshape everyday life. Accumulated Faction Influence among competing Major Factions is one pressure that can break Balance into World Collapse; the sealed being's return is a distinct possible catastrophe.

# World Seed

Every world is created from a **World Seed**.

The seed determines, at minimum:

- starting world conditions;
- starting Faction Influence values;
- which Main Goals are active and their concrete details;
- Side Event generation;
- priorities used by the World Engine.

The seed must change details enough that memorised answers from a previous world do not solve the next one. Player Knowledge still transfers as methods of reading and intervening, not as a solved checklist.

# Factions

## Major and minor factions

The setting has world-shaping **Major Factions** and smaller **Minor Factions**. Major Factions have long-term goals that can directly affect world Balance and World Collapse; the existing Faction Influence and World Engine model below applies to them. Minor Factions pursue narrower needs and interests. They can be beneficial or harmful, independent or aligned with a Major Faction, and can still shape local events or help a larger side. They do not have their own world-ending objective and are not direct drivers of World Collapse. How their local actions feed into Major Faction Influence remains open.

## Owner-approved setting direction: three cooperating major factions

The world already has multiple people at Pathway Stage 0, regarded as divine-level beings. This is a setting premise, not a designed progression system. The setting premise is that supernatural power in the living world ultimately comes from ancient cosmic powers; as a practitioner advances, those powers can take greater notice and seek to reclaim what came from them. The returned antagonist is an insane Stage 0 being through which that influence can act. The exact beings, means of influence, origins of individual Pathways, and belief/anchor/sanity mechanisms remain outside current system scope.

A past cataclysm moved every Stage 0 being to the **Divine Plane**, an unreachable realm. They cannot materialise in the real world without a very great number of sacrifices, so they act through their followers. The allied gods refused to sacrifice people; the sealed being's cult chose to do it. The **Spirit World** is a separate place, the other side of the real world, which higher followers of Pathways can reach.

The **Stage Occupancy Rule** ([05 Progression](05%20Progression.md)) limits the deepest stages on every Pathway: while a Pathway's Stage 0 is occupied, that Pathway has no Stage 1; while it is unoccupied, that Pathway has at most three Stage 1 practitioners.

Three large, church-associated Major Factions form one cooperative side that the player may support. They help one another while keeping distinct roles and areas of influence. They are not the world's only factions. The primary opposing Major Faction is outlined below; its branches and other hostile groups remain future design work. The labels below describe roles only, not final faction names.

| Working role | Owner-approved direction |
|---|---|
| **Internal order and supernatural oversight** | A hierarchical, church-backed institution responsible for safety and order among practitioners. It requires everyone who gains a supernatural Pathway stage to register that stage and report to the institution to be recognized as an official practitioner. Its doctrine is that unregistered practitioners should not exist. Historically it resisted unauthorized entry into this sphere and could suppress or destroy unregistered practitioners; it has become more tolerant, while registration remains the expectation. Its forces patrol districts, cities, roads, and sometimes travel between cities or beyond them. |
| **Artifice, industry, and research** | A largely independent institution focused on creating and modifying items and artifacts, mechanisms, field research, archaeology, and recovering old artifacts and secrets. It seeks practical uses for supernatural forces, artifacts, and ingredients that can improve ordinary life. It does not generally police practitioners, but may support the first faction when needed. |
| **External and maritime security** | A publicly visible, hard-edged military-like institution focused on exploration, frontier and maritime security, sea routes, inter-island travel and supply, and small settlements outside established state control. It is comparatively tolerant of unregistered practitioners when they are not engaged in illegal activity. |

The first two factions are concentrated near one another; the third is oriented toward oceans, routes, islands, and outlying settlements. Exact borders, jurisdiction, church-to-patron mapping, and the formal shape of their cooperation remain open. Their beneficial framing does not cancel the existing Balance and Price of Power principles: each still pursues goals that change the world and may create costs or conflicts.

These role descriptions are original project placeholders. References to familiar religious structures or other fiction are comparative inspiration only; do not reuse distinctive names, symbols, doctrine, or lore from those sources.

## Political and playable-world scope

The current playable setting covers one state with several cities. A monarch and ordinary political institutions exist alongside the supernatural factions; a royal institution has been influenced by the primary opposing force, but the nature of that influence and whether it constitutes a distinct faction remain undecided. Neighbouring states exist in the wider world and may be mentioned, but their territories, systems, and simulation are outside the current playable scope.

## Primary opposing Major Faction: the Sealed Stage 0 Being

A separate, ancient Stage 0 being lost its sanity and became the object of a widespread opposing faith. It is shut away on the Divine Plane and cannot currently enter the world of living people. Its followers remain devoted. Some call it the **True God**; others call it the **False God**. These are rival in-world beliefs, not settled authorial labels: followers interpret its altered, incomprehensible and hostile state as the being's wrath against the world.

This Major Faction's central goal is to bring the being back into the living world. Its followers understand the being's hostile transformation as an expression of anger; the setting-level cause is that ancient cosmic powers influence the insane being and seek to reclaim supernatural power through it. If unopposed after returning, it first turns against other powerful beings and factions, then destroys people and eventually its own followers, whom it can sense through their connection to it. After returning, its ultimate goal is to destroy the planet through a separate world-ending ritual or comparable act, potentially sacrificing itself. This is distinct from the capital ritual that awakens it; the exact world-destruction mechanism is not defined. Three Stage 2 Cult Generals lead its field forces ([21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md)) and are the top of its living hierarchy: the being occupies Stage 0, so under the Stage Occupancy Rule its followers stop at Stage 2. Followers include practitioners at Stage 3 and at shallower stages. The faction's other branches remain open.

The player may oppose this threat before or after the being returns: disrupt the followers' plans, persuade or turn followers away, interrupt its emergence, or confront the manifested being. A successful prevention or defeat is the positive resolution of this threat. The player may join a large defence or confrontation assembled by allied factions, or keep acting elsewhere while the world responds; the confrontation is not mandatory. These are possible approaches, not a closed quest list or a prescribed moral route. The earlier idea of a mind-controlled ideal world is withdrawn and is not a current ending.

### Return, confrontation, and world destruction

The threat has distinct states: the followers prepare the return; the being begins to manifest; and, if it fully returns, it can pursue its destructive plan in the living world. The player can try to stop it at any of these points. After its return, allied faction leaders and forces may assemble to oppose it, including in a battle to defend a settlement or reach the site of its final act. The player can take part or continue other actions elsewhere while the threat advances.

If no one stops it, the being may destroy the other powerful beings and factions, then people and its own followers, before carrying out the act that destroys the planet. The player's playable story can end earlier: once the being has manifested, the player's death is defeat and no same-world successor appears, even if the being is later defeated. The game does not need to follow that character's death with a view of the planet's destruction.

The previous rough concept of the being overpowering the assembled opposition remains an unresolved balance hypothesis, not a fixed number or outcome. The capital awakening is the manifestation point; the final capital confrontation may resolve without the player. At the lowest Combat Difficulty, allied forces are expected to defeat the being without the player; at Normal, the sides are near-even with a slight enemy advantage; higher Combat Difficulty settings increasingly favour the enemy. Exact odds, force values, and which named leaders or groups take part remain open, as do effects from the other independent difficulty sliders. The later planet-destruction rite is separate from the capital awakening; its trigger and timing remain open. The relationship between a positive campaign result and the world's later finite life-cycle also remains open. Once manifestation has happened, defeating the being never restores same-world successors.

### Campaign operations and completion

The campaign's pre-manifestation stage is a war for the playable state's cities; its operating rules are in [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md). At world creation every city is under allied control and the cult holds none. Allied forces are generated then and replaced only slowly. Cult Forces generate much faster, and killing their rank and file does not exhaust them. Three Stage 2 Cult Generals lead them, each acting in a different style, and all three capture cities by conducting rituals in them. City Capture requires the cult to remove every follower of other factions from a city, by driving them out or killing them, and to hold it. Ordinary residents are not attacked: a capture ritual turns the city into a Place of Power, and they are kept alive as future sacrifices, a purpose nobody in the world knows. City Liberation is symmetrical, and cult followers may retreat instead of dying. The cult's campaign goal is the same in every world; the World Seed changes its details. The World Engine schedules these operations, and active windows can overlap. City Control replaces the earlier required ritual count and hidden return threat: the capture of cities is the cult's progress, and liberating a city undoes it. Rituals can run in parallel in two cities when the cult has gathered enough followers; whether difficulty changes this, and the cap and scheduling interval, remain open.

Each cult ritual has a current active-window target of six in-game hours (360 World Ticks). At the shared clock's initial rate, that is about one real hour during uninterrupted free exploration; paused dialogue and tactical deliberation can extend the real elapsed time. There is no player-facing numerical countdown. The window expires in world time and the operation resolves whether or not the player attended. It starts as the capture begins, and it can be stopped while it runs.

The player learns about operations through partial evidence and public reporting: daily news, rumours, notes, witnesses, ambient NPC speech, physical traces, assignments, and allied-faction briefings. These use [Information Sources](09%20Information%20Sources.md) and [Points of Interest](04%20Points%20of%20Interest.md), with their clock defined by [World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md). At each in-game day boundary, the player receives a daily summary of their own actions plus significant events known to public reporting, including ritual outcomes and local faction-control changes. The public news issue reports only events available to its publisher; other NPCs learn those events through a valid channel. The interactive map records publicly reported or personally discovered event locations and shows faction presence or control qualitatively; it never shows numeric Faction Influence or a ritual-count meter. Reported City Control is the player's reading of campaign progress: once every other city has fallen, the capital is the next target. Hidden operations become visible only through a valid information channel. A faction member may receive a direct operation briefing; a Factionless or independent character can learn through public sources and rumours. Whether a report marks an exact site or only a region depends on its source.

All three allied Major Factions receive advance intelligence about planned cult rituals and dispatch groups to disrupt them. Their forces and the cult's team converge at the operation site and act without requiring the player's presence. The player may join the allied force or ignore the confrontation. When the player is absent, the World Engine resolves the clash as a bounded faction-level operation; when the player joins, the location can become an active POI and a Confront encounter. The exact group-strength resolution and the amount by which the player's help changes it remain open. The allied factions fight for their cities but treat the rituals themselves with scepticism, because the rituals claim no civilian victims.

A ritual can be stopped by killing or capturing everyone responsible for conducting it, by killing the senior who started it, or, while the cult has not fully taken the city, by meeting conditions that differ from ritual to ritual, such as persuasion or disguise ([21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md)). Those conducting a disrupted ritual lose most of their power and try to leave. Some operations may be led by a deputy who can withdraw if they believe the site is secure. This is a possible encounter pattern, not a universal rule. Capture, custody, escape, and exact non-combat interruption rules remain open under [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md).

The **Capital Gate** governs the final step: the cult begins one final awakening ritual at the centre of the capital only when every other city is under Cult Control. This location is fixed. The ritual draws the cult's remaining forces and the allied Major Factions into the capital for the decisive confrontation; the player may join or remain elsewhere. Followers conduct smaller rituals in every captured city at the same moment, and the people of those cities and of the capital die as the sacrifice. Manifestation succeeds only if every city was captured at the moment of the capital ritual and that ritual was not interrupted; a city liberated less than a game day earlier still counts, because its Place of Power keeps its force for that day. If it completes, the being manifests there. It can no longer be delayed or contained; defeating it is the only way to end the threat. Allied gods cannot physically enter the living world, but their followers, including powerful Stage 2 practitioners, may assist. Those gods occupy Stage 0, so the allied factions have no Stage 1.

The being remains at the capital and can be challenged there after the initial clash; the player may return later while it remains undefeated. At low Combat Difficulty, allied forces may defeat it without the player's participation. If the cult wins the capital confrontation, it occupies the capital, surviving allied groups make repeated attempts to retake it, and cult forces move against any city that has been liberated. The player may help those fronts or return to challenge the being. Defeating the being ends the campaign positively even if the allied factions have suffered severe losses. Player death after manifestation remains terminal defeat with no same-world successor. Before manifestation, a successor needs at least one uncaptured city with no battle in it, so death during the final attack on the last free city is also final.

The campaign has two completion paths: kill all three Cult Generals before manifestation, which is the only way to end it then, or defeat the manifested being. Eliminating every rank-and-file follower is not required, and it cannot succeed: new followers keep appearing until the cult's leadership is destroyed. When either path completes, the campaign ends and presents a rating of the player's record. The current rating direction includes disrupted cult operations, completed official assignments, and help to ordinary people. It is a campaign summary, not character XP or a real-time threat meter; its categories and scoring remain open.

Other supernatural practitioners in the world pursue overlapping goals independently of the player and may also oppose the cult. Their autonomy, impact on ritual outcomes, and relationship to player-created campaign events remain open.

The initial difficulty direction is tied to the **Combat Difficulty** slider: at its lowest value, allied forces should be able to defeat the manifested being without the player's participation; at a middle value, the fight is close with a slight advantage to the cult; and at its highest values, the cult is increasingly favoured. Compare these points while holding the other seven sliders constant. Whether any other slider changes final-battle support or outcomes remains open. Allied strength at the capital is what survives the war for the cities, so this direction must hold for that emergent result, not only for starting values. At the lowest setting the game should win by itself: allied forces can kill all three Cult Generals without the player. Campaign pace is owned by the Cult Pace slider. This is a balance hypothesis, not a bundled difficulty preset; exact mapping and results remain open.

## Candidate beneficial Minor Factions

These are owner-provided examples of smaller groups the player could discover or support. Their working descriptions are not final names or complete system rules.

| Working role | Current concept |
|---|---|
| **Covert alchemical network** | A secretive, difficult-to-access group focused on potions, ingredients, formulas, and alchemical research. It offers work such as finding rare ingredients or lost formulas and can provide useful knowledge. It covertly shelters or organizes practitioners who operate outside the major oversight faction's law, gives them work, and helps them develop. Its head is generated as a Stage 2 or Stage 3 practitioner; never Stage 0 or Stage 1. This is a full-game world premise, not a requirement to include Stage 2 or 3 in V1; V1 remains limited to Stages 9–7. Leadership may vary between worlds. Its activities are treated as unlawful because they indirectly support unregistered practitioners and the circulation of supernatural capability. Alchemy is now a separate, potion-only profession; its rules are in [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md). |
| **Healing and medical association** | A more open group with a discoverable base. It combines ordinary medicine with research and treatment involving supernatural forces, and studies loss of sanity and ways to protect mental stability. It has both lawful and unlawful practitioners and no single unified leader. This research concerns observable practitioner health and treatment; it does not define the out-of-scope Stage 0 belief/anchor mechanism. |

Other beneficial or harmful Minor Factions may exist. Additional hostile groups that seek supernatural rule over ordinary people, reject or destroy the human world, or pursue another obscure goal are reserved for a later design pass; they are not defined factions yet.

Under the existing world-simulation model, each Major Faction owns:

- one or more Main Goals drawn from a limited pool;
- available Operations;
- current Faction Influence;
- conditions required to execute each Operation;
- access to followers, mentors, artifacts, and economic niches scaled by Influence.

Major Factions pursue Main Goals independently of the player. The player may participate, interfere, ignore, support, or betray.

## Main Goals

Major Faction Main Goals come from a **limited pool**.

The seed changes details such as:

- location;
- timing;
- involved actors;
- required conditions;
- local dressing of the same underlying goal type.

When a Major Faction completes a Main Goal:

1. its Faction Influence increases;
2. the world changes in a readable way under that Influence;
3. future Operations become easier, more frequent, or better supported for that faction.

## Side Events

Side Events are secondary, seed-generated situations. They are useful, but never free power.

In the playable world, Side Events are normally delivered as timed [Points of Interest](04%20Points%20of%20Interest.md): dormant until the player enters their area, finite in player world-time, and capable of unattended resolution if missed.

A Side Event is an Allegiance-bearing decision:

- helping one side yields one benefit;
- helping the other yields a different benefit;
- Influence shifts a little toward the supported side;
- the exact Influence shift stays hidden unless the player understands the faction affiliations involved;
- the choice may open some paths and close others.

Valid Side Event outcomes include allies, mentors, consumables, clues, artifacts, skills for followers, or Knowledge. The design rule is not "always a gift," but "always a meaningful fork with world residue."

# World Engine

The World Engine is a lightweight scheduler of consequential Major Faction actions. It does **not** simulate civilizations, populations, or every NPC decision.

At defined world intervals, the World Engine inspects each Major Faction's current Main Goal, requirements, and eligible Operations, then attempts an eligible Operation. This keeps the simulation bounded, legible, and feasible for a solo developer while allowing the world to evolve without the player.

Operations may include, for example:

- perform a ritual;
- infiltrate an organization;
- corrupt an official;
- steal an artifact;
- assassinate a target;
- spread a disease;
- create a cult;
- sabotage a location.

Every operation may:

- succeed;
- fail;
- be stopped by the player;
- be stopped by another faction;
- fail because of an observable world event.

Random world events must never function as opaque outcome rolls. They are observable conditions or events with readable effects. The player cannot always prevent them, but can learn how they alter operation outcomes and use that knowledge to predict consequences.

## Player intervention

The player may intervene in any faction confrontation or Operation if they are in the right place at the right time.

Place and time are Information problems. The player may learn them from faction followers or related NPCs through Approaches such as:

- persuasion / dialogue, affected by character traits and Knowledge;
- intimidation;
- barter of an artifact, favour, or service;
- combat, which may yield information only if the player has a method, skill, or artifact for extraction — otherwise the NPC may simply die with the secret;
- stalking and eavesdropping, if the player has a stealth-capable method, skill, or artifact;
- a **Yield Threshold**: before defeat or death, an NPC who values survival may offer the needed information in exchange for being spared.

These Approaches instantiate the Decision Engine. Combat is never the only valid route to information.

## Incremental scope

**V1:** A small number of factions, a fixed Operation set, Main Goals from a small pool, seed-driven details, simple Side Events with Allegiance forks, and one attempted Operation per interval. Every Operation or Side Event creates at least one Symptom or Readable Consequence.

**V2:** Operations and Side Events modify one another's conditions and Faction Influence more richly. Stalking, barter chains, and Yield Threshold behaviour become common tools.

**V3:** Longer Operation chains, emergent unintended faction cooperation, and denser seed variation while remaining operation-based rather than a full civilization simulation.

# Symptoms

The player never sees faction progress values or hidden Faction Influence. Instead, the player observes **Symptoms** and reads the world.

Influence spreads through everyday systems, for example:

- trade prices, quality, and availability;
- rest and services becoming restricted or faction-coloured;
- mentors appearing or leaving;
- shops closing or changing owners;
- churches, sermons, and public mood shifting;
- disappearances and corrupted locations;
- random encounters growing more frequent or dangerous at night, then later in daytime;
- otherworldly pressure becoming more likely to find the player;
- rumours, patrols, and unsafe districts.

Symptoms must be consistent enough for hypothesis-forming. They create the context-gathering phase of the Core Loop and let Knowledge improve future decisions without exposing a progress bar.

# Starting Faction and Difficulty

Only the **first character in a newly generated world** begins aligned with a **Starting Faction** and receives an initial understanding of that faction's current Main Goal and what pursuing it requires.

Later characters in the same world begin Factionless and must earn membership through Faction Entry. Full rules live in [07 Character Life](07%20Character%20Life.md).

A member may later betray, defect, or realign. Allegiance change is real and costly: Influence, access, Symptoms, and available paths all change.

## Difficulty Settings

The previous single World Difficulty scale (Easy / Normal / Hard / Hell) is superseded by **eight independent difficulty sliders**. The player can tune distinct aspects of the world separately; a single setting must not silently bundle unrelated effects.

The agreed sliders are:

1. **Combat Difficulty** — changes the generated power distribution of opponents and dangerous creatures. Higher settings make higher attributes and higher Pathway stages more common among generated threats. It does not multiply HP, damage, defence, or other canonical statistics, and does not introduce difficulty-specific character formulas.
2. **World Inhabitants** — controls the quantity, density, diversity, prevalence, and activity of creatures and NPCs. This is independent of their power: a dense world can contain weak inhabitants, while a sparse world can contain dangerous ones.
3. **Resource Gathering** — controls resource availability and the conditions, difficulty, and yield of gathering.
4. **Crafting** — controls recipe requirements, including ingredient quantities and the possible inclusion or increased likelihood of rarer components at higher settings. It does not change crafting time. The allowable output-quality range is not changed by this slider.
5. **Economy** — controls market conditions such as assortment, prices, stock, availability, and access to goods and services. Faction Influence remains the primary driver of local economic conditions.
6. **Equipment Quality** — controls the prevalence and availability of equipment by quality and rarity; higher settings can make rare and high-quality items less common.
7. **Wear and Maintenance** — controls equipment wear and the burden or availability of repair and maintenance.
8. **Cult Pace** — controls how fast Cult Forces generate followers and so how fast the cult advances ([21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md)). It does not change character formulas.

These sliders affect world-generation conditions, distributions, availability, and costs. They do not create alternative versions of the underlying character, action-check, progression, or item-effectiveness formulas. Where a slider changes generated character strength, the same canonical attributes, skills, Pathway rules, and derived-stat formulas apply to player characters and non-player actors alike.

Combat Difficulty and World Inhabitants are orthogonal: one controls the power profile of generated threats, the other controls how many inhabitants exist and how varied or active they are. Economy and Equipment Quality are also distinct: Economy governs market conditions and access; Equipment Quality governs the quality/rarity distribution of equipment entering the world and its markets.

Exact slider ranges, presets, numerical distributions, and interactions remain open for later design and tuning. The old Easy / Normal / Hard / Hell faction-pressure direction is not retained as a bundled global difficulty model; faction pressure and Operation behaviour must be specified through their owning systems and any explicitly assigned settings.

# Economy Hook

Economy is an expression of Faction Influence.

As a Major Faction's Influence grows, it occupies niches: better access, better prices for allies, worse prices or closed services for opponents, stronger artifacts and support. Detailed economy simulation is deferred, but future economy design must treat Influence as the primary driver rather than an isolated shop spreadsheet.

# World Collapse

A world never ends because of a timer. Irreversible Balance failure can push it into a terminal state, and an unopposed return of the sealed Stage 0 being can physically destroy the planet. The two are distinct world-ending routes.

The player may delay collapse, redirect which terminal state occurs, or accelerate collapse. The player's defeat after the being manifests can occur before physical destruction: no successor life is available, while the world catastrophe may continue beyond the playable character.

Both campaign completion routes end the campaign and present a rating. Whether the surviving character can continue afterward, how campaign victory relates to the world's later finite life-cycle, how Balance can recover without the player, and each Major Faction's exact terminal condition remain open. No implementation may invent answers before they are documented.

# Knowledge

The world becomes increasingly understandable through play. Failure should result primarily from incomplete understanding, poor preparation, or accepting a known risk — not from unknowable randomness.

By observing Symptoms, Operations, and consequences, the player learns to anticipate likely future changes. Knowledge never guarantees control: it improves prediction and opens better interventions, while the world retains uncertainty and independent agency.

Important places, faction tells, information Approaches, and the long-term meaning of bargains are prime Knowledge targets. Character power systems that use formulas, ingredients, or pathways are future Progression design; they must obey Price of Power and must not replace Knowledge with a pure success-rate bonus.

# External Power Principle

Any power granted by otherworldly entities, churches, deities, or similar external sources is a **Bargain**:

- the advantage is real;
- the price is readable eventually;
- the price is often delayed and escalates;
- refusal remains a valid choice.

Detailed curse, blessing, and pathway systems belong to future Progression / Magic documents. They may not contradict this principle.

- **Core Loop:** Compatible. Symptoms, Side Events, and intervention Approaches feed notice → context → action → consequence → understanding.
