# Purpose and authority

This document defines the shared derived-statistics layer for player characters and NPCs. It records the owner-approved structure and relationships, not a complete numeric specification or implementation authorisation.

Derived statistics are calculated from the seven ordinary attributes, skills where relevant, equipment, Pathway progression, abilities, conditions, and context. They are not additional independently levelled progression tracks unless a future system explicitly establishes an exception.

The design draws selectively on established RPG patterns, especially Path of Exile 2's layered relationship between attributes, resources, equipment, and defences; Baldur's Gate 3's attribute/skill checks and initiative; and Fallout 2's clear Strength-to-carrying-capacity relationship. These are design references, not formulas to copy.

# 1. Design principles

- Keep the derived-stat layer compact and legible.
- Do not create a separate stat for every intermediate value used by a check.
- Preserve the seven ordinary attributes as the character's core progression tracks.
- Derived values may be modified by equipment, skills, Pathways, abilities, conditions, and context.
- Different systems may use different derived values; not every value needs to be displayed to the player at all times.
- Avoid duplicate costs and redundant mechanics. In particular, combat AP is not a universal substitute for physical Stamina.
- Exact formulas, scales, caps, thresholds, and UI presentation remain open unless explicitly marked otherwise.

# 3. Resources

## 3.0 Universal resource Stage Multiplier

**Owner-approved shared rule (2026-10-31):** maximum HP, maximum Stamina, and maximum Will Power all use the same Pathway-stage multiplier S. The multiplier applies regardless of Pathway type; a Pathway focused on Intelligence or Perception still increases the character's maximum HP and Stamina as its stage advances. A character without a Pathway uses S = 1.00.

| Pathway stage | Multiplier S |
|---:|---:|
| 9 | 1.00 |
| 8 | 1.15 |
| 7 | 1.32 |
| 6 | 1.52 |
| 5 | 1.75 |
| 4 | 2.01 |
| 3 | 2.31 |
| 2 | 2.66 |
| 1 | 3.06 |
| 0 | 3.52 |

The shared formulas are:

\\[HP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

\\[Stamina_{max}=(20+1.6\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

\\[WP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Will)\\times S\\]

A stage increase raises the maximum capacity of each pool but does not automatically restore its current value. Equipment and explicit effects may further modify maximum pools; their stacking order remains open.


## 3.1 Health

**Owner-approved maximum HP formula (2026-10-31):**

\\[HP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

Maximum Health is derived from Endurance and the universal Pathway-stage multiplier S. Equipment, skills, abilities, and explicit effects may further modify it under their own rules. Health is deliberately scarce.

**Owner-approved ordinary HP recovery rule (2026-10-31):** HP naturally recovers at 1% of maximum HP per 10 world ticks during ordinary recovery, unless a condition or effect blocks recovery. Sleep advances approximately 6–8 in-game hours and fully restores ordinary HP only if no active condition or effect blocks recovery. Exact rates and exceptions belong to the health, healing, and world-time specifications.

## 3.2 Stamina

Endurance is the primary source of maximum Stamina. Stamina is a finite resource for additional or sustained physical exertion. It is deliberately not a tax on ordinary world traversal: normal walking and ordinary running do not consume Stamina.

**Owner-approved Stamina expenditure and recovery rules (2026-10-31):**

- **No Stamina cost:** ordinary walking and ordinary running.
- **Stamina cost:** accelerated / sprint running, climbing, gathering and other physically demanding work, and other explicitly designated exertion.
- **Carrying load:** high load increases Stamina expenditure where physical exertion is involved. Overloaded and especially Critical Overload states cause substantially greater Stamina expenditure than an ordinary load. Exact load-band multipliers and thresholds remain open.
- Stamina is not the universal combat action resource. Combat actions use AP under [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md). A specific ability or action may additionally cost Stamina only where its rules explicitly say so.
- When the character is not performing a Stamina-consuming physical activity, Stamina regenerates at **5% of maximum Stamina per world tick**. At this rate, an empty pool is restored in 20 ticks, absent modifiers or blockers.
- Regeneration is paused while performing an activity that consumes Stamina and resumes when that exertion ends.
- Ordinary exploration and ordinary running therefore do not prevent Stamina regeneration. The player can travel without being forced to stop, while repeatedly sprinting, climbing, gathering, and carrying excessive loads creates meaningful resource-management decisions.
- Monsters and other relevant creatures may have faster Stamina regeneration than the player character, so a pursued player cannot reliably escape indefinitely by alternating short bursts of exertion and pauses. Exact creature rates are creature/balance parameters, not a universal player rate.

**Owner-approved maximum Stamina formula (2026-10-31):**

\\[Stamina_{max}=(20+1.6\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

Action-specific Stamina costs, load-band multipliers, and creature-specific recovery modifiers remain open.

## 3.3 Will Power / magical energy

**Owner-approved model (2026-10-30):** Will is the ordinary 1–100 attribute governing the character's capacity to use supernatural and Pathway abilities. Will Power is a separate, visible, spendable resource pool. Do not conflate the attribute with the resource.

- Pathways determine which abilities the character can obtain and use. Not every Pathway must grant conventional spells; abilities may instead provide self-enhancement, detection, or other supernatural effects, and some Pathways may have no conventional magical abilities.
- Will determines how much use the character can support; it does not determine which abilities a Pathway grants.
- Pathway progression may increase Will. Items and explicit effects may also modify Will under the ordinary attribute-modification rules.
- Abilities spend Will Power according to their individual costs.

**Maximum Will Power:**

\[
WP_{max}=(20+0.8\times Will)\times S
\]

Here, S is the **universal resource Stage Multiplier** for the character's current Pathway stage. The same S applies to maximum HP, maximum Stamina, and maximum Will Power, regardless of the character's Pathway type:

| Pathway stage | Multiplier S |
|---:|---:|
| 9 | 1.00 |
| 8 | 1.15 |
| 7 | 1.32 |
| 6 | 1.52 |
| 5 | 1.75 |
| 4 | 2.01 |
| 3 | 2.31 |
| 2 | 2.66 |
| 1 | 3.06 |
| 0 | 3.52 |

A character without a Pathway uses **S = 1.00**. This multiplier is universal: Pathway type does not restrict which resource pools benefit from stage progression. A character on an Intelligence- or Perception-focused Pathway receives the same stage-based scaling to maximum HP and Stamina as any other Pathway.

A Pathway-stage increase raises maximum resource capacity but **does not automatically refill current HP, Stamina, or Will Power**. The current amount remains unchanged unless a separate effect explicitly restores it.

Equipment and explicit effects may further modify maximum resource pools. The exact order for stacking additional percentage modifiers remains a tuning detail.

**Initial ability-cost balance bands (guidelines, not rigid categories):**
- Ordinary abilities: approximately 10–20% of maximum Will Power.
- Powerful abilities: approximately 25–40%.
- Exceptional abilities: approximately 50–80%.
Each ability defines its own actual cost; Pathway and ability design may depart from these initial bands when justified.

**Recovery:**
- Ordinary Will Power regeneration is approximately 1% of maximum Will Power per 10 world ticks.
- Sleep is expected to restore the ordinary Will Power pool fully unless an active effect damages or suppresses Will or its recovery.
- Will Power recovery and Sanity recovery are separate processes.

**Full exhaustion and Will progression:**
- Reaching zero current Will Power through ability use triggers a full-exhaustion event.
- Full exhaustion grants a small amount of Will progression XP. Ordinary repeated ability use does not independently grant Will XP.
- Only one exhaustion reward is granted per continuous exhaustion episode. The character must recover from the exhausted state before another reward can be earned; spending and restoring a trivial amount must not allow reward farming.
- The reward must be modest and subject to meaningful-expenditure / Pathway-stage eligibility so trivial abilities cannot be exploited to train Will. Exact XP and eligibility thresholds remain open.
- Full exhaustion also causes Sanity damage; see §3.4.

Will remains one of the seven ordinary 1–100 attributes, not a new independent progression track. Effects that damage or impair Will may reduce effective Will and therefore ability-use capacity. Whether a particular effect reduces effective Will, maximum Will Power, current Will Power, or a combination—and its duration, treatment, and recovery—must be declared by that effect's rules.

## 3.4 Sanity

Sanity is a separate accumulated measure of mental integrity, ranging from 0 to 100. It is not Will Power and is not a synonym for the Will attribute.

**Sanity bands:**

| Sanity | State |
|---:|---|
| 100 | Stable |
| 70–99 | Mild disturbance |
| 40–69 | Significant disturbance |
| 10–39 | Severe disturbance |
| 1–9 | Critical |
| 0 | Loss of sanity |

These bands establish the shared scale. Exact symptoms, penalties, and gameplay manifestations for each nonzero band remain to be specified.

**Will mitigation of Sanity damage:**

For a source of Sanity damage that uses the shared Will-mitigation rule:

\[
D_{sanity}=D_{base}\times\frac{100}{100+Will}
\]

Will reduces incoming Sanity damage but does not make the character immune.

**Sanity damage from full Will Power exhaustion:**

\[
D_{exhaustion}=D_0\times S\times\frac{100}{100+Will}
\]

D₀ is the balance-tuned base Sanity damage for exhaustion; S is the same Pathway-stage multiplier used for maximum Will Power. Higher Pathway stages therefore make full exhaustion more dangerous to Sanity, while higher Will mitigates the damage. Exact D₀ remains open.

Other possible Sanity-damage sources include dangerous rituals, corrupted or unsuitable substances, improper supernatural actions, entities, artefacts, places, curses, and mental attacks. Each source must define its base impact and whether shared Will mitigation applies; do not assume every source uses the same mitigation rule.

Sanity is not automatically restored by ordinary Will Power regeneration or sleep. Recovery requires dedicated items, abilities, treatments, safe activities, or rare world events. The exact methods and rates remain open.

**Sanity reaching zero is a character-life transformation, not ordinary death:**
- At Sanity 0, the character permanently loses their human personality and ceases to be player-controlled.
- The character remains in the world as a game-controlled creature and may retain abilities, equipment, and artefacts.
- Other characters may encounter the transformed former character.
- Sanity must be recoverable before reaching zero; zero is not intended as an ordinary temporary state.
- This outcome is distinct from ordinary character death.

# 4. Combat values

## 4.1 Accuracy

Accuracy is a calculated combat value, not a new independently levelled attribute. Its inputs depend on attack type and include relevant attributes, the applicable weapon/ability skill, weapon or ability properties, equipment, effects, and circumstances.

Perception contributes to every attack explicitly targeted at an opponent. The attack-type relationships already recorded in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) remain authoritative until their formulas are reconciled with the shared check model.

## 4.2 Evasion

Evasion is derived primarily from Dexterity and Perception and may be modified by equipment, abilities, and conditions. It represents avoiding an attack, not reducing the damage after a hit.

Immobilisation, stun, binding, and similar states may reduce or remove the ability to evade. There is no standalone Evasion skill.

## 4.3 Physical and Magical Defence

The initial defence model has exactly two derived defence values:

- **Physical Defence** mitigates physical damage.
- **Magical Defence** mitigates magical damage.

Equipment and explicit abilities/effects provide these values. The relevant defence value is converted into percentage damage reduction separately for each damage type, subject to an upper cap so ordinary equipment cannot provide complete immunity.

Do not add separate elemental damage types or elemental resistance stats at this stage. Blocking, parrying, barriers, absorption, reflection, and other special defensive mechanics may be granted by specific equipment or abilities; they are not required to become universal derived statistics.

The conversion curves, caps, stacking, penetration, and interactions remain open in the combat specification.

## 4.4 Initiative and Action Points

The existing owner-approved rules in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) remain in force:

- Initiative depends on Dexterity, Perception, and applicable item, skill, and effect modifiers.
- Every character has the same fixed base AP pool.
- Dexterity gradually increases AP; items and skills may add bonuses.
- Actions have variable AP costs.
- AP remaining at the end of a turn is retained only for defensive reactions outside the character's turn and expires when the next turn begins.
- There is no universal Attack Speed attribute.

Exact formulas, values, costs, and limits remain open.

# 5. Movement and carrying load

## 5.1 Movement Speed

Dexterity is the primary proposed attribute influence on movement speed. Endurance is relevant to sustaining physical effort, not simply to instantaneous movement speed.

World movement in free third-person spaces and movement on the square-grid Combat Arenas are distinct contexts. Their units and formulas must not be conflated. Movement Speed and the Stamina cost of sustained movement are separate concepts: a faster character does not automatically have unlimited endurance.

## 5.2 Carrying Capacity and load bands

Strength is the primary source of Carrying Capacity. Carrying load is evaluated relative to the character's capacity, not by requiring every item to be categorised as a special heavy item.

**Agreed load bands:**

1. **Normal load:** ordinary movement and activity without an overload penalty.
2. **High load:** approaching or exceeding the comfortable carrying range; some penalties may begin.
3. **Overloaded:** substantial excess load; movement and physical activity become meaningfully harder.
4. **Critical overload:** extreme excess load; severe restrictions or inability to perform some activities may apply.

The system uses graduated consequences rather than a single binary can/cannot-carry threshold. Carrying load also affects Stamina expenditure during exertion: high load increases the cost, while Overloaded and especially Critical Overload cause substantially greater expenditure. Exact weight thresholds, load multipliers, whether any hard maximum exists, and the consequences for movement speed, checks, and combat remain open.

# 6. Contextual capabilities, not standalone stats

## Detection / Awareness

Detection is resolved contextually rather than through one universal Detection stat. Relevant factors include Perception, distance, lighting, noise, target concealment, environmental conditions, and the nature of what is being detected.

## Stealth / Concealment

Stealth is likewise contextual. Relevant factors include Dexterity, the applicable skill, equipment, noise, lighting, movement/behaviour, and the observer's capabilities (including Perception). Do not create a universal Stealth percentage that replaces action checks and opposing detection.

# 7. Recovery

Recovery is not one universal statistic. Keep separate processes for:

- HP recovery;
- Stamina recovery;
- Will Power recovery;
- removal or recovery from individual negative conditions.

Endurance may influence selected physical recovery and condition-resolution processes, but it is not a universal resistance or universal recovery multiplier. Each resource and condition must specify its own blockers, treatment, and recovery rules.

# 8. Explicit exclusions

The current design does not introduce:

- a universal Attack Speed attribute;
- a standalone Evasion skill;
- a standalone universal Detection stat;
- a standalone universal Stealth stat;
- one universal resistance statistic covering all negative conditions;
- one universal action-speed statistic;
- a separately levelled Critical Chance or Critical Damage attribute;
- a universal magical resource shared as a fixed spell list across all Pathways.

Critical chance and overflow potency follow [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md). Ability access and content belong to the Pathway/ability specifications.

# 9. Open questions

- Whether Will Power recovery is exactly the same rate as HP or only approximately similar.
- Exact ability costs and how costs differ among Pathways and ability types.
- Exact rules for Will damage: temporary effective-attribute reduction, maximum-pool reduction, resource suppression, or a combination; duration, treatment, and recovery.
- Exact Movement Speed formula and separate world/arena units.
- Carrying Capacity formula, weight thresholds for the four load bands, and each band's consequences.
- Accuracy and Evasion formulas and reconciliation with the shared action-check model.
- Physical/Magical Defence conversion curves, caps, stacking, and penetration.
- Exact recovery rules for each resource and negative condition.
- Which derived values are visible to the player at different Intelligence levels.

Do not silently fill these in. The structure and principles above are owner-approved; numerical formulas and system-specific details remain open.
