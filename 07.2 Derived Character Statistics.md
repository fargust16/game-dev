# Purpose and authority

This document defines the shared derived-statistics layer for player characters and NPCs. It records the owner-approved structure and relationships, not a complete numeric specification or implementation authorisation.

Derived statistics are calculated from the seven ordinary attributes, skills where relevant, equipment, Arcana progression, abilities, conditions, and context. They are not additional independently levelled progression tracks unless a future system explicitly establishes an exception.

The design draws selectively on established RPG patterns, especially Path of Exile 2's layered relationship between attributes, resources, equipment, and defences; Baldur's Gate 3's attribute/skill checks and initiative; and Fallout 2's clear Strength-to-carrying-capacity relationship. These are design references, not formulas to copy.

# 1. Design principles

- Keep the derived-stat layer compact and legible.
- Do not create a separate stat for every intermediate value used by a check.
- Preserve the seven ordinary attributes as the character's core progression tracks.
- Derived values may be modified by equipment, skills, Arcana, abilities, conditions, and context.
- Different systems may use different derived values; not every value needs to be displayed to the player at all times.
- Avoid duplicate costs and redundant mechanics. In particular, combat AP is not a universal substitute for physical Stamina.
- Exact formulas, scales, caps, thresholds, and UI presentation remain open unless explicitly marked otherwise.

# 3. Resources

## 3.0 Universal resource Limit Multiplier

**Owner-approved shared rule (2026-10-31):** maximum HP, maximum Stamina, and maximum Will Power all use the same Limit multiplier S. The multiplier applies regardless of Arcana type; an Arcana focused on Intelligence or Perception still increases the character's maximum HP and Stamina as its stage advances. A character without an Arcana uses S = 1.00.

| Limit | Multiplier S |
|---:|---:|
| 9 | 1.00 |
| 8 | 1.15 |
| 7 | 1.32 |
| 6 | 1.52 |
| 5 | 1.75 |
| 4 | 2.01 |
| 3 | 2.31 |
| 2 | 2.66 |
| 1 | 3.06 |
| 0 | 3.52 |

The shared formulas are:

\\[HP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

\\[Stamina_{max}=(20+1.6\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

\\[WP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Will)\\times S\\]

A Limit increase raises the maximum capacity of each pool but does not automatically restore its current value. Equipment and explicit effects may further modify maximum pools; their stacking order remains open.


## 3.1 Health

**Owner-approved maximum HP formula (2026-10-31):**

\\[HP_{max}=(20+0.8\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

Maximum Health is derived from Endurance and the universal Limit multiplier S. Equipment, skills, abilities, and explicit effects may further modify it under their own rules. Health is deliberately scarce.

**Owner-approved ordinary HP recovery rule (2026-10-31):** HP naturally recovers at 1% of maximum HP per 10 world ticks during ordinary recovery, unless a condition or effect blocks recovery. Sleep advances approximately 6–8 in-game hours and fully restores ordinary HP only if no active condition or effect blocks recovery. Exact rates and exceptions belong to the health, healing, and world-time specifications.

## 3.2 Stamina

Endurance is the primary source of maximum Stamina. Stamina is a finite resource for additional or sustained physical exertion. It is deliberately not a tax on ordinary world traversal: normal walking and ordinary running do not consume Stamina.

**Owner-approved Stamina expenditure and recovery rules (2026-10-31):**

- **No Stamina cost:** ordinary walking and ordinary running.
- **Stamina cost:** accelerated / sprint running, climbing, gathering and other physically demanding work, and other explicitly designated exertion.
- **Carrying load:** high load increases Stamina expenditure where physical exertion is involved. Overloaded and especially Critical Overload states cause substantially greater Stamina expenditure than an ordinary load. Thresholds are defined in §5.2; exact load-band multipliers remain open.
- Stamina is not the universal combat action resource. Combat actions use AP under [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md). A specific ability or action may additionally cost Stamina only where its rules explicitly say so.
- When the character is not performing a Stamina-consuming physical activity, Stamina regenerates at **20% of maximum Stamina per world tick** (the owner's change of 2026-11-11, after the play test of S5a, to make it faster; the 20 % is an authoring default, 5 % before). At this rate, an empty pool is restored in 5 ticks, absent modifiers or blockers.
- Regeneration is paused while performing an activity that consumes Stamina and resumes when that exertion ends.
- Ordinary exploration and ordinary running therefore do not prevent Stamina regeneration. The player can travel without being forced to stop, while repeatedly sprinting, climbing, gathering, and carrying excessive loads creates meaningful resource-management decisions.
- Monsters and other relevant creatures may have faster Stamina regeneration than the player character, so a pursued player cannot reliably escape indefinitely by alternating short bursts of exertion and pauses. Exact creature rates are creature/balance parameters, not a universal player rate.

**Owner-approved maximum Stamina formula (2026-10-31):**

\\[Stamina_{max}=(20+1.6\\times Endurance)\\times S\\]

Action-specific Stamina costs, load-band multipliers, and creature-specific recovery modifiers remain open.

**Owner's direction, 2026-11-11 (the play test of S5a):** Stamina is a bar like the health bar, shown whenever it is spent or coming back. Running, gathering, chopping, crafting and other physical work spend it. When it runs out the work stops, and it starts coming back 2–3 seconds later; when the work stops before it runs out, it starts coming back at once.

The slice's readings, authoring defaults and working readings of S5a, nothing else decided:

- **Running** is read as the sprint (Shift). Ordinary running stays free, as the owner's rules of 2026-10-31 above keep it; making ordinary running cost Stamina too is one number if the owner wants it.
- **Costs:** the sprint 2 % of maximum Stamina a second; a gathering cycle 3 % ([19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.4); Item Creation 2 % a tick of work, and a piece is not begun without enough for all of it.
- **Swimming** (owner's decision, 2026-11-11): ordinary swimming is free, as ordinary running is, and the fast swim spends 2 % a second, as the sprint does ([10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md)).
- **Recovery:** 20 % a tick as above, spread over the tick's ten real seconds: 2 % a second, as fast as the sprint spends it, so an empty bar is full again in about 50 seconds; it stops while the world is paused. After running out it waits 2.5 seconds, and work that spends Stamina can begin again once a tenth has come back, so the sprint does not stutter.

## 3.3 Will Power / magical energy

**Owner-approved model (2026-10-30):** Will is the ordinary 1–100 attribute governing the character's capacity to use supernatural and Arcana abilities. Will Power is a separate, visible, spendable resource pool. Do not conflate the attribute with the resource.

- Arcana determine which abilities the character can obtain and use. Not every Arcana must grant conventional spells; abilities may instead provide self-enhancement, detection, or other supernatural effects, and some Arcana may have no conventional magical abilities.
- Will determines how much use the character can support; it does not determine which abilities an Arcana grants.
- Arcana progression may increase Will. Items and explicit effects may also modify Will under the ordinary attribute-modification rules.
- Abilities spend Will Power according to their individual costs.
- Infusion spends a flat 5 × S a shot or blow, so that more Will means more of them; it adds a fixed share of the weapon's base as magical damage, with no attribute in it ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f).
- **Will Power is the only limit of abilities** (owner's concept, 2026-11-13): an ability has its cost and no cooldown. A failure's own consequence, such as Завеса torn for the rest of a fight, is not a cooldown. In a fight Will Power comes back at the ordinary rate, so a fight is fought on the pool brought into it.

**Maximum Will Power:**

\[
WP_{max}=(20+0.8\times Will)\times S
\]

Here, S is the **universal resource Limit Multiplier** for the character's current Limit. The same S applies to maximum HP, maximum Stamina, and maximum Will Power, regardless of the character's Arcana type:

| Limit | Multiplier S |
|---:|---:|
| 9 | 1.00 |
| 8 | 1.15 |
| 7 | 1.32 |
| 6 | 1.52 |
| 5 | 1.75 |
| 4 | 2.01 |
| 3 | 2.31 |
| 2 | 2.66 |
| 1 | 3.06 |
| 0 | 3.52 |

A character without an Arcana uses **S = 1.00**. This multiplier is universal: Arcana type does not restrict which resource pools benefit from Limit progression. A character on an Intelligence- or Perception-focused Arcana receives the same stage-based scaling to maximum HP and Stamina as any other Arcana.

A Limit increase raises maximum resource capacity but **does not automatically refill current HP, Stamina, or Will Power**. The current amount remains unchanged unless a separate effect explicitly restores it.

Equipment and explicit effects may further modify maximum resource pools. The exact order for stacking additional percentage modifiers remains a tuning detail.

**Initial ability-cost balance bands (guidelines, not rigid categories):**
- Ordinary abilities: approximately 10–20% of maximum Will Power.
- Powerful abilities: approximately 25–40%.
- Exceptional abilities: approximately 50–80%.
Each ability defines its own actual cost; Arcana and ability design may depart from these initial bands when justified.

**Recovery:**
- Ordinary Will Power regeneration is approximately 1% of maximum Will Power per 10 world ticks.
- Sleep is expected to restore the ordinary Will Power pool fully unless an active effect damages or suppresses Will or its recovery.
- Will Power recovery and Sanity recovery are separate processes.

**Fatigue** (owner-approved, 2026-11-03: staying awake brings penalties; the numbers are authoring defaults):
- Fatigue counts the in-game hours a character has been awake since its last full sleep. A run starts rested.
- **Tired**, from 18 hours awake: −5 to hit, the good and perfect zones of a Reaction Check a fifth narrower, and Stamina recovering at half speed.
- **Very tired**, from 30 hours awake: −10 to hit, the zones two fifths narrower, Stamina at half speed, no Will Power regeneration, and −1 to Social Checks.
- A full sleep of 6 hours or more clears Fatigue. A shorter sleep takes off two hours of it for each hour slept.

In the slice a player who uses the night instead of sleeping reaches the invasion Tired or Very tired, so the night is a real choice.

**Full exhaustion and Will progression:**
- Reaching zero current Will Power through ability use triggers a full-exhaustion event. Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: Infusion counts as such use, with the same reward and the same Sanity damage ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b).
- Full exhaustion grants a small amount of Will progression XP. Ordinary repeated ability use does not independently grant Will XP.
- Only one exhaustion reward is granted per continuous exhaustion episode. The character must recover from the exhausted state before another reward can be earned; spending and restoring a trivial amount must not allow reward farming.
- The reward must be modest and subject to meaningful-expenditure / Limit eligibility so trivial abilities cannot be exploited to train Will. Exact XP and eligibility thresholds remain open.
- Full exhaustion also causes Sanity damage; see §3.4.

Will remains one of the seven ordinary 1–100 attributes, not a new independent progression track. Effects that damage or impair Will may reduce effective Will and therefore ability-use capacity. Whether a particular effect reduces effective Will, maximum Will Power, current Will Power, or a combination—and its duration, treatment, and recovery—must be declared by that effect's rules.

## 3.4 Sanity

Sanity is a separate accumulated measure of mental integrity, ranging from 0 to 100. It is not Will Power and is not a synonym for the Will attribute.

**Sanity bands:**

| Sanity | State |
|---:|---|
| 100 | Stable |
| 70–99 | Mild disturbance |
| 40–69 | Significant disturbance |
| 10–39 | Severe disturbance |
| 1–9 | Critical |
| 0 | Loss of sanity |

These bands establish the shared scale.

**Sanity in the slice.** Owner-approved, 2026-11-04: every band below Stable brings a light penalty and one symptom the player perceives, and Sanity 0 ends the run. The values are authoring defaults set by the agent the same day.

| Sanity | Penalty | Symptom |
|---:|---|---|
| 100 | None | None |
| 70–99 | Will Power regenerates at three quarters of its speed | Whispers: now and then a voice at the edge of hearing, with no words |
| 40–69 | −5 to hit, −1 to the preparation of every check of [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md), −1 to Social Checks | Glimpses: a figure at the edge of the screen, gone when the camera turns |
| 10–39 | −10 to hit, −2 to checks and Social Checks, maximum Will Power −25 % | False alarms: now and then a Notice Meter fills from nobody |
| 1–9 | −15 to hit, −3 to checks and Social Checks, maximum Will Power −50 %, the zones of a Reaction Check half as wide | The colour drains from the world, and whispers speak over the lines of a dialogue |

- A symptom deceives perception only. It never writes a false line in Character Notes, never moves or invents a trace, and never puts words in a person's mouth, so the player can learn to read it.
- Sanity's penalties add to those of Fatigue (§3.3).
- In the slice Sanity is spent by the Limit potions ([05.1](05.1%20Arcana%20and%20Limits.md)) and by full exhaustion, and restored by sleep.
- At 0 the character becomes a creature as described below, and in the slice the run ends.

**Will mitigation of Sanity damage:**

For a source of Sanity damage that uses the shared Will-mitigation rule:

\[
D_{sanity}=D_{base}\times\frac{100}{100+Will}
\]

Will reduces incoming Sanity damage but does not make the character immune.

**Sanity damage from full Will Power exhaustion:**

\[
D_{exhaustion}=D_0\times S\times\frac{100}{100+Will}
\]

D₀ is the balance-tuned base Sanity damage for exhaustion; S is the same Limit multiplier used for maximum Will Power. Higher Limits therefore make full exhaustion more dangerous to Sanity, while higher Will mitigates the damage. Authoring default, 2026-11-04: D₀ = 5, so a Limit 9 character with Will 6 loses about 5 Sanity, and a Limit 8 character with Will 12 about 5 as well.

Other possible Sanity-damage sources include dangerous rituals, corrupted or unsuitable substances, improper supernatural actions, entities, artefacts, places, curses, and mental attacks. Each source must define its base impact and whether shared Will mitigation applies; do not assume every source uses the same mitigation rule.

Sanity is not restored by ordinary Will Power regeneration. A full sleep restores a little, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-02, which replaces the earlier rule that sleep restores none: 3 Sanity after a full sleep of 6 hours or more (authoring default, 2026-11-04). Larger recovery requires dedicated items, abilities, treatments, safe activities, or rare world events. The exact methods and rates remain open.

**Sanity reaching zero is a character-life transformation, not ordinary death:**
- At Sanity 0, the character permanently loses their human personality and ceases to be player-controlled.
- The character remains in the world as a game-controlled creature and may retain abilities, equipment, and artefacts.
- Other characters may encounter the transformed former character.
- Sanity must be recoverable before reaching zero; zero is not intended as an ordinary temporary state.
- This outcome is distinct from ordinary character death.

# 4. Combat values

## 4.1 Accuracy

Accuracy is a calculated combat value, not a new independently levelled attribute. Its inputs depend on attack type and include relevant attributes, the applicable weapon/ability skill, weapon or ability properties, equipment, effects, and circumstances.

Since the combat rework (owner, 2026-11-13): an attack aims by its profile attribute, Dexterity for light melee and light firearms and Strength for heavy melee and heavy firearms, and the skill of its kind; a targeted ability by Intelligence and the Arcana Skill. Perception aims nothing and sets the critical chance ([07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md) §7). The formulas are in [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

## 4.2 Evasion

Evasion is derived primarily from Dexterity and Perception and may be modified by equipment, abilities, and conditions. It represents avoiding an attack, not reducing the damage after a hit.

Immobilisation, stun, binding, and similar states may reduce or remove the ability to evade. There is no standalone Evasion skill.

## 4.3 Physical and Magical Defence

The initial defence model has exactly two derived defence values:

- **Physical Defence** mitigates physical damage.
- **Magical Defence** mitigates magical damage.

Equipment and explicit abilities/effects provide these values. The relevant defence value is converted into percentage damage reduction separately for each damage type, subject to an upper cap so ordinary equipment cannot provide complete immunity.

Do not add separate elemental damage types or elemental resistance stats at this stage. Blocking, parrying, barriers, absorption, reflection, and other special defensive mechanics may be granted by specific equipment or abilities; they are not required to become universal derived statistics.

A working conversion and its cap are in [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b. Stacking, penetration, and interactions remain open in the combat specification.

## 4.4 Initiative and Action Points

The existing owner-approved rules in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) remain in force:

- Initiative depends on Dexterity, Perception, and applicable item, skill, and effect modifiers.
- Every character has the same fixed base AP pool.
- Dexterity gradually increases AP; items and skills may add bonuses.
- Actions have variable AP costs.
- AP remaining at the end of a turn is retained only for defensive reactions outside the character's turn and expires when the next turn begins.
- There is no universal Attack Speed attribute.

Exact formulas, values, costs, and limits remain open.

## 4.5 Prepared Abilities

Owner, 2026-11-13 (the combat rework's concept §6 and the answer to Q6, [COMBAT_REWORK_PLAN.md](unity/Assets/Plans/COMBAT_REWORK_PLAN.md) §3a): a character knows its abilities and prepares some of them for a fight. In a fight only prepared active abilities can be used. Passive abilities always work and take no slot.

- **Slots:** `3 + floor((Intelligence + Will − 2) / 14)`, at most 10. A character starts with 3, at Intelligence 1 and Will 1, and has 10 at a sum of 100 (owner). The step of 14 is the agent's, the one whole step that meets both ends; it is an authoring default.
- **Beyond 10.** Items and artefacts may add slots, also above 10 (the concept's §6 and §13). None does in the slice.
- **Changing the set** only out of a fight, taking one tick (owner).
- **Out of a fight** every known active ability can be used; the slots bind in a fight. This is the agent's working reading of the concept, which limits what is open "at once in a given fight".
- **NPCs** prepare the abilities of their Arcana and Limit; in the slice none knows more than its slots hold.

| Intelligence + Will | 2 | 16 | 30 | 44 | 58 | 72 | 86 | 100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Slots | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |

A slice character at Limit 8 has Intelligence + Will of about 10–20, so 3 or 4 slots, against at most four active abilities known; the choice of what to prepare starts to bite at the Limits beyond the slice.

*In the slice* (CR4, 2026-11-13; working readings): K opens the page of Prepared Abilities out of a fight. It lists the abilities a fight can use, each prepared or not with its price and AP, then those of exploration, open with no slot, then the passive ones. A click prepares or puts away one ability for one tick while a slot is free; until the character keeps a set, the first it knows are prepared. The set lives in the world state ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f *In the slice*).

# 5. Movement and carrying load

## 5.1 Movement Speed

Dexterity is the primary proposed attribute influence on movement speed. Endurance is relevant to sustaining physical effort, not simply to instantaneous movement speed.

World movement in free third-person spaces and movement on the square Combat Grid are distinct contexts. Their units and formulas must not be conflated. Movement Speed and the Stamina cost of sustained movement are separate concepts: a faster character does not automatically have unlimited endurance.

## 5.2 Carrying Capacity and load bands

Carrying Capacity is the character's comfortable carrying weight. Strength is its only primary input under this starting balance rule:

`Comfortable Capacity (kg) = 20 + Strength`

Strength uses its 1–100 attribute level, so the comfortable capacity is 21 kg at Strength 1, 70 kg at Strength 50, and 120 kg at Strength 100. Equipped items count toward total carried weight. Coins weigh 0. The item weight model is defined in [19 Items, Crafting, Inventory, Equipment, and Enchanting](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md).

**Load bands:**

| Band | Total weight as a share of comfortable capacity |
|---|---:|
| Normal | up to 100% |
| High | over 100% to 125% |
| Overloaded | over 125% to 150% |
| Critical Overload | over 150% to 200% |

The hard carry limit is 200% of comfortable capacity. A pickup or transfer that would take the character above that limit is rejected. These are initial balance values selected under the owner's delegation.

Load affects Stamina expenditure during exertion: High increases cost, and Overloaded/Critical Overload increase it further. In a fight it also takes Evasion and AP ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b, Load in a Fight), authoring defaults. Exact multipliers and any additional movement or check penalties remain open.

# 6. Contextual capabilities, not standalone stats

## Detection / Awareness

Detection is resolved contextually rather than through one universal Detection stat. Relevant factors include Perception, distance, lighting, target concealment, environmental conditions, and the nature of what is being detected.

## Stealth / Concealment

Stealth is likewise contextual. Relevant factors include Dexterity, the applicable skill, equipment, lighting, movement/behaviour, and the observer's capabilities (including Perception). Do not create a universal Stealth percentage that replaces action checks and opposing detection. The rules are in [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md).

# 7. Recovery

Recovery is not one universal statistic. Keep separate processes for:

- HP recovery;
- Stamina recovery;
- Will Power recovery;
- removal or recovery from individual negative conditions.

Endurance may influence selected physical recovery and condition-resolution processes, but it is not a universal resistance or universal recovery multiplier. Each resource and condition must specify its own blockers, treatment, and recovery rules.

# 8. Explicit exclusions

The current design does not introduce:

- a universal Attack Speed attribute;
- a standalone Evasion skill;
- a standalone universal Detection stat;
- a standalone universal Stealth stat;
- one universal resistance statistic covering all negative conditions;
- one universal action-speed statistic;
- a separately levelled Critical Chance or Critical Damage attribute;
- a universal magical resource shared as a fixed spell list across all Arcana.

Critical chance and overflow potency follow [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md). Ability access and content belong to the Arcana/ability specifications.

# 9. Open questions

- Whether Will Power recovery is exactly the same rate as HP or only approximately similar.
- Exact ability costs and how costs differ among Arcana and ability types.
- Exact rules for Will damage: temporary effective-attribute reduction, maximum-pool reduction, resource suppression, or a combination; duration, treatment, and recovery.
- Exact Movement Speed formula in free movement. On the Combat Grid a cell costs 1 AP (10.2 §5b).
- Stamina-cost multipliers and any movement, check, or combat penalties for High, Overloaded, and Critical Overload loads.
- Reconciliation of the working Accuracy and Evasion formulas of 10.2 §5b with the shared action-check model.
- Physical/Magical Defence stacking and penetration. The working conversion is in 10.2 §5b.
- Exact recovery rules for each resource and negative condition.
- Which derived values are visible to the player at different Intelligence levels.

Do not silently fill these in. The structure and principles above are owner-approved; numerical formulas and system-specific details remain open.
