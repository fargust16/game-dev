# Resolved Decisions

These were answered in design discussion and written into Draft documents. They remain here so agents do not reopen them casually.

## World Laws

**What changes Balance?**  
Faction Influence, which is the reading of what a Major Faction controls and how much force it has, not a separate number (owner, 2026-11-02). A completed Main Goal raises it, and Side Events and Allegiance choices change the war directly. Documented in [02 World Laws](02%20World%20Laws.md).

**How does the World Engine choose the next faction operation?**  
From the World Seed starting state plus each Major Faction's current Main Goal, requirements, and eligible Operations. Documented in [02 World Laws](02%20World%20Laws.md).

**How does the player read Influence without a meter?**  
Through everyday Symptoms: trade, services, encounters, rumours, otherworldly pressure, and related world changes. Exact stage-to-symptom tables remain open below.

**Starting faction?**  
Every character begins Uninitiated and Factionless and must earn Faction Entry. The Starting Faction was retired on 2026-11-02. Documented in [07 Character Life](07%20Character%20Life.md).

**Difficulty settings?**

Eight independent sliders replace the old bundled Easy / Normal / Hard / Hell scale. World Inhabitants also owns the angle of NPC sight, since the owner folded Vigilance into it on 2026-11-03. The eighth, Cult Pace, owns how fast Cult Forces generate; whether other settings affect faction pressure is open. Documented in [02 World Laws](02%20World%20Laws.md).

**What broad faction structure is established for this setting?**

Three church-associated Major Factions cooperate, with internal-order, industry/research, and external-security roles. The opposing Major Faction seeks to return an insane, sealed Limit 0 being, called the True God or False God by followers. Ancient cosmic powers are the setting-level source of supernatural power and seek to reclaim it through the returned being. If unopposed, it destroys other powerful beings and factions, then people and followers, and ultimately the planet through a later act distinct from its awakening. Before manifestation, the cult wages a war for the cities: three Limit 2 Cult Generals capture cities through rituals, removing other factions' followers without attacking ordinary residents, and the Capital Gate lets the final ritual begin only when every other city is under Cult Control. The player receives a private daily summary of their actions and learns about public events through daily news, rumours, notes, witnesses, traces, assignments, ambient NPC reports, or allied-faction briefings, without a global progress bar. A ritual lasts one in-game hour (60 World Ticks); all three allied Major Factions receive advance intelligence about attacks and send groups to defend the city, and off-screen clashes resolve autonomously. Some difficulty configurations may permit parallel active rituals. Once the Capital Gate is open, the final awakening ritual occurs at the fixed centre of the capital; if completed, the being remains there while the cult and allies contest the capital and cult forces move against any liberated city. The campaign ends either when all three Cult Generals are killed before manifestation, or when the manifested being is defeated; each route presents a rating. After manifestation, the being cannot be delayed or contained, player death is defeat with no same-world successor even if the being is later defeated, and allied followers may assist while the player can join or act elsewhere. Ritual concurrency, group resolution, capture, planet-destruction trigger, final-fight composition, rating criteria, and post-campaign play remain open. A royal institution is influenced by the antagonist; its role and classification remain open. One state with several cities is playable; neighbouring states are background only. The former mind-controlled ideal-world outcome is withdrawn. Two beneficial Minor Factions remain examples: a covert alchemical network and a healing/medical association. Limit 0 belief/anchor/sanity mechanics remain out of scope. See [02 World Laws](02%20World%20Laws.md) and [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**How many rituals must the cult complete before the capital?**

There is no fixed count. The Capital Gate opens when every other city is under Cult Control, so the count follows the number of cities plus every recapture forced by City Liberation. Reported City Control replaces the hidden return threat. The number of cities is CC-2. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**How long is the campaign, and on what map?**

At the middle difficulty it lasts 15–20 game days, about 40–50 real hours, and player skill can shorten or lengthen it. The target world is 3 × 3 tiles, 4200 × 4200 m, with 5–7 cities, ideally 7. Only cities are cult targets. Pressure comes first from information, and distance adds to it. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §2.

**Why does the cult spare ordinary residents?**

A capture ritual turns a city into a Place of Power, and residents are needed alive as sacrifices. At the Capital Awakening Ritual, followers conduct smaller rituals in every captured city, and the people of those cities and of the capital die. Residents cannot be evacuated, and the ending reports only a few survivors. Manifestation succeeds only if every city was captured at the moment of the capital ritual and that ritual was not interrupted. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §§5–6.

**How does the player learn what the rituals are for? (CC-21)**

Only at manifestation, so a player who wins before it never learns the purpose, which stays a reason to play again. Nobody in the world knows, a first-time player can only guess, and the allied factions treat the rituals with scepticism because they claim no civilian victims. The knowledge carries to later worlds as Player Knowledge only: no dialogue lets a character convince NPCs of it. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §5.

**Does anyone in the cult outrank the Generals, and how does the campaign end before manifestation? (CC-9)**

No living follower outranks them. The Limit Occupancy Rule leaves no Limit 1 on an Arcana whose Limit 0 is occupied, on any Arcana, and this holds for the allied factions too. Killing all three Generals is the only pre-manifestation ending. See [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md) and [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §7.

**In what order is a city captured? (CC-4)**

A Cult Sortie begins and the ritual starts with the attack, at one site the other followers guard. Other factions' followers are cleared, the one-hour ritual completes, and a garrison stays. A full city holds about 20 followers, and one a General has just left about 10. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**What sets the campaign's tempo? (CC-1)**

Generation, not a calendar. Each General launches a Cult Sortie when it has gathered enough followers, and the first sorties around day 3–5 follow from that. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**How is a ritual stopped? (CC-8)**

By leaving its Ritual Circle short of conductors for 10 ticks with nobody coming to take their places (owner, 2026-11-05), by killing the senior who started it, or, while the cult has not fully taken the city, by exploiting one of its Ritual Weak Points, which differ from ritual to ritual. A new ritual can be held in a city two game days after the previous one there. A fallen city is combat only. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**When does a character's death become final before manifestation? (CC-18)**

When no uncaptured city is free of battle: the Last Free City Rule. A manifestation during a Death Gap therefore leaves no successor. See [07 Character Life](07%20Character%20Life.md).

**What makes cult followers retreat? (CC-23)**

A Force Estimate very strongly against them. They head for the nearest city under Cult Control or the nearest cult force, and they can be chased while they stay in the fight. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**How are the two sides' forces generated?**

Asymmetrically. Allied forces are generated at world creation and replaced slowly at certain Limits. Cult Forces generate much faster, are not tied to the population's general rules, and are not exhausted by killing rank-and-file followers; new followers keep appearing until the cult's leadership is destroyed. Campaign pace depends on difficulty. Rates and sources are CC-12 and CC-13; the owning setting is CC-14. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

## Decision Engine

**Are the five axes accepted?**  
Yes: Attention, Approach, Allegiance, Commitment, Bargain.

**Which axis does V1 teach first?**  
Attention first, Approach immediately with it; Bargain as the second major lesson. Documented in [03 Decision Engine](03%20Decision%20Engine.md).

**Do Side Events give free bonuses?**  
No. They are Allegiance forks with useful different outcomes, hidden Influence residue, and possible path open/close effects.

**What is Knowledge for?**  
Player Knowledge unlocks reasoning, important places, methods, and prediction. It is not primarily a success-rate bonus. Character arcana power is Progression design constrained by Price of Power and Bargain.

**External powers / churches / curses?**  
Always Bargains with readable, often delayed escalating prices. Detailed systems deferred to content lists; rules documented in [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md).

**Two-axis rule for V1 nodes?**  
Important Decision Nodes require at least two axes. Rare one-axis moments are allowed only as non-major onboarding, not as main forks. Documented in [03.1 Decision Node Template](03.1%20Decision%20Node%20Template.md).

**How are Side Events delivered in the world?**  
As timed Points of Interest with lazy activation, readable Active Windows, and unattended resolution weighted by the forces present if missed. Documented in [04 Points of Interest](04%20Points%20of%20Interest.md).

## Progression

**How many Arcana in V1?**  
Two player Arcana with different styles of play, out of 22 in the game, one for each Tarot Major Arcana archetype. The other 16 follow after the slice. Documented in [05.1 Arcana and Limits](05.1%20Arcana%20and%20Limits.md).

**Which Limits in V1?**  
The player reaches 9 → 8, and Limit 7 is for NPCs only. Deeper Limits later.

**How is an Arcana obtained?**  
A faction soft-nudges toward one Arcana after Faction Entry; Arcana may also be gained through Bargain, quest, or discovery. Not a prison. Uninitiated remains valid.

**What belongs in the first Progression draft?**  
Arcana rules plus churches/entities as External Power Bargains. Lore names, full lists, and Limits below 7 come later.

**What is Alchemy's role in Arcana advancement?**

Alchemy is a separate potion-only profession. Every character must learn it before advancing a Limit, and each transition requires the correct Alchemy-brewed potion. Brewing takes six world ticks and includes a mini-game. See [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md); implementation details and balance remain open.

**What preparation is required for a Limit transition?**  
Each transition requires exactly two primary attributes and one secondary attribute. The two primary attributes are stable across all Limits of an Arcana; the secondary attribute is fixed per Arcana. Requirements are evaluated for the target Limit. Primary deficits cause substantially greater additional Sanity loss than secondary deficits; any shortfall causes a penalty, which grows with the gap. See [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md).

**Is Arcana advancement itself random, and what happens when sanity is lost?**  
No transition success roll: consuming the correct potion always grants the target Limit. The mandatory transition Sanity cost and any preparation penalty are subtracted from current Sanity. If sanity is lost, advancement remains successful, but the character becomes a game-controlled creature in the world rather than an ordinary player character. At Limits 9–6 the creature is human-shaped (zombie-like). At Limits 5–0 an Arcana- and stage-specific mystical form becomes possible, but is only a chance; the character may remain human-shaped. See [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md).

## Items, Inventory, Equipment, Item Creation, Enchanting, and Alchemy

Item and equipment rules are consolidated in [19 Items, Crafting, Inventory, Equipment, and Enchanting](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md); potion profession rules are in [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md).

- Item Creation and Enchanting are equipment/item-crafting professions; Alchemy is a separate potion-only profession required for Arcana advancement. Luck and Perception are attributes, not professions.
- Item Creation skill levels 1, 11, 21, …, 91 unlock item Limits 9, 8, …, 0. Its delegated starting formula generates 0–6 modifiers; Perception adds up to 10% relative chance and Luck applies the shared universal multiplier. Fixed base type weights are unaffected.
- Each base gives favored modifier types higher weights and other types lower weights. Higher item stages increase the weight of stronger modifier tiers without a hard tier ceiling. Exact tables/ranges and duplicate constraints remain open.
- Major/minor limits remain 3+3. Group assignment is random; the exact valid-split distribution and random-add group choice remain open.
- Quality is count-based: Normal 0; Magic 1–2; Rare 3; Epic 4; Legendary 5–6; Unique is separate. The category changes only when a count crosses a band; colour directions are specified in 19.
- Enchanting unlocks six steps in order: random addition; full reforge; locking; extraction/insertion; raising one tier; changing values of all unlocked modifiers while preserving identity and tier. Numeric skill thresholds and operation costs remain open.
- Insertion accepts any base only when it has a free slot in the modifier's own group. A broken item cannot be enchanted, but an unlocked modifier may be extracted from it; extraction destroys the source.
- The bag inventory has unlimited item entries and no grid footprints; weight is the only carrying limit, with a Strength-based capacity and zero-weight coins.
- Durability applies to hand equipment, the worn armour set, and gathering tools; jewellery and belts have none. Repair uses some ordinary creation ingredients and no rare ingredient.
- Ordinary monsters drop ingredients only. NPC equipped items are checked independently at a 35% base chance, modified by the current looter's Luck; Perception does not affect recovery. Transformed-NPC drops remain undecided.
- Death does not automatically transfer the former character's carried/equipped inventory or extracted modifier instances. A former character found as a world actor and defeated uses the same NPC equipment-recovery rule; Legacy Cache eligibility remains open.

See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) for the full specification and the remaining system questions.

## Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks

**Are there global character levels or a character-wide XP bar?**  
No. Each attribute and individual skill has its own action-based progression track. Ordinary development is separate from Limits. See [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

**What is the attribute range?**  
Seven attributes use 1–100. Levels 1–99 are ordinary progression; 100 is an exceptional apex requiring special criteria. Attribute bands are not automatically Limits or skill levels.

**How do primary attributes and skills combine for an action check?**  
They have equal importance. Effective primary preparation is their arithmetic mean on a comparable task-difficulty scale. A high attribute can help a character enter a new profession, but it does not make the character a professional master. There is no additional mismatch penalty beyond the average.

**How do secondary attributes affect success?**  
Only positively. All applicable secondary attributes have equal weight; together they can add at most 10% of base success probability, applied relatively, not as percentage points. Low secondary values never reduce the baseline chance.

**How does Luck affect probability?**  
Luck is separate from the secondary-attribute bonus and uses the shared relative formula `P_after = min(0.99, P_before × (1 + Luck / 100))` for declared probability checks. Luck may modify overall success and conditional critical chance separately, and does not rewrite fixed weighted content tables. See [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

**What are the three action outcomes?**  
Failure, normal success, and critical success are mutually exclusive and sum to 100%. Critical chance is conditional on success, not a separate independent roll. Gathering animation cycles are individual attempts.

**What is the derived-statistics structure?**
The agreed register and principles are in [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md). Derived values are calculated from attributes, skills, equipment, Arcana, abilities, conditions, and context; they are not additional independent progression tracks by default.

**How do maximum HP, Stamina, and Will Power scale with Limit?**
All three maximum resource pools use one universal Limit Multiplier S, regardless of Arcana type. A character without an Arcana uses S = 1.00. The agreed formulas are HPmax = (20 + 0.8 × Endurance) × S; Staminamax = (20 + 1.6 × Endurance) × S; and WPmax = (20 + 0.8 × Will) × S. The shared Limit table is 9→0: 1.00, 1.15, 1.32, 1.52, 1.75, 2.01, 2.31, 2.66, 3.06, 3.52. Advancing a stage increases maximum capacity but does not automatically refill the current resource amount. See [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md). Equipment/effect stacking order, Stamina action costs, and other detailed interactions remain open.

**How do Will and Will Power work?**
Will is the ordinary 1–100 attribute; Will Power is a separate visible spendable resource. Arcana determine ability access, while Will determines capacity. Maximum Will Power is \((20+0.8\times Will)\times S\), with S determined by Limit (9→0 multipliers: 1.00, 1.15, 1.32, 1.52, 1.75, 2.01, 2.31, 2.66, 3.06, 3.52). Ordinary regeneration is about 1% per 10 world ticks; sleep normally fully restores it. Full exhaustion grants one modest Will XP reward per continuous episode and causes Sanity damage. See [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md). Ability costs, exact XP eligibility, D₀, and effect-specific Will damage remain open.

**How does Sanity work?**
Sanity is a separate 0–100 mental-integrity measure, distinct from Will and Will Power. Its bands are 100 Stable, 70–99 Mild disturbance, 40–69 Significant disturbance, 10–39 Severe disturbance, 1–9 Critical, and 0 Loss of sanity. Shared Will mitigation is \(D_{sanity}=D_{base}\times100/(100+Will)\). Full-exhaustion damage is \(D_0\times S\times100/(100+Will)\). Ordinary Will Power recovery does not restore Sanity, and a full sleep restores a little. At zero, the character permanently loses human personality and becomes a game-controlled creature in the world; this is distinct from death. See [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md). Resolved for the slice, 2026-11-04 (authoring defaults): band symptoms and penalties, D₀ = 5, and 3 Sanity from a sleep of 6 hours or more ([07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md)). Source-specific mitigation and other recovery methods remain open.

**How is carrying load handled?**
Comfortable Carrying Capacity is `20 kg + Strength`; load bands are Normal ≤100%, High >100–125%, Overloaded >125–150%, and Critical Overload >150–200%. Pickup above 200% is blocked. Exact exertion multipliers and additional movement/check/combat penalties remain open. See [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md).

**How is defence represented?**
Two derived defence values are agreed: Physical Defence and Magical Defence. Special mechanics such as blocking, parrying, barriers, absorption, or reflection may come from specific equipment and abilities rather than universal extra stats.

**May combat become the main progression engine?**  
No. Confront constraints forbid XP-from-kills, abstract loot tables, and universal best payoff. Documented in [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md).

**What is V1 Confront presentation?**

Through reusable Information Sources with bias and partial truth: boards, newspapers, bartenders, rumours, faction contacts, witnesses, physical traces, Soft Memory. Documented in [09 Information Sources](09%20Information%20Sources.md).

**What is the combat action economy?**  
Combat uses Initiative and an AP pool with variable action costs and retained end-of-turn AP for defensive reactions, as defined in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md). **How do sleep, healing, and First Aid work?**  
Sleep advances approximately 6–8 in-game hours while the world continues simulating; it can be interrupted and is not guaranteed safe. It fully restores ordinary HP only when no active condition blocks recovery, and it restores a little Sanity. Poison and Bleeding may naturally end through per-tick probability checks, but sleep does not guarantee their removal. First Aid treats specific conditions and injuries; doctors and specialists provide more complete treatment. No HP-restoring potions are planned. Exact rates, probabilities, injury categories, comfort bonuses, and treatment recipes remain open in [18 World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md). The slice's forms of sleep and First Aid are owner-approved ([11 Vertical Slice](11%20Vertical%20Slice.md)). **GEO-4. Coast contract. [before Stage 4C and G6] — target direction RESOLVED 2026-10-21 (owner direction); production contract open.** Every relief term today is exactly zero at and seaward of the shore and gated over the 200 m coastal rise, so the land meets the sea on one gentle profile everywhere. The reference shows cliff coasts, terraced coves and headlands. Should relief be allowed to rise steeply immediately inland of an unchanged shoreline?
- *Options:* (a) keep the gate; coastal character only within it (Stage 4C as designed); (b) allow steep relief just inland of the shore with the land mask bit-identical; (c) allow the shoreline itself to move (a Stage 1 reopening).
- *Consequences:* (a) no sea cliffs or headlands; (b) a new coastal contract and new tests that the mask does not move; road, POI and settlement siting near the shore must be re-measured; (c) moves golden hashes and every downstream input.
- *No recommendation.*
- *Evidence since (G3):* the 200 m gate removes only 2–6 % of a central mountain system's volume — it constrains coasts, not the mountains; G3 kept it unchanged.
- *Evidence since (G3b, research record §12.4, §12.8; not an answer):* for drainage the 200 m gate is the binding constraint — the gated ramp sends water straight to the shore and keeps every basin at ≤ 0.21 km². A sensitivity with a 100 m gate (`G3-net-g100`, evidence, not a proposal) roughly doubled tributaries (6–13 per seed) and reached Strahler order 3 on one seed, but left 116 shallow S8 depressions across all seven seeds, because its troughs and floors were built for a 200 m gate; a shorter gate would need its own trough design. The land mask and the ocean stayed unchanged in every variant.
- *Owner direction (2026-10-20, G2 task and brief D5):* spatially varied coastal transitions are the preferred direction — beaches and low shores, river mouths and coastal lowlands, coves and inlets, cliffs and steep coastal mountains — caused by the inland geography, not one uniform ramp. In G3c the shoreline outline is **fixed** within each footprint and the coastal elevation profile varies by segment with its hinterland. **Still open:** the production coastal contract — options (a)–(c) above — and which stage owns the profile, given that `H1` is golden-hashed Stage 1 height (brief §6.3).
- *Evidence since (G3c, research record §14.7; not an answer):* a per-segment coastal profile behind a fixed shoreline — low at outlets and lowland fronts, rocky before uplands, cliffs before the highland and plateaus — gave coastal settlement sites on all twenty lattices, against none on any surface measured in G2, and shore classes consistent with their hinterland on 19 of 20. The shoreline is the contour's by construction (land is written at 24.5 m or above, sea cells are copied from Stage 1).
- *Decision record (2026-10-21, review §2.4).* **Target — Resolved — owner direction (2026-10-20, 2026-10-21):** varied coasts caused by their hinterland — beaches and low shores at lowland fronts and outlets, coves at valley mouths, rocky shores and headlands, cliffs where high ground meets the sea — never one uniform ramp; option (a) as the coast model is therefore not the target. **Production contract — Research required, then Proposed at G4:** (b) against (c) and the owning stage, given `H1`'s golden hashes; default for research **(b)**, with planform variety measured and left to G3d after a model is promising. **Caveat:** G3c's R6 checks classes the construction assigned by hinterland and passed while the views show an over-continuous cliff band, so it is **not** validation of coastline diversity; contours A and B differ in shape and area. [before G6] — policy RESOLVED 2026-10-21: (b); realisation research required.** The hydrology design, whose MVP the owner approved on 2026-09-03 ([HYDROLOGY_GENERATION_PLAN.md](unity/Assets/Plans/HYDROLOGY_GENERATION_PLAN.md) §§1, 9, 17), routes every river system to the ocean and fills every closed depression to its spill level (no endorheic basin); V0 water is ocean only. - *Consequences:* (a) basins appear only as filled flats or dry hollows; (b) needs B4 (basin depth) and Stage 3F F2, plus inland water rendering, outside V0; (c) also changes the hydrology validator's ocean-outlet invariant.

- *Since G2 (2026-10-20):* the approved target D6 includes lakes "where the structure holds them" and rivers reaching designated coastal outlets. That makes this question part of the target but does not answer it: G3c builds no lakes and reports lake-site candidates only.
- *Decision record (2026-10-21, review §2.5).* **Principle — Resolved — owner direction (D6 point 6):** lakes are part of the target where the structure holds them; never placed at arbitrary points, never required on every seed. **Policy — Resolved — evidence-backed policy: option (b).** Three classes are kept apart: a **natural closed basin** the geography declares (enclosure, spill point, outlet), which may hold a lake or be realised as a flat (3F-10, 3F-11, 3F-14 decide realisation); an **intentional lake** — a declared basin with a retention rule (surface at the spill level, one outlet to the ocean, uncarved sill, `HYDROLOGY_GENERATION_PLAN.md` §9); and an **accidental pit** — any undeclared closed depression, a defect.
