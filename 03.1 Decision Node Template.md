# Purpose

This document turns [03 Decision Engine](03%20Decision%20Engine.md) into a practical card.

Use it every time you design an important player choice.

If a situation cannot fill this template honestly, it is not yet a good decision node.

# What Is a Decision Node?

A **Decision Node** is one important moment where the player must choose between meaningful options and the world becomes different afterward.

It is not:

- a pure lore dump;
- a reward chest with no choice;
- a fake branch where all options lead to the same result;
- a combat encounter that exists only to spend HP.

It is:

- a fork with tension;
- partial information;
- at least one Readable Consequence;
- valuable failure if things go wrong.

# When to Use This Template

Use the full template for:

- Side Events;
- Main Goal interventions;
- first discoveries of dangerous power;
- any moment meant to teach Attention, Approach, Allegiance, Commitment, or Bargain.

Do not use the full template for tiny logistics like "open inventory" or "walk left."

# V1 Composition Rule

Important Decision Nodes activate **at least two Decision Axes**.

Rare onboarding moments may temporarily spotlight one axis only if they are clearly tutorials and not presented as major story forks. Prefer teaching through a real two-axis node whenever possible.

V1 teaching focus:

1. Attention
2. Approach immediately with it
3. Bargain as the second major lesson

# Field Guide for Beginners

## Situation

Write one concrete scene, not a system essay.

Bad: "A faction conflict occurs."  
Good: "At dusk, city guards drag an apothecary from his shop while neighbours argue in the street."

## Active Decision Axes

Name the axes out loud.

Example:

- Primary: Allegiance
- Secondary: Approach
- Optional: Attention if the player can walk away

## What the player knows / What stays hidden

This protects Partial Uncertainty.

The player should know enough to reason, not enough to solve the node like homework.

## Options

Aim for 2 to 4 real options in V1.

Each option needs a different flavour of cost or consequence.
If two options differ only by flavour text, merge them.

Include "walk away / delay" when Attention is active. Ignoring is a real choice because the world continues.

## World residue

Always answer: "If I come back tomorrow, what is different?"

If nothing is different, the node is too weak.

## Validation

If any required answer is No, redesign before writing dialogue or combat numbers.

# Worked Example A — Apothecary or Guard

This is a Side Event style node for teaching Allegiance with Approach.

```text
## Decision Node: Street Arrest of the Apothecary

Status: Draft example
World Seed sensitivity: Medium
(seed changes place, names, exact rewards, and which faction each side quietly serves)

### 1. Situation
In a crowded street, guards try to arrest an apothecary accused of selling banned mixtures.
The apothecary begs for help. A guard captain says the man endangers the district.
The player arrives while the argument is unfinished.

### 2. Active Decision Axes
Primary: Allegiance
Secondary: Approach
Optional third: Attention (leave and let the world resolve it)

### 3. What the player knows
Observable facts:
- arrest is happening now;
- both sides want the player's immediate help or non-interference;
- the shop contains medicines and locked drawers.

Symptoms / rumours / clues:
- recently medicines became expensive;
- rumours say banned rites use similar ingredients;
- guards have been more aggressive at night.

Relevant Player Knowledge that can help:
- knowing which side usually fronts for which faction;
- knowing that mercy or brutality here may matter for later Yield Threshold behaviour.

### 4. What stays hidden
Hidden Influence or affiliations:
- small Influence shift toward the helped side;
- exact faction sponsorship of apothecary or guard office, unless the player already understands the tell.

Hidden delayed consequences:
- the ignored side may remember the slight;
- a future mentor, recipe clue, or patrol pattern may appear or vanish.

Information the player can discover with effort:
- eavesdrop on the captain and a messenger;
- persuade a neighbour;
- inspect the shop if access is gained.

### 5. Options

Option name: Defend the apothecary
Axes used: Allegiance + Approach
Method / Approach: confrontation, persuasion, or distraction
Immediate cost or risk: conflict with guards; possible injury, fine, or pursuit
Likely Readable Consequence: apothecary remains free and offers aid later
What may open: medicines, buffs, a hidden ingredient clue, a quieter ally network
What may close: easy cooperation with this guard office; some legal mentors
Failure result: arrest fails, shop is damaged, but player learns how this district's power actually works

Option name: Help the guards seize him
Axes used: Allegiance + Approach
Method / Approach: assist arrest, testify, or block escape
Immediate cost or risk: public blame from locals who trust the apothecary
Likely Readable Consequence: guards gain control of the shop and favour the player
What may open: weapon/skill mentor, patrol information, legal protection in this district
What may close: apothecary aid path; soft access to banned mixture knowledge
Failure result: apothecary escapes with a secret, and the player learns what the guards truly wanted from the shop

Option name: Stalk and eavesdrop first
Axes used: Attention + Approach
Method / Approach: stealth observation before committing
Immediate cost or risk: spends world-time; the arrest may conclude without the player
Likely Readable Consequence: player hears who ordered the arrest or where contraband is hidden
What may open: a smarter later intervention; barter leverage; Yield Threshold advantage
What may close: the clean public "heroic save" window
Failure result: spotted, trusted less by both sides, but gains a partial clue about Influence in the district

Option name: Walk away
Axes used: Attention
Method / Approach: non-intervention
Immediate cost or risk: loses direct rewards from both sides
Likely Readable Consequence: by next day the street result is visible through Symptoms
What may open: later investigation of aftermath, Legacy traces, rumour chains
What may close: both immediate mentor/medicine offers
Failure result: not a fail state; the world resolves the fork and teaches the cost of delay

### 6. World residue
Faction Influence shift: small hidden shift toward the supported side, or toward whoever wins if the player walks away
Symptom changes: shop open/closed, medicine prices, patrol frequency, local rumour tone
Path open / path close: mentor path vs mixture-clue path are mutually pressured
Legacy potential: freed apothecary, seized shop, or ruined storefront can persist after character death

### 7. Curiosity check
Hypothesis examples:
- "If the apothecary is guilty, why do neighbours defend him?"
- "If the guards are righteous, why do they want the drawers more than the man?"
Remaining question after outcome:
- "Who actually benefits from controlling this shop?"

### 8. Validation
Creates curiosity? Yes
Teaches something about the world? Yes
Every gained power has a price? Yes
Multiple viable options? Yes
Failure valuable? Yes
World different afterward? Yes
```

# Worked Example B — Attention First Lesson

Smaller node for the V1 teaching order.

```text
## Decision Node: Locked Cellar Hum

Status: Draft example
World Seed sensitivity: High

### 1. Situation
Behind a quiet tavern, a cellar door vibrates with a low hum.
A barmaid says nobody goes there after dark. The hum does not match normal cellar noise.

### 2. Active Decision Axes
Primary: Attention
Secondary: Approach

### 3. What the player knows
- the hum is abnormal;
- the door is locked;
- locals avoid it after dark;
- the player can leave and return later.

### 4. What stays hidden
- whether the hum is a ritual, machine, trapped spirit, or faction cache;
- whether waiting makes it worse;
- the exact Bargain waiting below, if any.

### 5. Options
- Leave it for now (Attention: defer)
- Ask / bribe staff for the key (Approach: social)
- Pick or force the lock (Approach: intrusion)
- Wait and eavesdrop at night (Approach: stalk)

Each option changes timing, risk, and what Symptom appears next.
Failure may wound the character or alert owners, but should reveal one true rule about the hum.

### 6. World residue
Someone else may claim the cellar if the player delays too long.
A Symptom appears the next day: silence, stronger hum, or a sealed door with a faction mark.

### 7. Curiosity check
"What makes a cellar hum like that?"

### 8. Validation
All six checks should pass before the node is content-ready.
```

# How to Design With This Template

## Step-by-step for a solo beginner

1. Write the Situation in one short paragraph.
2. Pick two axes before inventing rewards.
3. Write what is known and hidden.
4. Create 2 to 4 options with different costs.
5. Force every option to change the world somehow.
6. Make sure failure teaches or redirects.
7. Run the Validation list.
8. Only then write dialogue, items, or encounter stats.

## Anti-patterns

- "Good option" and "bad option" where one is obviously correct.
- All options give the same reward with different text.
- Hidden outcomes with no discoverable logic.
- Failure that only says "try again" and wastes time.
- Combat as the only Approach with real payoff.

# Incremental Scope

## V1

- Use the blank template for every important handmade node.
- Require two axes on important nodes.
- Ship Side Events and early discoveries using Examples A and B as models.
- Keep option count small.

## V2

- Nodes can branch into follow-up nodes using previous residue.
- More delayed consequences and eavesdropping setups.
- Seed-driven reward variation becomes common.

## V3

- Dynamic composition from live World Engine state.
- Cross-life Hidden consequence reveals through Legacy.
- Templates reused by generation rules without becoming generic filler.

# Relationship to Later Systems

| Later system | How it uses this template |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Quests | Quests are chains of Decision Nodes |
