---

## Curiosity

Primary player motivation.

---

## Knowledge

Player understanding that survives Character Lives and worlds. It unlocks better reasoning and option recognition. It must not act primarily as a success-rate bonus.

---

## Player Knowledge

Synonym emphasis for Knowledge held by the real player across lives and worlds, as distinct from temporary character power.

---

## Legacy

Everything remaining after a character dies.

---

## Character

Playable individual living inside one world.

---

## Player

The real person.

The only entity preserving knowledge between worlds.

---

## World

A finite simulation containing civilizations, factions and history.

---

## World Seed

The generation input that determines a world's starting conditions, Major Faction states and Main Goal details, Side Events, and related variation.

---

## World Cycle

The complete lifespan of one generated world.

---

## Character Life

The playable lifespan of one character.

---

## Meta Loop

Progression connecting multiple characters and worlds.

---

## Core Loop

Repeated gameplay cycle during one character's life.

---

## Pillars

Non-negotiable design principles.

---

## Price of Power

Every gained strength requires sacrifice.

---

## Experiment

Any intentional attempt to discover unknown behaviour.

---

## Consequence

A change caused by player action, failure, or World Engine activity. May be immediate or delayed, visible or hidden, temporary or permanent. Important decisions require at least one Readable Consequence.

---

## Living World

A world evolving independently of player presence.

---

## Finite World

A world that eventually reaches its end.

---

## Balance

Dynamic equilibrium of competing world conditions. It constantly shifts and cannot be permanently preserved by any Major Faction. It changes primarily through Faction Influence.

---

## World Engine

A lightweight system that periodically attempts eligible Major Faction operations and applies their readable consequences. It does not simulate entire civilizations.

---

## Operation

A single action by a Major Faction with conditions and possible outcomes: success, failure, interruption by the player or another faction, or failure caused by an observable world event.

---

## Main Goal

A Major Faction's primary world-shaping objective drawn from a limited pool. The World Seed changes details such as place, time, and actors. Completion raises Faction Influence and reshapes the world.

---

## Side Event

A seed-generated secondary situation. It offers useful but forked outcomes, changes the war directly by a little and so Major Faction Influence, can open or close paths, and never exists as pure free power.

---

## Symptoms

Observable changes through which the player reads hidden faction activity and influence instead of seeing progress values. Includes trade, services, encounters, rumours, and other everyday pressure.

---

## Faction Influence

The hidden reading of what a Major Faction controls and how much force it has, and so of its capacity to pursue its world-shaping goals and make operations available. It is not a separate number: City Control and the followers in each city are the state. Higher Influence shows as more followers, more frequent attempts, stronger artifacts, and control of economic niches. It is communicated through Symptoms, not a player-facing value. Minor Factions and local groups have none.

---

## Major Faction

A faction with world-scale goals, tracked through Faction Influence and the World Engine, that can directly affect Balance and World Collapse.

---

## Minor Faction

A smaller group pursuing bounded local needs and interests. It may be beneficial or harmful and can support or oppose a Major Faction, but it has no own world-ending objective and is not a direct driver of World Collapse.

---

## True God / False God

Competing in-world titles used by followers for the sealed Limit 0 Being. Neither title settles the being's objective nature or the setting's moral interpretation of it.

---

## Starting Faction

Retired on 2026-11-02. Every character now begins Uninitiated and Factionless.

---

## Character Archetype

A preset start that gives 11 attribute points in a fixed order, against 9 spent by hand. The first three are Боец, Сыщик, and Оборванец. In the full game, archetypes are assembled from templates by seed and may add a backstory, a faction, an Arcana, or both, and an older world may offer deeper Limits. See [07 Character Life](07%20Character%20Life.md).

---

## Factionless

Character state with no faction membership. Default for every life after the first in the same world. Joining requires Faction Entry.

---

## Faction Entry

A required trial, quest, or Decision Node chain to join a faction after starting Factionless.

---

## Death Gap

Fixed short span of world-time after character death during which the world advances and resolves outstanding pressure before the next character is created.

---

## Legacy Cache

An intentional stash prepared for a future Character Life in the same world. In V1 it creates a map marker for the next character and is the simple reliable inheritance channel.

---

## Soft Memory

Lightweight world remembrance of a previous character through rumours, dialogue hooks, and opinion shifts. Not bloodline power inheritance.

---

## Difficulty Settings

Eight independent world-generation sliders for combat difficulty, inhabitants and their watchfulness, resource gathering, crafting, economy, equipment quality, wear/maintenance, and Cult Pace. They do not form one bundled Easy / Normal / Hard / Hell scale; faction-pressure interactions remain with their owning systems. Cult Pace controls how fast Cult Forces generate followers and so how fast the cult advances.

---

## Yield Threshold

An NPC's internal point at which survival matters enough to offer information or surrender terms before death or total defeat.

---

## World Collapse

The terminal transition of a world after Balance is irreversibly broken and a Major Faction's objective dominates. The sealed Limit 0 Being's return is one possible terminal catastrophe. World Collapse is caused by world state, never by a timer.

---

## Decision Engine

The systemic rules that make every important player choice interesting. Foundation for quests, exploration, combat, progression, factions, and world interaction.

---

## Decision Node

One important player choice moment designed with the Decision Node Template. It activates Decision Axes, preserves Partial Uncertainty, produces Readable Consequences, and leaves world residue.

---

## Point of Interest

A seed-driven map opportunity that can host a Decision Node or discovery beat. It stays computationally dormant until the player enters its interaction area or its Active Window expires.

---

## Active Window

The finite lifetime of a Point of Interest measured in player world-time from its appearance, never from its discovery. Missing the window causes unattended resolution. V1 classes: Brief 2–4 hours, Short 6–24 hours, Standard 2–3 days, Long 5–8 days, Persistent until a named world event ([04 Points of Interest](04%20Points%20of%20Interest.md)).

---

## Lazy Activation

Performance and design rule: full encounter logic for a POI starts only when the player enters its interaction area. Timers and abstract world residue still advance without staging the scene.

---

## Unattended Resolution

Outcome applied when a POI's Active Window ends without player participation. Weighted by the forces present and local state, never by opaque meaningless randomness. Leaves Symptoms or other Readable Consequences.

---

## Decision Axis

One of five minimal decision categories: Attention, Approach, Allegiance, Commitment, Bargain. Important nodes activate at least two axes.

---

## Attention

Decision axis: engage the unknown, or defer/ignore it. Primary V1 teaching axis.

---

## Approach

Decision axis: choose how to act when multiple methods remain viable. Immediate companion to Attention in V1 teaching.

Also used for one concrete method from the V1 set in [06 Approaches](06%20Approaches.md): Confront, Convince, Threaten, Exchange, Veil, Withdraw.

---

## Confront

Approach: force the issue openly, including fight or open defiance. Hard constraints live in [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md).

---

## Break Away

Сбежать. A pressured escape from Confront. A costly form of Withdraw after violence has begun. In V1 it is an action of 2 AP, open only to a fighter no enemy sees, 15 cells or more from the nearest enemy; the enemies who meant to strike it search the last place they saw it (owner, 2026-11-12). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §9.

---

## Diegetic Loot

Rewards that exist because a target carried or guarded them, not because a fight completion spawned abstract treasure.

---

## Tactical Combat

Turn-based combat used only for Confront, in place on a square **Combat Grid**. Combatants act in Initiative order and spend Action Points (AP) on movement, attacks, abilities, and other actions. The shared world advances by one tick after each complete round, not during an individual turn. See [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md).

---

## Combat Action Economy

Combat uses an Action Point (AP) pool. At the start of a character's turn, the character receives a fresh AP pool; actions have variable AP costs. Unspent AP at turn end remains available only for defensive reactions outside the character's turn, then expires when the character's next turn begins. See [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md).

---

## Vertical Slice

The smallest playable prototype that proves the game fantasy and core systems without building the full game. It is one city and a small territory at player Limits 9–8, built around the Spirit General's invasion on day 1–2 and a ritual the player must stop.

---

## Veilmouth

The Vertical Slice's city, at the mouth of the river Veil. The name can still change. The slice is a hand-built scene, so its river is hand-made water there; the Overworld's V0 water stays ocean only. Its districts are the centre round the Penny, Parsons' End by the cathedral, the Sleepy Quarter, Salt End, Lower Veil and the Reek, the works over the river, with Fishlip Quay on the east, Crooked Hamlet across the river and Gloomwood beyond it. Its streets carry folk names, among them Long Tongue, Salt Gullet, Widows' Row and Powder Embankment. The map is C5 in [VEILMOUTH_CITY_PLAN.md](unity/Assets/Plans/VEILMOUTH_CITY_PLAN.md) §29 (2026-11-10): C4's map (§28), the owner's, cut into City Blocks. It follows C4, C3 (§24), C2 (§21) and C1, which replaced C0 of [VERTICAL_SLICE_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md](unity/Assets/Plans/VERTICAL_SLICE_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) §2. Its houses are the owner's Victorian Kit, and its look follows [WORLD_STYLE_GUIDE.md](unity/Assets/Plans/WORLD_STYLE_GUIDE.md).

**Crown Axis** — C3 only: Veilmouth's north–south line at px 612, the Governor's Palace, Crown Avenue, the clock tower's corner, the Market Place and Market Street down to the sea (city plan §24.3). C4 has no such axis; its streets run at right angles from the Penny.

**City Block** — In C5, a part of Veilmouth framed by streets and ringed by pavement. It is rectangular for now, and only the Penny's four corner blocks are L-shaped. Its houses stand flush on the pavement, back to back round its Inner Yard (city plan §29). A square, a park or the cemetery is a block with no houses round it.

**Inner Yard** — The open ground behind a City Block's houses, reached by one passage, its way in and out, or by two passages that make a way through. A rich district's yard is a small park, and a poor district's holds mud, rubbish and drunkards (city plan §29.1 item 4).

**Old Ward** — A faint, centuries-old sign over a door's keystone or rune on old stone in Veilmouth. It is dressing with no rule, and never a trace of a Weak Point ([WORLD_STYLE_GUIDE.md](unity/Assets/Plans/WORLD_STYLE_GUIDE.md) §6.3; 99 VS-WARD).

## Lower Veil District

Placeholder name for a district of Veilmouth, on the lower reach of the Veil: a poor/industrial urban area of medicine, night routes, factory pressure, and Warden order. In C4 it lies between Salt End and Powder Embankment, west of Fishlip Quay, with the Black Puddle, Harrow & Tincture and the Soot & Bell; the works across the river are the Reek.

---

## City District

**Pre-pivot / archive:** a named region of the retired seed town scaffold (Ridge, Lower Veil, Undercroft) — radial wedges from a market center. Not a reuse source. Post-pivot, "Lower Veil" survives only as the name of one of Veilmouth's hand-built districts in the Vertical Slice, not as a spatial scaffold; the Ridge was a district of C0 to C3 and is not in C4. See [13 City Generation](13%20City%20Generation.md) (deprecated).

---

## Anchor Site

**Pre-pivot / archive:** a mandatory landmark / NPC / POI placement that every retired city regenerate had to include. Post-pivot, deterministic POI presence is owned by [W.4 POI Placement](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md) in [17 Game Systems Architecture](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md), not this term.

---

## Grid World

**Pre-pivot term.** Square-grid gameplay truth now applies only during Confront, on a **Combat Grid**. Outside Confront, overworld and interiors are free third-person spaces. See [16 Architecture Pivot](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md) and [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md).

---

## Architecture Pivot

2026-08-07 presentation split: Valheim-like seeded overworld, Genshin-like free third-person move, separate interior scenes. Confront runs in place on a Combat Grid since 2026-11-01. Authority: [16 Architecture Pivot](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md).

---

## Overworld Chunk

Seeded exploration zone of the overworld (biome, terrain, POI slots, creature spawns). **V0 chunk size = 64 m**; the V0 playable world is **2800 × 2800 m** (2×2 Unity Terrain tiles of 1400 m), with the chunk API written as if the world can expand. Streamed/chunked intent like Valheim-shaped worlds. No harvest loop as core progression. See [16 Architecture Pivot](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md) and [17 Game Systems Architecture](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md).

---

## Game Systems Architecture

Draft document [17 Game Systems Architecture](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md): systems map authority — System Cards, dependency DAG, micro-iteration backlog. Does not override spatial locks in [16 Architecture Pivot](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md).

---

## System Card

Standard description of one gameplay system in [17](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md): responsibilities, dependencies, public interfaces, data flow, save/load, multiplayer, performance, extensibility, V1 scope, owning folder.

---

## Interior Scene

Separate scene (or equivalent isolated space) entered from an overworld door/portal. Free third-person movement; **no grid**. Generated or templated from Building DNA / recipes. See [15](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md) and [16](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md).

---

## Combat Grid

The square grid that forms around the player during Confront, built from the scene's real geometry and reaching 30 cells in every direction in the slice. It follows the player every step and is built only during a real fight. Fights the player cannot see have no grid and are simulated tick by tick. An interior scene has its own grid. It replaces the retired Combat Arena. See [08](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md) and [16](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md).

---

## BuildingTemplate

**Pre-pivot / archive:** reusable schematic interior cell paint for continuous OrganicTown enterable buildings (D1). Deprecated and not a reuse source — post-pivot interior content targets **Interior Scenes** built fresh by Piece-Based Assembly. See [15](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md).

---

## Building DNA

Seeded identity of one building instance (archetype, district, wealth, style, LandmarkClass, InteriorPolicy, and interior stubs such as secrets/occult). Upstream driver for **Exterior Shell Grammar** and, when Portal, interior scene assembly. See [15 Procedural Building Generation](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md).

---

## Exterior Shell Grammar

Ordered, DNA-driven assembly of overworld building exteriors from an Exterior Piece Kit on a modular Bay/Storey grid: foundation → storeys → roof → vertical → trim, constrained by PlotFootprint and StreetContext. Background fabric does **not** build a Room Graph. See [15 Procedural Building Generation](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md).

---

## Shell Non-Overlap Rule

Hard lock for Exterior Shell Grammar: floor-stack slabs and shell pieces must abut without intersecting. Build bottom-up by storey; party bays emit nothing; step faces use AtticWindow; chimneys sit flush on the roof. Enforced by `ShellOverlapGuard`. Spec: `unity/Assets/Plans/SHELL_NON_OVERLAP_RULE.md`.

---

## AtticWindow

Opening type on a **step face** — the wall of a continuing storey that overlooks a lower roof where another slab ended. Distinct from standard storey `Window` so art/kit can treat dormer/attic lights separately.

---

## StreetContext

Per-face classification of a building plot (`Street`, `Alley`, `Yard`, `Square`, `Party`) used by Exterior Shell Grammar to place doors, shopfronts, windows, and blank party walls. See [15 Procedural Building Generation](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md).

---

## PlotFootprint

Building pad for Exterior Shell Grammar: bay width/depth, shape (V1 rectangle / corner), orientation. **Not** the full land lot — that is Plot Parcel. Selected after Property zoning, before archetype/DNA sampling. Distinct from the assembled ShellInstance. See [15 Procedural Building Generation](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md), `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md`.

---

## Plot Parcel

Geometric land lot (frontage × depth, origin, yaw) subdivided from a settlement block. May be larger than the building pad. Owned by Settlement / Property generation (`PlotParcel`). See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md`.

---

## Property (Settlement)

Logical unit on a Plot Parcel: property archetype, zones (front garden, rear yard, service, …), and a BuildingPad (`PlotFootprint`) for Shell. Not ownership simulation. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md`, [17 Game Systems Architecture](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md) W.5.

---

## Property Archetype

Immutable fabric recipe (`PropertyArchetypeDef`) that drives lot size, setbacks, and which property zones exist for a `DistrictKind` (e.g. PoorRow, WorkerYard, AffluentGarden). Shell silhouette still comes from DistrictProfile + building DNA. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md`.

---

## Property Boundary

Fence, iron fence, or wall edge segments around a Property lot, optionally with a front gate. Lab uses greybox placeholders (`PropertyBoundarySegment`); production TownDress meshes come later. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md` (S2).

---

## Property Prop

Dressing placed inside a Property zone (garden lamp, planter, crate, …) via `PropertyPropCatalog` rules. Tagged with PropSemantic so fantasy/industrial/occult pieces have a reason to exist. Street and building-mounted props are separate systems. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md` (S3).

---

## PropSemantic

`Domestic` | `Service` | `Industrial` | `Occult` | `Civic`. Semantic tag on Property Props — prevents random fantasy clutter. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md` (S3).

---

## Settlement Lane

Service / rear lane strip between property frontages inside a settlement block (`SettlementLaneSpec`). Used by double-frontage and rear-lane parcelizers (S4). Not a full StreetNetwork. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md` (S4).

---

## Settlement Street

Primary or cross street ribbon (`SettlementStreetSpec`) on the Settlement Street Spine. Centerline + width only — not a pathfinding graph and not OrganicTown `TownRoadGraph`. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SETTLEMENT_PROPERTY_V0.md` (S5).

---

## Street Prop

## Road Passability

Which ground a road may occupy, as opposed to what that ground costs to build on. **Mountain terrain is traversable, not a hard obstacle.** Roads may cross it; the planner is expected to prefer cheaper ground — passes, contours, switchbacks — over a direct expensive climb, and it expresses that preference as **price, not refusal**. `MountainPenalty` and `MountainCost` are soft costs and must never become `+Infinity` merely because the biome is Mountain. Only three conditions block a route outright: water / `+Infinity`, earthwork beyond `MaxCutDepth` / `MaxFillHeight`, and grade at or above `MaxWalkableGrade`.

Passability is a property of the ground and never of what was built beside it. `ExistingCorridorDiscount` expresses corridor-reuse preference in **construction cost only** and must not reach any hard-obstacle predicate — read passability from `LandscapeCostField.TerrainCost` / `TerrainCostAtTerrain` (undiscounted) and construction cost from `Evaluate` / `EvaluateAtTerrain` (discounted, what A* prices). **Approved but not Canonical** — reviewed and approved by the project owner 2026-08-31; awaits only a Canonical host document. Source: [WORLD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md](unity/Assets/Plans/WORLD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md) §3; see [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) § Accepted Draft Decisions and [99 Open Questions](99%20Open%20Questions.md) § Resolved Decisions.

## Terrain Bake

The overworld terrain pipeline: **TerrainGen → Unity `TerrainData` → Unity Terrain**. `OverworldTerrainBake` creates the 2×2 tiles of 1400 m (height budget **700 m**), evaluates TerrainGen's authoritative relief onto one world lattice (**Stage 1 landmass + Stage 2 massifs + Stage 4A island relief**, i.e. `H4A` — Stage 3C–3E topology exists but is not read), applies a pending hydrology carve, and writes heights, surface weights, trees and ground cover through `TerrainData`'s own API. Surface and vegetation are project-owned world-space rules (`TerrainSurfaceRules`, `TerrainVegetationRules`); randomness is stateless per world cell from the world seed. Seams are exact by construction. Terrain shading = five canonical `TerrainLayer`s → **URP TerrainLit**; MicroSplat is out of the pipeline. Water look = `Assets/Settings/Water/OverworldWater.mat` (clone of WaterWorks SSR_Water). See [OVERWORLD_TERRAIN_ARCHITECTURE.md](unity/Assets/Plans/OVERWORLD_TERRAIN_ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Island Relief (Stage 4A)

TerrainGen's floor relief: what makes the island's middle read as geography instead of as an extruded coastline. Composition `H4A = H1 + U2 + R4A` with `R4A = M(d) + Γ·(P + K) + A`: a 1-D **coastal floor** `M` that gives the Stage 1 profile an inland slope of at least τ = 0.05, **plate-block uplift** (macro-plate districts with a 20 % lowland reservation), **knolls** (resistant crustal cores, slope-budget capped; none placed on the canonical seeds under the analytic bound) and the **massif apron** (talus and piedmont, domain-limited to the massif axis reach). All gated so the land mask cannot move. Noise has no role. **The production terrain authority since 2026-09-18** (`GeneratorVersion` 2): `TerrainAuthorityField` evaluates `IslandElevation`, so the bake, the surface rules and vegetation all read `H4A`. Stage 3D/3E still declare their basins and outlets on `StructuralElevation` (`H2`), the surface they were frozen against; Stage 3F carves `H = H4A − C3F` after this layer. See [PROCEDURAL_TERRAIN_STAGE_4_ISLAND_RELIEF_DESIGN.md](unity/Assets/Plans/PROCEDURAL_TERRAIN_STAGE_4_ISLAND_RELIEF_DESIGN.md) (R1 folded in) and [the revision change record](unity/Assets/Plans/PROCEDURAL_TERRAIN_STAGE_4_ISLAND_RELIEF_DESIGN_REVISION.md).

## Triangle Reading

How Unity serves the height between heightmap nodes, measured (handoff §18): nodes as `TerrainData` stores them (integer steps of `size.y / 32766`, 2.136 cm on a 700 m tile), each cell split along its **SW→NE** diagonal, (x0, z0) → (x0+1, z0+1) — for local coordinates u ≥ v the triangle (h00, h10, h11), otherwise (h00, h11, h01) — with tile-edge vertices shared exactly. World x points east and z north; with lattice spacing `step`, x0 = ⌊x / step⌋, z0 = ⌊z / step⌋, u = x / step − x0, v = z / step − z0, and h_ij is the stored height of node (x0 + i, z0 + j). `Terrain.SampleHeight` and the `TerrainCollider` match it within 0.4 mm; bilinear interpolation does not (up to 2.59 m at twisted cell centres). **Proposed** as the point-query contract of the **Final Terrain Surface** (3F-6).

## Geographic Hierarchy

**Vocabulary proposed — owner review pending; the target it serves was approved on 2026-10-20** as D6 in [GEOGRAPHY_TARGET_BRIEF_G2.md](unity/Assets/Plans/GEOGRAPHY_TARGET_BRIEF_G2.md) §9 (a regional geography with a landform grammar, from the owner's concept image — a project reference, not an official DragonSword: Awakening map). The levels the overworld's geography is described and checked in, each caused by and placed relative to the level above it: **level 0** island footprint, **level 1** relief regions (highlands, uplands, lowland plains and corridors), **level 2** mountain system (main divide, culminations, saddles, major and subsidiary ridges, spur-toe foothills), **level 3** valley and drainage system, **level 4** landforms (plateaus, escarpments, outcrops, knolls, basins), **level 5** coast, **level 6** water. Not a set of generators and not a new terrain authority: a vocabulary for what TerrainGen's stages must jointly produce, with relationship rules such as "one divide tree", "valleys are complements of ridges" and "coasts are caused by what stands behind them". Its **single-dome failure** is an island whose height is essentially a function of coast distance plus one mound. Distinct from the relief *tiers* T1–T3 of `WORLD_RELIEF_PRINCIPLES.md`, which classify forms by height and footprint, and from the heights `H1`, `H2`, `H4A`, which are surfaces, not levels. Since the post-G3c review (2026-10-21): the level-2 mountain system is what the relief tier T1 names — one branching system counts as one T1 (GEO-2, resolved); whether D6's large plateaus become a region-level class outside the tier table is proposed, not decided (**Large Plateau**, GEO-8). ## Declared Basin

**Policy resolved 2026-10-21 (GEO-5 (b), evidence-backed policy).** A closed basin the geography itself declares — its enclosure, spill point and one outlet continuing to the ocean — such as an intermontane hollow, a cirque or a plateau-top hollow. The deleted implementations are **not** a foundation to build on — that is the explicit lesson recorded in [14 World Generation Roadmap](14%20World%20Generation%20Roadmap.md) § Future Hydrology / River Generation, which also states what a future system would have to model. **Owner, 2026-11-09.** Новость. One item of the News Pool, made by one occurrence of an event that has happened. ## Shape Irregularity

0–1 knob scaling low-frequency organic shore harmonics on a lake ellipse (default ~0.35). 0 ≈ perfect ellipse; ~0.45 = noticeable smooth bends; never fine tooth-noise.

---

## Terrain Conformity

0–1 knob scaling how strongly macro terrain (smoothed height / basin) pulls lake shore radius and orientation (default ~0.75). Limited so lakes stay elongated ellipses, not copies of heightmap noise.

## Mountain Occupancy

Hard rule for lake placement: no water silhouette cell may sit on `BiomeId.Mountain` (or steep highland). Soft-pass and min-count helpers must not violate it — fewer lakes beat a sheet through a ridge. Water meshes also safety-clip mountain cells above the water plane. ## Attribute

A broad character quality that affects multiple actions and derived capabilities. The seven attributes are Strength, Endurance, Dexterity, Perception, Intelligence, Will, and Luck. Each has its own progression track; the agreed attribute range is 1–100. See [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

## Skill

A trained, individually progressing capability associated with one or more actions or professions. Skills develop independently from attributes through relevant practice. A skill is not a global character level. See [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

## Primary Attribute

The principal general character quality used by a particular action check.

## Primary Skill

The principal trained capability used by a particular action check, where the action has a relevant skill.

## Secondary Attribute

An additional attribute that may provide a small positive modifier to an action check. Secondary attributes have equal weight, cannot reduce the baseline chance, and collectively provide at most a 10% relative increase to base success probability.

## Effective Primary Preparation

The arithmetic mean of an action's primary attribute and primary skill, expressed on a comparable task-difficulty scale. It is compared with the action's difficulty to determine baseline success probability using the shared curve in [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

## Task Difficulty

The difficulty rating assigned to an action, resource, or challenge. It is compared with effective primary preparation; a mismatch affects effectiveness and outcome probability.

## Luck Modifier

The universal relative probability modifier: `P_after = min(0.99, P_before × (1 + Luck / 100))`. It does not rewrite fixed item-weight tables. See [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md).

## Critical Success

A mutually exclusive exceptional outcome within a successful action check, determined as a conditional share of successful attempts rather than an independent roll layered on top of normal success.


## Itemisation and Enchanting

**Item base** — A defined item identity with foundational properties and a modifier-weight table. A base may be used at an item stage when its required materials are available. See [19 Items, Crafting, Inventory, Equipment, and Enchanting](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md).

**Item stage** — The item's stage on the 9-to-0 item progression scale. It affects applicable resources and modifier-tier probabilities. It is distinct from a Character's Limit and a skill progression step.

**Major / minor modifier** — One of two modifier groups. An item can hold at most three of each. Group assignment is random, subject to the capacity limit; a modifier keeps its group when reforged or transferred.

**Modifier tier** — A strength band of a modifier, numbered 9 through 0; tier 0 is strongest under the current design. A modifier's numeric value is rolled within its tier's range.

**Full reforge** — An Enchanting operation that regenerates every unlocked modifier's identity, tier, and value while preserving total count and major/minor group distribution. Locked modifiers remain unchanged.

**Locked modifier** — A modifier protected from full reforge. Locks are permanent and irreversible under the current direction; successive locks on one item cost more. An inserted modifier is locked.

**Extracted modifier instance** — One non-copyable physical instance extracted from an item. It preserves identity, tier, and major/minor group but not numeric value; insertion consumes it and is allowed only when the target has a free slot in that group.

**Broken item** — An item at zero durability. It remains present and unusable, can be repaired with some ordinary creation ingredients, and cannot be enchanted except for extracting an unlocked modifier.

**Item Creation** — The general profession that creates equipment from bases and resources. Skill level primarily sets modifier-count probability; Perception provides up to a 10% relative increase and Luck applies the shared multiplier. Base-specific modifier-type weights remain fixed.

**Enchanting** — The profession that unlocks random addition, full reforge, locking, extraction/insertion, tier raising, and numerical-value changes in progression order.

**Weight-only inventory** — An unlimited bag with no grid capacity or variable item footprints. Every item has the same-size inventory entry; total carried weight, including equipped items, is limited by Strength-based capacity. Coins weigh zero.

**Comfortable Carrying Capacity** — The starting weight a character can carry without exceeding the Normal load band. Under the current balance rule it is `20 kg + Strength`; the hard carry limit is twice that amount.

**Quality colour** — The agreed display colour family for item category: Normal grey/desaturated, Magic green, Rare cyan/light blue, Epic purple, Legendary yellow-orange, Unique red. Exact colour values and final UI treatment remain open.

**Alchemy** — A potion-only profession, separate from Item Creation and Enchanting. A character must learn it before Limit advancement. It discovers recipes, identifies and finds ingredients, and brews potions; its approved foundation is in [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md).

**Manifestation of the Sealed Being** — The state in which the sealed Limit 0 being has returned to the living world. From manifestation onward, player-character death ends the playable story in defeat and no same-world successor appears, even if the being is later defeated.

**Cult Ritual** — A timed operation of the opposing faction through which it captures a city and turns it into a Place of Power. It lasts one in-game hour (60 World Ticks), with no player-facing numeric countdown; the operation resolves through faction action when the window ends. It runs alongside City Capture. Its schedule, concurrency limits, and detailed interruption rules remain open.

**Place of Power** — A captured city prepared for the manifestation, with its residents kept alive as future sacrifices. Nobody in the world knows this purpose, and the player learns it only at manifestation. A Place of Power needs no visible mark. After its city is liberated, its ritual lapses a game day later, because a ritual must be maintained, unless Ritual Undoing ends it sooner.

**Ritual Undoing** — Reversing a completed Cult Ritual at its site, sooner than it would lapse by itself. A defender of Limit 5 or deeper who knows the method does it within 60 ticks of reaching the site. Allied factions have the method, and a player outside them can earn it through trust as a quest reward. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**Cult Retreat** — The collective flight of cult followers from every point of a city at once, triggered by a Force Estimate very strongly against them. The order reaches every follower at once, and it ends a running ritual. Followers who see no chance of escaping stay and fight instead. They can be chased and killed while they stay in the fight, and they head for the nearest city under Cult Control or the nearest cult force.

**Social Check** — A dialogue option with a chance of success, rolled on two dice: Persuasion, Intimidation, or Deception in the slice. The listener resists with the same two attributes, and the check needs an Argument. See [10.4 Dialogue and Social Checks](10.4%20Dialogue%20and%20Social%20Checks.md).

**Attitude** — How one NPC feels about the character. It changes the chance of a Social Check.

**Ritual Circle** — The magic circle on the ground at which a Cult Ritual runs. Its Conductors stand round it, the other followers of the ritual group guard them, and the senior who started the ritual leads the guards. A circle with fewer Conductors than it needs is short: the ritual does not advance, and after 10 ticks short with nobody coming it has failed. In the slice it has three places. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**Conductor** — A cult follower who stands at a Ritual Circle and conducts the ritual. A Conductor never answers an attack. When one dies, a guard with no enemy beside it, or another free follower, takes its place. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**Ritual Weak Point** — A way to stop a running ritual without killing its conductors: Сомневающийся, Посланник, Грязная тайна, or Основа ритуала. In the slice a ritual has two, chosen by the seed; in the campaign two at the middle setting and one at the higher settings. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**Argument** — Something the character has gained in this life that gives a Social Check a reason: a clue, a fact, an item, or something it saw. Without one the option does not appear.

**Final Check** — A Social Check against an enemy. It cannot be tried again, and an enemy who can fight starts a fight if it fails.

**Proof Task** — What a friendly or neutral listener sets after a failed Social Check: a task, or a Reputation to reach. After it, the check opens once more or the listener simply tells the character.

**Deeds Record** — The statistic of what the character has done, as in Space Rangers. Reputation and Danger are read from it.

**Reputation** — How good a name the character has with a faction, read from tasks done, NPCs helped, cities liberated, and thefts and break-ins noticed. It changes the chance of a Social Check.

**Danger** — How dangerous the character is, read from kills and Limit. NPCs can judge it by looking at the character. It changes the chance of a Social Check.

**Stealth Mode** — A special action in which the character crouches and stays hidden until someone notices it. See [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md).

**Vision Cone** — The area an NPC sees: 15 cells deep (10 before 2026-11-12), facing the way it looks, with an angle set by the World Inhabitants Difficulty Setting.

**Notice Meter** — The mark over an NPC that fills while the character is in its Vision Cone. When it is full, the NPC has noticed the character. At even odds it fills in 3 seconds at 5 cells ([10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md)).

**Alert** — The state of an NPC that noticed the character and lost sight of it. It raises the NPC's own Perception by 25 %, so its Notice Meter fills faster, and lasts 10 ticks after the meter empties. See [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md).

**Vigilance** — Retired 2026-11-03: the former ninth Difficulty Setting, folded by the owner into World Inhabitants, which now sets the angle of Vision Cones.

**Half Cover** — Cover that gives a small hindrance to ranged attacks, such as a fence. Cover of any kind gives no advantage in melee. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md).

**Full Cover** — Cover that gives a large hindrance to ranged and magical attacks, such as a tree. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md).

**Combat Numbers V0** — The working formulas and values of combat, authoring defaults checked by simulation. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b, and §5f for what the combat rework of 2026-11-13 changed.

**Target Impact** — The score by which an NPC chooses a target: the harm that enemy does to its side plus the worth of its abilities, times how much of that enemy it can take off in a turn. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5a.

**Reaction Attack** — The free blow an enemy holding a melee weapon strikes at a character who leaves a cell beside one of its sides, once a round from each enemy. It is the ordinary blow of the weapon, with no Infusion and no ability, and it can miss or be parried. It replaced Held. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b.

**Surrounded** — A character with two or more enemies beside its sides. Attacks on it get +10 to hit with two and +20 with three or four. It replaced the bonuses for a flank and the rear. See 10.2 §5b.

**Cleave** — The trait of a greatsword: its attack also strikes one enemy beside the target and across the attacker's corner, for half damage. See 10.2 §5b.

**Weapon Set** — What a character holds in the hands. Every character carries two and changes between them in a fight for 2 AP. See 10.2 §5b.

**Melee Skill** — Renamed **Light Melee** on 2026-11-13 by the combat rework; what a character trained is kept. See 10.2 §5f.

**Ranged Skill** — Renamed **Light Firearms** on 2026-11-13 by the combat rework; what a character trained is kept. See 10.2 §5f.

**Point-Blank Shot** — A shot from a firearm while an enemy stands beside the shooter. It aims 10 worse, and a target on a neighbouring cell meets plain physical defence, not doubled. See 10.2 §5b.

**Cartridge Pouch** — The item every firearm needs in the other hand or the off-hand slot. Its cartridges never run out, ordinary or silver; it has its own modifiers and qualities, and the hand on it loads each shot. See 10.2 §5b and [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §4.2.

**Modifier Catalogue** — The working list of modifier types and what each gives per tier, authoring defaults. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §2.4.

**Outfit** — The Shop Skill at which a group's ranks have their gear made. Outfit V0 had both sides buy at a city shop or a capital Master; **Outfit V1** (owner, 2026-11-13) supplies the allies from their industry faction's makers and the cult poorly. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b, §5f and [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.1.

**Master** — The keeper of a shop, who also teaches that shop's profession. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.1.

**Shop Skill** — The skill at which a Master's stock is made, by the formula of 19 §3. It sets the quality on the shelves and rises with World Age.

**World Age** — How long a world has lived. Better items reach the shops as it grows.

**Faction Gift** — An item a character receives on joining a faction, chosen from an offer set by the World Seed: Magic or Rare, and Epic in very rare cases. See 19 §6.2.

**Wait** — A fighter hands its turn to the end of the round. Those who waited act after everyone else, the lowest Initiative first. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b.

**Defensive Stance** — A fighter that has not attacked ends its turn in a stance. The AP it still holds raise its Evasion and its chance to block or parry until its next turn. See 10.2 §5b. Not to be confused with physical defence, a statistic.

**Counterattack** — A blow struck from a Defensive Stance in answer to a melee attack, for the weapon's attack AP plus 2.

**Infusion** — A character pours Will Power into a shot or blow, at a flat cost, and it deals magical damage of 0.40 of the weapon's base on top of its physical damage, with no attribute in it (owner, 2026-11-13; 10.2 §5f). It works with any weapon and needs no spell. Any character who has entered an Arcana can infuse, with no training. Chain halves it, and plate stops it. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b.

**Magical Damage** — An item modifier that adds magical damage to every blow of a weapon, with no Will Power. It is what makes a magic pistol or a magic rifle. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §2.4. Not to be confused with magical damage, one of the two damage types.

**Reload** — Retired on 2026-11-03 by the owner: loading is part of a shot, which costs 6 AP with a rifle and 4 with a pistol, more than any blow. Before that, the AP a firearm needed before its next shot. See 10.2 §5b.

**Load in a Fight** — What the load bands of [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md) take from Evasion and AP during a fight, so that heavy armour needs Strength. See 10.2 §5b.

**Attribute Requirement** — The attributes a weapon or a piece of armour asks of its user; a weapon asks only its Profile Attribute (owner, 2026-11-13). Below them the item still works at a moderate penalty: a weapon hits less often and armour gives less defence, in proportion to the shortfall: at half the requirement, half. An item of stage S asks (10 − S) times its values of stage 9. See 10.2 §5b and [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §4.2.

**Hold Back** — An NPC rule: a fighter that can strike nobody this turn does not walk into the reach of an enemy still to act, and stands in a Defensive Stance instead. See 10.2 §5a.

**Campaign Numbers V0** — The working rates, forces, and off-screen fight calculation of the campaign, authoring defaults checked by simulation. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**Allied General** — A Limit 2 follower of an allied Major Faction on its head's Arcana, the strongest it can have because its Limit 0 head occupies that Arcana. "General" is a figure of speech, and a faction may have several. A named actor who hunts the Cult Generals. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**Divine Blessing** — A short strengthening that an allied god gives from the Divine Plane to one or several of its direct followers, now and then. A Limit 0 head helps only through blessings, predictions, and hints. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**Liberation Contract** — An allied assignment open to other factions, Factionless characters, and otherworldly people: free a city for a stated reward. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**Fatigue** — The penalties of staying awake: Tired from 18 in-game hours awake, Very tired from 30, cleared by a full sleep. See [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md).

**Reaction Check** — A timing check: a marker runs once along a bar with a good zone and, for some uses, a smaller perfect zone inside it, and the player presses Space. The zone's width and the marker's pace follow the character's attribute and skill ([07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md) §8a). It comes at the work's Key Frame, the world pausing for it, but in theft. Used in brewing, theft, picking a lock, gathering, Item Creation, Skinning, and First Aid. See [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md) §3.

**Suspend Save** — The slice's one save slot: written when the player saves and quits outside a fight or dialogue, deleted when it is loaded, and also written after each full sleep and when the invasion starts. See [17 Game Systems Architecture](17%20Game%20Systems%20Architecture.md) X.3.

**Dwelling** — An NPC's home that the player can enter. In the slice one shared scene assembles its rooms from the house's type and id, the same each time. An **Office** is a Dwelling that is no home: a civic building's, the palace's or the clock tower's rooms, assembled the same way (owner, 2026-11-09). See [15 Procedural Building Generation](15%20Procedural%20Building%20Generation.md).

**Lock** — The value a dwelling's door sets against picking it: 2 in a poor home, 4 in a middling one, 6 in a rich one. See [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md) §6.1.

**Lockpicks** — What a character needs to pick a lock; a failed attempt breaks one. Sold by Veinmarket. See [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md) §6.1.

**Stash** — The chest or hidden box a dwelling may hold, with money or an item that fits who lives there, and sometimes papers. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.3.

**Arrest** — The dialogue a Warden opens when it stops a character for a crime: surrender, a bribe, Social Checks with their Arguments, a fight, or flight. See [10.4 Dialogue and Social Checks](10.4%20Dialogue%20and%20Social%20Checks.md) §6.

**Assault** — A blow at a person who is not fighting, when the one struck sees who struck it. It starts a fight at once, fetches the Wardens, and costs Reputation by the harm done; a kill is known to the Wardens even unseen, because the goddess tells them. A wound nobody saw is no Assault and costs nothing (owner, 2026-11-12). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5c, §5e.

**Accusation** — An Arrest a Warden opens of its own for a character whose Reputation has fallen low, paid off by the fine, the lock-up, or a bribe. See [10.4](10.4%20Dialogue%20and%20Social%20Checks.md) §6.

**Skinning** — Taking leather from a killed beast. Unlike gathering it needs a lesson from a Master. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.4.

**Ritual Site Candidate** — One of the hand-built places in a city where a ritual can stand; the World Seed picks one by its own key. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**Modular Kit** — The blocks of houses, streets, and props from which the slice's city is assembled by hand, built to one standard of size, grid, colliders, and layers. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SLICE_MODULAR_KIT_STANDARD.md`.

**Stand-in** — A plain box at a Modular Kit block's exact size, collider, layer, and name, holding its place until the real block arrives. See `unity/Assets/Plans/SLICE_MODULAR_KIT_STANDARD.md` §9.

**Coin** — One of the three units of money: bronze, silver, and gold, 100 bronze to a silver and 100 silver to a gold. Every price is a whole number of bronze, shown in coins. Coins weigh zero. See [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §6.3.

**Beast Territory** — The ground, about 50 m across (30 m before the owner's play test of S5a), that a wolf pack or a boar holds; the pack wanders inside it. The beast warns a character who comes in, attacks one who comes closer or stays, and chases no further than its edge. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md), *Beasts of the slice*.

**Bystander** — An ordinary resident caught on the Combat Grid when a fight begins. It has HP, AP and attributes from its role, can be targeted, and flees on its turns, or fights back when attacked if its Force Estimate gives it a chance (owner, 2026-11-12). When it may join a fight it was not attacked in is open (VS-BYSTANDER). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5a.

**Call for Help** — The cry of a person attacked. It is an Alarm: every NPC within 30 cells hears it and acts by the Alarm Check (owner, 2026-11-12). In the slice it is a line over the head. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5a, §5e.

**Alarm** — Тревога. One event at one point, a shot, a Call for Help or a Warden's whistle, heard by everyone within 30 cells of it, through walls; one inside a dwelling is heard in the street as if at its door (owner, 2026-11-12). Hearers know where it came from. It is no noise simulation, which the game has none of, and not Alert, which follows sight. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5e.

**Alarm Check** — Проверка тревоги. The one rule by which everyone who hears an Alarm acts, by its kind: Wardens run to the place and, outnumbered, whistle and send a runner to a post, joining against the character in an Assault and beside it against the cult, the Veinmarket and beasts; townsfolk walk away or, by Boldness, go to look; beasts scatter, but wolves and glowing beasts converge; Cult Forces call help through the Cult Thought Link. Not a roll. Not Danger, which is how dangerous a character is. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5e.

**Aimed Shot** — Прицельный выстрел. A shot in free exploration with the right mouse button held, the firearm raised to the eye as at a shooting range, the view closer, and the barrel and its front sight the only mark, with no crosshair; a seen projectile flies with physics and is rolled only when it touches a person or a beast, a missed roll being a graze. While aiming, the character walks any way facing the aim, the Aimed Walk. It is an Alarm, and an Assault only if the one hit sees the shooter (owner, 2026-11-12). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5e.

**Boldness** — Смелость. A resident's number from 0 to 1, drawn once by the seed from its name, that decides whether it goes to look at an Alarm; its role sets the share who go, from one in two to one in ten (owner, 2026-11-12; shares are authoring defaults). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5e.

**Sponsored Operation** — A sortie against a city that the player buys from a faction's representative, with money, ingredients, or items, which may mean fulfilling an order to make them. Working label. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §8.

**Stage Factor** — The multiplier that an item's stage applies to weapon damage and armour values. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b.

**Will Contest** — The roll of an ability that a target resists by Will: the caster's Intelligence and Arcana Skill against the target's Will (since 2026-11-13). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Character Notes** — Short records of the facts a character found, one line each in the order found, with no hint of what they are for. They never sort, mark, or link facts, name a place to visit, or set a task, so they are not a quest journal. See [09 Information Sources](09%20Information%20Sources.md).

**Force Estimate** — The calculation over the strength of each side from which any group, cult or allied, decides whether to fight, flee, or stay out of a fight. A senior's death or a disrupted ritual counts only through the strength it changes. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**Cult Thought Link** — A chain of command at a distance, not a shared mind. It carries orders from above and reports from below, only in exceptional cases that need everyone to act together. A reported fight can draw the city's followers, and those of the nearest city.

**Divine Plane** — The unreachable realm where every Limit 0 being has been since a past cataclysm. They cannot materialise in the real world without a very great number of sacrifices.

**Spirit World** — The other side of the real world, reachable by higher followers of Arcana. The Spirit General lives there. It is not the Divine Plane. The player cannot enter it for now.

**Sephira** — One of the ten Kabbalistic spheres that a Limit corresponds to, from Malkuth at Limit 9 to Keter at Limit 0.

**Limit Occupancy Rule** — On every Arcana, while Limit 0 is occupied there is no Limit 1; while it is unoccupied there are at most three Limit 1 practitioners at a time. When someone reaches Limit 0, every Limit 1 practitioner of that Arcana moves back to Limit 2. Limit 2 has no limit.

**Cult Sortie** — A General's attempt on a city, launched when it has gathered a certain number of followers. It can succeed or fail depending on the city's defence.

**Last Free City Rule** — A new character can appear only while at least one uncaptured city has no battle in it. Otherwise a character's death is final, even before manifestation.

**Unclaimed** — The City Control value of a city liberated with no allied faction present. The first allied faction whose follower arrives claims it. A Cult Sortie takes it with the ritual alone when nobody resists openly.

**City Control** — The state saying who holds a city: Allied Control by one allied Major Faction, Cult Control, or Unclaimed. It changes only when City Capture or City Liberation completes, or when a faction claims an Unclaimed city. Reported City Control is the player's public reading of campaign progress. See [21 Cult Campaign and City Control](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md).

**City Capture** — The cult taking a city: a Cult Sortie begins, the ritual starts trying to run, every follower of other factions is driven out or killed, the one-hour ritual completes, and a garrison holds the city. Ordinary residents are not attacked.

**City Liberation** — The return of a city from Cult Control by driving out or destroying every cult follower in it.

**Capital Gate** — The rule that the Capital Awakening Ritual can begin only when every other city is under Cult Control.

**Follower** — A character who belongs to a Major Faction's forces. Ordinary residents are not followers. Beside followers, any initiated character who openly resists counts for City Capture and City Liberation: a Minor Faction member, an unaffiliated practitioner, or the player character. See [21](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md) §4.

**Cult General** — One of three Limit 2 followers who each lead a Cult Force and together form the top of the cult's living hierarchy. Their working role labels are the Spirit, Holding, and Raiding Generals; these are not final names.

**Cult Force** — The followers led by one Cult General. Cult Forces generate much faster than allied forces and are not exhausted by killing rank-and-file followers. When its General falls, its free followers join the nearest living General and no new followers of its kind appear.

**Capital Awakening Ritual** — The final cult ritual, held at the centre of the capital once the Capital Gate is open, with smaller rituals in every captured city at the same moment. The people of those cities and of the capital die as the sacrifice. It succeeds only if every city was captured at the moment of the ritual and the ritual was not interrupted. Its completion manifests the sealed being there. It is distinct from the later planet-destruction rite.

**Planet-Destroying Ritual** — The separate act the manifested being eventually intends to perform to destroy the planet after attacking other powerful beings, factions, people, and its own followers. Its exact trigger and timing remain open.

**Daily Character Summary** — A private report delivered to the player at each in-game day boundary. It records the character's actions whether or not they became public knowledge.

**Daily Public News** — A digest delivered at the in-game day boundary from events known to its publisher through valid information channels. Its reports may be partial, biased, or late; it does not reveal secret operations automatically.

**Campaign Completion** — The campaign ends either when all three Cult Generals are killed before manifestation, or when the manifested being is defeated. Both routes present a Campaign Rating.

**Campaign Rating** — A post-campaign summary of the player's record, including cult disruption, official assignments, and aid to ordinary people. It is not character XP or a threat meter; scoring categories and weights remain open.

**Ancient Cosmic Powers** — The setting-level origin of supernatural power in the living world. They seek to reclaim that power and can take greater notice of practitioners as they advance; their identities and influence mechanisms are outside current system scope.

**Playable State** — The single sovereign state and its cities covered by the current playable-world scope. Neighbouring states exist in background lore but are not part of the current playable geography or simulation.

**Key Frame** — The moment in an action's animation where its Reaction Check comes: the axe meeting the tree, the hands done at the ground, the hammer's last blow. The world pauses there, but in theft. See [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md) §8a.

**Activity Spot** — A place in the city where a walking townsperson stops to do something: a bench, a stall front, the chapel's pews, the tavern's tables. See [11 Vertical Slice](11%20Vertical%20Slice.md).

**Two Weapons** — A one-handed melee weapon in each hand, never with a shield or a firearm. Each blow is struck with either hand at that weapon's own AP, and the pair parries as its better weapon. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5b.

**Profile Attribute** — Профильная характеристика. The attribute that aims a weapon or an attack and that a weapon asks as its Attribute Requirement: Dexterity for light melee and light firearms, Strength for heavy melee and heavy firearms, Intelligence for a targeted ability (owner, 2026-11-13). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Light Melee** — Лёгкое ближнее оружие. The skill of light melee weapons, the dagger, sword, axe, mace, the Wardens' baton and fists; with Dexterity it aims them. Formerly Melee Skill. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f and [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md) §10.

**Heavy Melee** — Тяжёлое ближнее оружие. The skill of the greatsword, the greataxe and the maul; with Strength it aims them. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Light Firearms** — Лёгкое огнестрельное оружие. The skill of the pistol and the rifle; with Dexterity it aims them. Formerly Ranged Skill. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Heavy Firearms** — Тяжёлое огнестрельное оружие. The skill of heavy firearms, none of which exists yet; with Strength it aims them. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Arcana Skill** — Навык Арканы. The skill of the abilities of the character's own Arcana, trained by using them. With Intelligence it aims a targeted ability and wins a Will Contest. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Prepared Abilities** — Подготовленные способности. The active abilities a character has ready for a fight, the only ones it can use in one: 3 to 10 by Intelligence + Will, 10 at a sum of 100, more from items; changed only out of a fight, in one tick. Passive abilities take no slot. An NPC prepares by the seed. See [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md) §4.5.

**Ability Pool** — Набор предела. The 4–6 abilities a Limit gives, all at once; the game has no learning or picking of abilities, only the choice of which active ones to prepare (owner, 2026-11-13). The slice's pools hold three. See [05.1](05.1%20Arcana%20and%20Limits.md).

**Shot Line** — Линия выстрела. The line traced at the moment of an attack from the muzzle or the caster's hand to the target's chest, head and hips, one rule for every projectile, in a fight and out of one. Solid obstacles stop it, partial ones make the attack Obscured, and people on it intercept. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Solid Obstacle** — Сплошная преграда. Whatever stops a Shot Line: a wall, a pillar, a door, a cart, a tree's trunk, every collider not marked partial. A target or an area point behind one cannot be chosen. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Partial Obstacle** — Частичная преграда. Bushes, hedges, low plants, haze, smoke, a tree's crown and Пелена: they never stop a Shot Line but make a targeted attack through or into them Obscured; the one the shooter stands in does not count, Пелена's haze included (owner, 2026-11-13). See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Obscured** — Помеха. A targeted attack whose Shot Line crosses a Partial Obstacle, or whose target stands in one: −20 to hit, once. It adds to cover. An area ability is never Obscured. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Interception** — Перехват. A person standing on a Shot Line takes the shot, in 60 % of shots for each, nearest first, before the target is rolled; the shot is then rolled against it. Friends, enemies and bystanders alike; the fallen take none. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Area Ability** — Способность по области. An ability cast at a point, which needs a line free of Solid Obstacles from the caster; it strikes everyone in its area with a clear line from the point, friends too, with no roll to hit and no critical hit. See [10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) *Shot Line*.

**Heavy** — Тяжёлое. A weapon's trait: while its set is in hand, a step costs 2 AP a cell. Not every two-handed weapon has it: the greatsword, the greataxe, the maul and heavy firearms do (owner, 2026-11-13). See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Wardens' Baton** — Дубинка стражи. The Wardens' light melee weapon, which subdues: a person it brings to 0 HP falls unconscious and is arrested, not killed, a beast dies, and a subdued cultist can be questioned for an Argument (owner, 2026-11-13). Every Warden carries it as its second set beside its firearm. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f.

**Will Power** — Сила воли. The pool abilities and Infusion spend, `(20 + 0.8 × Will) × S`. Only a character with an Arcana has it; for one without, Will is only its resistance to losing Sanity (owner, 2026-11-13). See [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md) §3.3.

**Shield** — Щит. Held with a sword, an axe or a mace, it blocks melee blows and is Half Cover, −15, against shots and targeted spells at its carrier (working value, COMBAT-10); out of the slice. See [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f and [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md) §4.2.

**Screen Message** — Экранное сообщение. A short plain line on the screen for 2–3 s that says an action's outcome or a hint, such as "Провал — цель далеко" (owner, 2026-11-12). It never stands on the thing interacted with and never keeps the next try back; one that tells what Intelligence or Perception gates shows only at that gate. Not a prompt, which says what E does now, nor the Notice Board or the Notice Meter. See [07.1](07.1%20Character%20Attributes%2C%20Skills%2C%20and%20Action%20Checks.md) §11.
