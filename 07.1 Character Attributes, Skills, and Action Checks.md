# Purpose and authority

This draft records the character-development and shared action-check principles approved by the project owner on 2026-10-30. It is not a complete specification or implementation authorisation. Agreed principles are recorded separately from illustrative values and open questions.

It defines ordinary attributes, professional skills, and a shared probability-check foundation. It does not replace Pathway progression in [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md) or combat-specific rules in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md).

# 1. Character development

- There is no global character level, character-wide XP bar, or allocation of generic level-up points.
- Each attribute and each individual skill has its own progression track.
- Growth comes from performing relevant actions in the world.
- Attributes and skills progress independently; an action may train one or more of each.
- Ordinary attribute/skill development is separate from Pathway stages and supernatural progression.
- Character-specific development belongs to that Character Life; Player Knowledge persists between lives, not the previous character's attributes or skills.
- A character begins with all seven attributes at level 1. Available skills begin at level 1; the complete skill catalogue and discovery rules remain open.

## Attribute range

All seven attributes use **1–100**. Levels 1–99 are ordinary progression; 100 is an exceptional apex requiring special criteria and/or a special task. Attribute bands are 1–9, 10–19, 20–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–79, 80–89, and 90–99; 100 stands apart. A strongly specialised character may commonly reach roughly 50–70 during a substantial Character Life (target feel, not a guarantee or hard cap); 70–90 is rare/excellent, 90–99 exceptional, and 100 extraordinary but achievable. Attribute bands are not automatically Pathway stages or skill levels. The unique benefit and requirement for 100 remain open.

# 2. The seven attributes

Roles are agreed conceptually; exact derived-stat formulas and action mappings remain subject to their system specifications.

- **Strength:** physical power; melee, physical labour, carrying capacity. Developed by chopping, mining, carrying, blacksmithing, melee use. Contributes to melee offensive capability and physical damage.
- **Endurance:** sustained physical capacity and resilience; main contributor to Stamina for running, gathering, climbing, and sustained work; contributes to maximum HP within limits and helps negative conditions expire sooner/resist them without being a universal resistance. Developed by running, jumping, climbing, prolonged work, and stamina-consuming actions.
- **Dexterity:** coordination, speed, precision, manual control; contributes to movement/action speed, evasion, initiative, AP growth, accuracy, stealth, and precise work. Developed by fishing, ranged attacks, precise tools, evasion, fencing, and crafts.
- **Perception:** sensory awareness and detection of hidden objects, opponents, traces, resources, and environmental cues; contributes to accuracy of targeted melee, ranged, and magical attacks, and may contribute to evasion and initiative. Developed by exploration, observation, finding resources, combat, and detection.
- **Intelligence:** analysis, identification, understanding, interpretation; supports assessment of observations and outcomes and progressively reveals more UI information (probabilities, damage estimates, enemy condition, item/equipment properties, yields, fishing odds, crafting outcomes, effects). Does not grant undiscovered recipes, formulas, or knowledge. Developed by professions, crafting, First Aid, research, identification, and analysis.
- **Will:** mental and supernatural resolve; resistance to intimidation, manipulation, fear, psychological/supernatural effects and curses; also magical control. Developed by resisting pressure/fear/effects, meditation, and spiritual practice.
- **Luck:** independent probability modifier for hit/critical outcomes, rare drops/finds, and favourable random events. Does not grant expertise or bypass requirements. Developed through discovering deliberately placed hidden caches/secrets. Luck is not an ordinary secondary attribute.

# 3. Action-based attribute and skill progression

- Each action has fixed base XP values for each relevant attribute and/or skill track, based on task difficulty; no detailed physical-load/fatigue simulation is required to calculate base XP.
- Attribute and skill XP are calculated independently.
- As a track outgrows an action's difficulty, that action grants progressively less XP. A tiny positive residual may remain but should be practically inefficient.
- Higher-difficulty tasks have higher potential base XP.
- A task far above the character's preparation must not award enormous XP merely because its base value is high; mismatch reduces progress.
- XP required for each next level grows non-linearly: early growth is relatively quick, later growth slower, but finite and reachable (not an asymptote).

Conceptual model:

`XP_awarded = BaseXP(action, track) × K(current track level, task difficulty)`

The modifier, exact costs, growth curve, residual floor, and failure/critical XP remain open.

The task-difficulty threshold for attribute growth is shared across actions that develop that attribute, not reset per profession/resource. Skills have independent progression: Logging can advance through harder trees, Mining through harder ores. There are no strict universal level gates; higher tasks can be attempted with lower effectiveness and more failures. Higher-tier resources are expected to involve dangerous zones, exploration, preparation, and risk.

# 4. Shared action-check foundation

An action check identifies its primary attribute, primary skill (where applicable), task difficulty, applicable secondary attributes, tools/equipment, conditions/effects, contextual modifiers, and whether it has a critical outcome.

## Effective primary preparation

**Agreed:** the primary attribute and primary skill have equal importance and are combined by arithmetic mean on a comparable task-difficulty scale:

`L_main = (L_primary attribute + L_primary skill) / 2`

Their character values need not be numerically equal. If the tracks use different numeric progressions, they must first be mapped to comparable task-equivalent ratings; the exact conversion remains open.

| Primary attribute | Primary skill | Effective preparation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 4 | 5 |
| 4 | 6 | 5 |
| 9 | 1 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 9 | 9 | 9 |

Thus Strength 9 + Logging 1 has the same baseline preparation for a suitable logging task as Strength 5 + Logging 5. This is intentional: high general capability helps enter a new profession without returning to trivial tasks, but does not make the character a professional master. Higher-tier logging still requires Logging to develop. There is no extra mismatch penalty beyond the average.

The baseline success probability compares effective preparation with task difficulty:

`Δ = L_main − D`

**Agreed base success-probability curve (owner-approved 2026-10-30):**

| Δ | Base success probability |
|---:|---:|
| −8 and below | 1% |
| −7 | 2% |
| −6 | 5% |
| −5 | 10% |
| −4 | 20% |
| −3 | 35% |
| −2 | 50% |
| −1 | 65% |
| 0 | 80% |
| +1 | 90% |
| +2 | 95% |
| +3 | 98% |
| +4 and above | 99% |

For fractional Δ values, use **linear interpolation** between adjacent integer entries. For example, Δ = −5.5 gives 7.5%, halfway between 5% at Δ = −6 and 10% at Δ = −5. The lower and upper tails are flat at 1% and 99%, respectively.

This is the base probability before secondary-attribute bonuses, Luck, equipment, conditions, and other applicable modifiers.

# 5. Secondary attributes

**Agreed:** secondary attributes can only improve the result, never reduce the baseline chance.

- A check may declare zero or more secondary attributes.
- All declared secondary attributes have equal weight.
- Each secondary attribute is evaluated relative to the task difficulty.
- A secondary attribute below task difficulty contributes proportionally less; at or above task difficulty it contributes its full share.
- The combined bonus is capped at **10% of base success probability**, applied relatively, not as percentage points.
- The contributions of all declared secondary attributes are **averaged**, not added. Declaring more secondary attributes does not increase the maximum bonus.

For each secondary attribute:

`R_i = min(1, L_i / D)`

The combined bonus is:

`B_secondary = 0.10 × (Σ R_i / N)`

where `L_i` is the task-equivalent level of secondary attribute `i`, `D` is task difficulty, and `N` is the number of declared secondary attributes. If there are no secondary attributes, the bonus is zero.

`P_after_secondary = P_base × (1 + B_secondary)`

Example: at task difficulty 20, one secondary attribute at level 10 contributes 50% of its share. Two secondary attributes at level 10 each still produce a total relative bonus of 5%; two at level 20 or above produce the maximum 10%. Values above task difficulty do not increase the bonus further.

# 6. Luck as a universal relative probability modifier

Luck is outside the primary attribute/skill average and the secondary-attribute bonus cap. It is a universal modifier for **every probability-based event** in the game: action success, combat hit chances, critical outcomes, rare resource drops, discoveries, finding locations or hidden caches, and other chance-based events.

**Agreed principles:**

- Luck modifies the specific existing probability relatively; it never adds fixed percentage points.
- The same formula and scaling apply to every probability-based event. There are no event-specific Luck coefficients.
- Luck is applied to each relevant probability independently. For example, it can modify both an action's overall success probability and its conditional critical-success probability.
- Luck 100 doubles the relevant base probability before the universal cap is applied.
- A probability may never exceed **99%** after modifiers. There is always at least a 1% chance of the opposite outcome when the event is binary.
- Luck does not grant expertise, bypass task requirements, or guarantee an outcome.

The universal formula is:

`P_after_Luck = min(0.99, P_before_Luck × (1 + Luck / 100))`

Here `P_before_Luck` is the concrete probability entering the Luck stage, expressed from 0 to 1. Thus Luck 20 multiplies a 50% chance by 1.2, producing 60%; a 5% chance becomes 6%. Luck 100 multiplies a probability by 2, subject to the 99% cap.

For a three-outcome check, Luck modifies the overall success probability and the conditional critical chance separately; the normal, critical, and failure probabilities are then recalculated using the existing mutually exclusive outcome equations, so they still sum to 100%.

**Progression context:** Luck is developed primarily through exploration and discovery, not by repeatedly performing an ordinary action. The owner intends one Pathway in which Luck is a primary characteristic to develop; in other Pathways it remains secondary. Reaching Luck 100 is therefore intended to be exceptionally rare and practically unattainable for most characters.

# 7. Three mutually exclusive outcomes

Each attempt using this model resolves into exactly one outcome:

1. **Failure:** no normal resource/result; may still grant reduced development progress and have a concrete cost/consequence.
2. **Normal success:** expected result.
3. **Critical success:** exceptional result (for example, extra yield or rare material), only where the object's/action's result table permits it.

There is no partial-success outcome. Let `P_success` be the final chance of any success and `C` the critical chance conditional on success:

`P_critical = P_success × C`

`P_normal = P_success × (1 - C)`

`P_failure = 1 - P_success`

Luck may modify `P_success` and `C` separately. After modifiers/caps, outcomes are recalculated so they always sum to 100%. Critical success is not an independent extra roll layered over normal success.

# 8. Gathering attempt example

For gathering such as chopping a tree, each completed working-animation cycle is an independent attempt/check. Each cycle resolves into one of the three outcomes. The object's result table determines ordinary and exceptional materials; not every resource object needs rare materials. Critical chance is expected to be low and may depend on Luck, skill, attributes, tools, and effects.

Illustrative only: Strength around 15 and Logging around 12 attempting a tree about six difficulty tiers higher was discussed as 95% failure, 4.8% normal success, and 0.2% critical success. This expresses the intended direction (extremely unlikely critical success at a large mismatch), not final values or the final curve.

Failure XP around half normal and critical XP around 125–150% were examples only, not approved numbers. Failure may cause significant tool wear; successful work usually causes much smaller wear. Figures such as 5–10% failure wear or tenths of a percent on success are illustrative only.

# 9. Combat relationship

The shared model informs combat but does not replace [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md). Current combat relationships include: Strength, Dexterity, Perception, and weapon proficiency for melee offensive capability; Dexterity and ranged proficiency for ranged offensive capability; Perception for accuracy of all targeted attacks; Intelligence and Will as principal magical characteristics; Dexterity and Perception for evasion/initiative; Endurance for HP and negative-state resilience; no universal Attack Speed attribute. The exact reconciliation between the shared primary-attribute/skill check and multi-attribute combat formulas remains open.

# 10. Intelligence and information disclosure

At lower Intelligence, the player may receive outcome notifications without exact progress bars, XP, or probabilities. As Intelligence grows, the interface may progressively reveal progress, XP, action suitability, chances, damage, enemy condition, item properties, yields, fishing odds, and crafting outcomes. This is progressive information disclosure, not an obligation to reveal every formula. Intelligence does not create knowledge or grant undiscovered recipes.

# 11. Open design questions

- Secondary-attribute value-to-bonus mapping, thresholds, and aggregation (next design step).
- Mapping/normalisation if attribute and skill tracks use different numeric scales.
- Primary attribute/skill selection and checks without a dedicated skill.
- Secondary-value-to-bonus mapping, thresholds, and aggregation.
- Luck modifier curves, caps, and per-event scaling.
- Base critical chances and their exact contributing factors.
- Probability caps/floors and handling of modifiers above 100%.
- Exact XP costs, nonlinear curve, residual floor, failure XP, and critical XP.
- Tool durability loss and repair.
- Attribute 100 requirements and unique bonus.
- Complete skill catalogue, discovery, and action-to-attribute/skill mapping.
- Intelligence-based UI disclosure thresholds.
- Reconciliation with multi-attribute combat attacks and defence.

Do not silently fill these in. The base success-probability curve is owner-approved. The next design step is the secondary-attribute bonus mapping.
