# Purpose and authority

This draft records the character-development and shared action-check principles approved by the project owner on 2026-10-30. It is not a complete specification or implementation authorisation. Agreed principles are recorded separately from illustrative values and open questions.

It defines ordinary attributes, professional skills, and a shared probability-check foundation. It does not replace Arcana progression in [05 Progression](05%20Progression.md) or combat-specific rules in [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md).

# 1. Character development

- There is no global character level, character-wide XP bar, or allocation of generic level-up points.
- Each attribute and each individual skill has its own progression track.
- Growth comes from performing relevant actions in the world.
- Attributes and skills progress independently; an action may train one or more of each.
- Ordinary attribute/skill development is separate from Limits and supernatural progression.
- Character-specific development belongs to that Character Life; Player Knowledge persists between lives, not the previous character's attributes or skills.
- A character begins with all seven attributes at level 1. Available skills begin at level 1. Alchemy is a confirmed profession required for Arcana advancement; its capability scope is defined in [20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md). The remaining skill catalogue and discovery rules remain open.

## Attribute range

All seven attributes use **1–100** and start at 1 ([07](07%20Character%20Life.md)). Levels 1–99 are ordinary progression; 100 is an exceptional apex requiring special criteria and/or a special task. Attribute bands are 1–9, 10–19, 20–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–79, 80–89, and 90–99; 100 stands apart. A strongly specialised character may commonly reach roughly 50–70 during a substantial Character Life (target feel, not a guarantee or hard cap); 70–90 is rare/excellent, 90–99 exceptional, and 100 extraordinary but achievable. Attribute bands are not automatically Limits or skill levels. The unique benefit and requirement for 100 remain open.

# 2. The seven attributes

Roles are agreed conceptually; exact derived-stat formulas and action mappings remain subject to their system specifications.

- **Strength:** physical power; melee, physical labour, carrying capacity. Developed by chopping, mining, carrying, physical labour, and melee use. It aims heavy melee and heavy firearms and is asked by heavy armour; it adds no damage ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f, owner, 2026-11-13).
- **Endurance:** sustained physical capacity and resilience; main contributor to Stamina for running, gathering, climbing, and sustained work; contributes to maximum HP within limits and helps negative conditions expire sooner/resist them without being a universal resistance. Developed by running, jumping, climbing, prolonged work, and stamina-consuming actions.
- **Dexterity:** coordination, speed, precision, manual control; contributes to movement/action speed, evasion, initiative, AP growth, stealth, and precise work, and aims light melee and light firearms ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f). Developed by fishing, ranged attacks, precise tools, evasion, fencing, and crafts.
- **Perception:** sensory awareness and detection of hidden objects, opponents, traces, resources, and environmental cues; is the one attribute of the critical chance, of attacks and of every other check (§7), and aims no attack (owner, 2026-11-13, [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f); it contributes to evasion and initiative (working reading, COMBAT-6 in [99](99%20Open%20Questions.md)). It also modestly increases the chance of modifier count during Item Creation under the formula in [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md), without changing modifier-type weights. Developed by exploration, observation, finding resources, combat, and detection.
- **Intelligence:** analysis, identification, understanding, interpretation; supports assessment of observations and outcomes and progressively reveals more UI information (probabilities, damage estimates, enemy condition, item/equipment properties, yields, fishing odds, crafting outcomes, effects). Does not grant undiscovered recipes, formulas, or knowledge. With the Arcana Skill it aims a targeted ability, and with Will it sets the number of Prepared Abilities ([07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md) §4.5); it adds no damage ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f). Developed by professions, crafting, First Aid, research, identification, and analysis.
- **Will:** mental and supernatural resolve; resistance to intimidation, manipulation, fear, psychological/supernatural effects and curses; it sets the Will Power pool and, with Intelligence, the Prepared Abilities, and never aims an ability or adds damage ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f). Developed by resisting pressure/fear/effects, meditation, and spiritual practice.
- **Luck:** universal relative probability modifier for hit/critical outcomes, rare drops/finds, NPC equipment recovery, modifier-count rolls during Item Creation, and other chance-based events. It does not change fixed item type-weight tables, grant expertise, or bypass requirements. Developed through discovering deliberately placed hidden caches/secrets. Luck is not an ordinary secondary attribute.

# 3. Action-based attribute and skill progression

**Owner-approved progression principles (2026-10-31):**

- There is no global character level and no Limit-based XP bonus or penalty. A character's Limit does not make ordinary attributes or skills progress faster or slower.
- Each of the seven attributes and each individual skill has its own independent progression track.
- Task difficulty and the current level of the attribute/skill being trained are each classified on the shared ten-stage progression scale. XP is evaluated separately for every eligible attribute and skill track.
- The XP rule is universal across activity types. Woodcutting, mining/stone gathering, fishing, crafting, and other eligible activities do not have unrelated activity-specific XP formulas. Their XP follows the same calculation using task difficulty, the current stage of the track being trained, and the resolved outcome.
- Attribute XP and skill XP are calculated independently. The relevant attribute's task-difficulty comparison is shared across different activities that develop that attribute; it does not reset for each profession or resource. Individual skills have their own progression: Woodcutting advances through appropriate woodcutting tasks, Mining through appropriate mining tasks, and so on.
- Exact correspondence between the task's difficulty stage and the current stage of the track being trained gives the maximum XP for that task stage.
- A mismatch between task difficulty and current track stage reduces XP. As a track outgrows an action's difficulty, that action becomes progressively less useful for its development. A task far above the character's preparation must not grant enormous XP merely because its difficulty tier has a higher base value.
- Higher-difficulty task stages have higher potential base XP, while the amount required to advance a track also increases with its level.
- XP required for each next level grows non-linearly: early growth is relatively quick, later growth slower, but finite and reachable (not an asymptote).
- No strict universal task gates are introduced: higher-tier tasks may be attempted with lower effectiveness and more failures.

**Attempt and outcome resolution:**

- For repeatable work such as gathering, each completed working-animation cycle is an independent attempt/check. The attempt is resolved when that cycle completes.
- Each completed attempt resolves as failure, normal success, or critical success under the shared action-check model. The resolved outcome determines the XP award; failure and critical success are not excluded from progression.
- The same progression calculation is used regardless of whether the activity is chopping a tree, gathering stone, fishing, or another eligible action. The task's difficulty stage and the track being trained determine the progression comparison; the outcome determines the applicable outcome adjustment.

**Conceptual model (exact numeric curves remain to be recovered/approved):**

`Δ_XP = TrackStage − TaskDifficultyStage`

`XP_awarded = BaseXP(TaskDifficultyStage) × K(Δ_XP) × OutcomeModifier`

At `Δ_XP = 0`, the track and task stages match and the stage-based award is maximal. A mismatch reduces the award according to the shared progression curve. The ten-stage delta table, base XP values by task stage, outcome modifiers, and nonlinear XP-to-next-level curve must be specified once and then reused universally.

**Anti-artificial-progression requirement:**

- Repeating trivial, safe, or deliberately self-created actions must not provide an efficient way to raise attributes or skills indefinitely.
- Repeated actions whose difficulty is far below the track's current stage must become practically ineffective for progression.
- The final anti-exploit rules must also prevent trivial cycles, low-risk object resets, and intentionally manufactured attempts from bypassing the shared XP curve. The owner's answer of 2026-11-13 leaves the zero floor of Progression Numbers V0 as the only safeguard (below); the world's own limits, a purse once a day, a node that runs out, do the rest.

**Every use grows** (owner, 2026-11-13, the answer to S5-1). There is no classic levelling of the character: only its skills and attributes grow, each by its own use, and this is a core mechanic of the Vertical Slice. Every use of a skill, gathering, crafting, a theft, a lock, an attack, an ability, a Social Check or any other check, gives XP to that skill and to the attributes in the use's formula, every time it is used, with no limit by round, day, or count. A failure, a missed attack or a bad Reaction Check, gives half a normal success's XP, and a critical success twice it. The numbers are Progression Numbers V0 below, whose zero floor is the only brake. Only the player's character grows: NPCs are made as they are and stay so to the end. A use with no task, a blow at the air or a shot that touches nobody, gives nothing. Perception also grows by the Hidden Caches the character finds.

**Progression Numbers V0.** Authoring defaults set by the agent on 2026-11-04, because the owner leaves balance numbers to the agent (2026-11-02). They are checked by `tools/balance/progress_sim.py` and meant to change in playtest.

- **A track's stage.** Levels 1–9 are stage 9, 10–19 stage 8, and so on to 90–99 at stage 0, following the bands of §1. Level 100 stands apart.
- **A task's stage.** Every task names its stage. An ordinary herb, tree, or vein and a stage 9 base are stage 9; stage 8 work is stage 8. Authoring defaults, 2026-11-13: an attack's or a targeted ability's stage is its target's Limit, 9 for an Uninitiated person or an ordinary beast and 8 for a glowing beast; an ability with no target is stage 9 at Limit 9 and 8 at Limit 8; a brewing is its potion's Limit; a theft, a lock, a Social Check, a Hidden Cache or any other check reads its difficulty on the bands of §1, 1–9 being stage 9 and 10–19 stage 8.
- **BaseXP:** `10 × 2^(9 − task stage)`, so 10 at stage 9, 20 at stage 8, and 5,120 at stage 0.
- **A task at or below the track:** `XP = BaseXP(task stage) × K(Δ_XP)`, with K = 1 at Δ 0, 0.4 at −1, 0.1 at −2, and 0 at −3 or lower. The residual floor is zero: a task three stages below teaches nothing.
- **A task above the track:** the award a matched task would give, × 0.8 for each stage above. A harder task therefore never teaches faster than a matched one, and it fails more often.
- **Outcome:** failure × 0.5, normal success × 1, critical success × 2 (owner, 2026-11-13; × 1.5 before).
- **XP to the next level:** `10 × 1.1^(level − 1)`: 10 from level 1 to 2, about 24 from 10 to 11, and about 1,070 from 50 to 51. With matched tasks a skill needs about 1.5 actions a level below 10, two to four up to 40, and 15 near 95; level 50 takes about 130 matched actions.
- **Attributes.** A use gives each attribute in its formula one hundredth of the XP it gives its skill where the attribute is primary, and half that where it is secondary (§5; authoring default, 2026-11-13), counted on the attribute's own level and stage; two attributes of equal weight in one formula each take the full hundredth. An action with no skill gives its attribute the same hundredth of what a matched action would give. Full exhaustion gives Will 2 XP once an episode ([07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md) §3.3). A Hidden Cache found is a check of Perception against the cache's difficulty and gives Perception its XP as any check does (owner, 2026-11-13), and Luck 2 XP.

# 4. Shared action-check foundation

An action check identifies its primary attribute, primary skill (where applicable), task difficulty, applicable secondary attributes, tools/equipment, conditions/effects, contextual modifiers, and whether it has a critical outcome.

## Effective primary preparation

**Agreed:** the primary attribute and primary skill have equal importance and are combined by arithmetic mean on a comparable task-difficulty scale:

`L_main = (L_primary attribute + L_primary skill) / 2`

Their character values need not be numerically equal. If the tracks use different numeric progressions, they must first be mapped to comparable task-equivalent ratings; the exact conversion remains open.

| Primary attribute | Primary skill | Effective preparation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 4 | 5 |
| 4 | 6 | 5 |
| 9 | 1 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 9 | 9 | 9 |

Thus Strength 9 + Woodcutting 1 has the same baseline preparation for a suitable woodcutting task as Strength 5 + Woodcutting 5. This is intentional: high general capability helps enter a new profession without returning to trivial tasks, but does not make the character a professional master. Higher-tier woodcutting still requires Woodcutting to develop. There is no extra mismatch penalty beyond the average.

The baseline success probability compares effective preparation with task difficulty:

`Δ = L_main − D`

**Agreed base success-probability curve (owner-approved 2026-10-30):**

| Δ | Base success probability |
|---:|---:|
| −8 and below | 1% |
| −7 | 2% |
| −6 | 5% |
| −5 | 10% |
| −4 | 20% |
| −3 | 35% |
| −2 | 50% |
| −1 | 65% |
| 0 | 80% |
| +1 | 90% |
| +2 | 95% |
| +3 | 98% |
| +4 and above | 99% |

For fractional Δ values, use **linear interpolation** between adjacent integer entries. For example, Δ = −5.5 gives 7.5%, halfway between 5% at Δ = −6 and 10% at Δ = −5. The lower and upper tails are flat at 1% and 99%, respectively.

This is the base probability before secondary-attribute bonuses, Luck, equipment, conditions, and other applicable modifiers.

**Exception.** Social Checks use the two-dice model of [10.4 Dialogue and Social Checks](10.4%20Dialogue%20and%20Social%20Checks.md), chosen by the owner on 2026-11-02 for conversation only. Luck still applies to them.

# 5. Secondary attributes

**Agreed:** secondary attributes can only improve the result, never reduce the baseline chance.

- A check may declare zero or more secondary attributes.
- All declared secondary attributes have equal weight.
- Each secondary attribute is evaluated relative to the task difficulty.
- A secondary attribute below task difficulty contributes proportionally less; at or above task difficulty it contributes its full share.
- The combined bonus is capped at **10% of base success probability**, applied relatively, not as percentage points.
- The contributions of all declared secondary attributes are **averaged**, not added. Declaring more secondary attributes does not increase the maximum bonus.

For each secondary attribute:

`R_i = min(1, L_i / D)`

The combined bonus is:

`B_secondary = 0.10 × (Σ R_i / N)`

where `L_i` is the task-equivalent level of secondary attribute `i`, `D` is task difficulty, and `N` is the number of declared secondary attributes. If there are no secondary attributes, the bonus is zero.

`P_after_secondary = P_base × (1 + B_secondary)`

Example: at task difficulty 20, one secondary attribute at level 10 contributes 50% of its share. Two secondary attributes at level 10 each still produce a total relative bonus of 5%; two at level 20 or above produce the maximum 10%. Values above task difficulty do not increase the bonus further.

# 6. Luck as a universal relative probability modifier

Luck is outside the primary attribute/skill average and the secondary-attribute bonus cap. It modifies every declared probability check, including action success, combat hit chances, critical outcomes, rare resource drops, NPC equipment recovery, discoveries, and modifier-count trials during Item Creation. A fixed weighted content table is not itself rewritten by Luck unless its system explicitly says so; Itemisation keeps base modifier-type weights fixed, while the modifier-count and NPC-recovery checks use Luck under this shared rule.

**Agreed principles:**

- Luck modifies the specific existing probability relatively; it never adds fixed percentage points.
- The same formula and scaling apply to every probability check that uses Luck. There are no event-specific Luck coefficients.
- Luck is applied to each relevant probability independently. For example, it can modify both an action's overall success probability and its conditional critical-success probability.
- Luck 100 doubles the relevant base probability before the universal cap is applied.
- A probability may never exceed **99%** after modifiers. There is always at least a 1% chance of the opposite outcome when the event is binary.
- Luck does not grant expertise, bypass task requirements, or guarantee an outcome.

The universal formula is:

`P_after_Luck = min(0.99, P_before_Luck × (1 + Luck / 100))`

Here `P_before_Luck` is the concrete probability entering the Luck stage, expressed from 0 to 1. Thus Luck 20 multiplies a 50% chance by 1.2, producing 60%; a 5% chance becomes 6%. Luck 100 multiplies a probability by 2, subject to the 99% cap.

For a three-outcome check, Luck modifies the overall success probability and the conditional critical chance separately; the normal, critical, and failure probabilities are then recalculated using the existing mutually exclusive outcome equations, so they still sum to 100%.

**Progression context:** Luck is developed primarily through exploration and discovery, not by repeatedly performing an ordinary action. The owner intends one Arcana in which Luck is a primary characteristic to develop; in other Arcana it remains secondary. Reaching Luck 100 is therefore intended to be exceptionally rare and practically unattainable for most characters.

# 7. Critical success: Perception, Luck, and critical-effect potency

Critical success is a conditional enhancement of a successful action. It does not create a fourth outcome and does not change the ordinary success/failure check.

## Perception-based conditional critical chance

**Agreed principles:**

- The base conditional critical-success chance is 5%.
- Perception is the universal primary attribute for increasing conditional critical chance wherever an action's result table supports a critical outcome.
- This applies across eligible action categories, including attacks, gathering, discovery, crafting, and First Aid.
- Perception affects the split between normal and critical success after the action has succeeded. It does not increase ordinary success probability.
- The actual conditional critical chance has an absolute cap of 95%, including after Luck.

The Perception-based chance grows linearly from 5% at Perception 1 to 95% at Perception 100:

`C_P = 0.05 + 0.90 × ((Perception − 1) / 99)`

Illustrative checkpoints: Perception 1 = 5%; 25 ≈ 26.8%; 50 = 50%; 75 ≈ 72.3%; 100 = 95%.

## Luck and the critical chance

Luck modifies the Perception-based conditional critical chance using the same universal relative modifier as other probabilities:

`C_raw = C_P × (1 + Luck / 100)`

The actual conditional critical chance is:

`C_chance = min(0.95, C_raw)`

The specific 95% critical-chance cap is more restrictive than the general 99% probability cap.

## Critical-chance overflow becomes critical-effect potency

**Agreed principle (2026-10-30):** a calculated critical chance above the 95% cap is not discarded. The excess is retained as a separate value that increases the strength of the critical effect.

`C_overflow = max(0, C_raw − 0.95)`

- `C_chance` determines how often a successful action becomes a critical success.
- `C_overflow` determines additional critical-effect potency where the relevant action system defines how that potency is used.
- Overflow is not probability, does not increase ordinary success chance, does not raise the 95% critical chance cap, and is not passed through Luck again.
- The overflow-to-effect conversion is **system-specific**. No universal conversion coefficient is established here.
- Combat adds it to the critical damage multiplier one to one ([10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md)). Gathering may use it to improve quantity and/or rarity, and discovery to improve the quality or rarity of findings. Those two are examples of possible mappings, not approved formulas.

The gathering, discovery, crafting, and other relevant specifications must define their own conversion rules when those systems are designed.

## Critical chance progression and development

Perception has no dedicated repeatable action category that directly trains it. It may receive small indirect XP when it meaningfully contributes to successful detection, discovery, or a critical outcome; exact qualifying events and awards remain open. Major Perception development is expected to come primarily from rare items, effects, and special abilities. A Perception-related Arcana may exist, but Perception need not be its only primary track.

# 8. Three mutually exclusive outcomes

Each attempt using this model resolves into exactly one outcome:

1. **Failure:** no normal resource/result; may still grant reduced development progress and have a concrete cost/consequence.
2. **Normal success:** expected result.
3. **Critical success:** exceptional result (for example, extra yield or rare material), only where the object's/action's result table permits it.

There is no partial-success outcome. Let `P_success` be the final chance of any success and `C` the critical chance conditional on success:

`P_critical = P_success × C`

`P_normal = P_success × (1 - C)`

`P_failure = 1 - P_success`

Luck may modify `P_success` and `C` separately. After modifiers/caps, outcomes are recalculated so they always sum to 100%. Critical success is not an independent extra roll layered over normal success.

# 8a. Reaction Checks

Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: a **Reaction Check** is a timing check. A marker runs once along a bar, the bar has a good zone and, for some uses, a smaller perfect zone inside it, and the player presses Space. Stopping in the perfect zone is a perfect result, in the good zone a good one, and anywhere else or not at all a bad one ([20 Alchemy and Potioncraft](20%20Alchemy%20and%20Potioncraft.md) §3). The three results are §8's three outcomes: bad is a failure, good a normal success, and perfect a critical success.

Owner's direction, 2026-11-11 (the play test of S5a): crafting, gathering, picking a lock and theft are each played as Reaction Checks, because the mini-game makes the work more interesting. The good zone's width and the marker's pace both follow the character's attribute and skill against the task: the weaker the character, the narrower the zone and the faster the marker. Even at its best the bar stays a challenge, so the zone has a widest size and the marker a slowest pace. Until the owner's second direction of the same day, below, the world paused for theft alone.

**Owner's second direction, 2026-11-11: the check follows the animation.** No Reaction Check is timed by the clock. The work's animation plays to its key frame, and there the check comes: an axe's blade or a pick meeting the tree or the rock, the hands done at the ground as the character begins to rise, the hammer's last blow on the anvil, the knife's cut, the bandage drawn tight, the pick turning in the lock. The world pauses for the check, and after it the animation plays to its end. Chopping and mining are one check a swing, the next swing coming after the last has played out. A herb or flax patch is gathered in one go: one check, after which the patch is empty whatever the result, so its animation plays whole, with no seams. Theft alone does not pause the world: it is played like any other check and is called off when the person moves more than 1 m from where it stood when the theft began ([10.3](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md) §6). Skinning and First Aid are Reaction Checks too. Item Creation keeps its ticks of work, and its check comes on the last blow. For the crafts and for theft's metre the owner chose the agent's recommended readings.

**As built, AN3 (2026-11-12): the agent's working readings, not decisions** (`ANIMATION_PIPELINE_PLAN.md` §10):

- Between checks the world runs at its own pace; it pauses only while a bar shows. A swing at a tree or a vein lasts about a second, so a cycle follows the swing, not the tick.
- Work whose ticks the rules count, Item Creation, Skinning and First Aid outside a fight, passes them as the character steps to the work, and its clip then plays to the key frame. A move key does not break such work off; at a tree, a vein, a patch or a pocket a move key breaks the work off before its check.
- Theft is made crouched, as the character steals only in Stealth Mode. A try called off by the person's metre, or by a move key, is not spent.
- In a fight First Aid's check comes as soon as the action is chosen, as the turn stands still anyway; a kneel of five seconds would hold every turn up. Open for the owner in [99](99%20Open%20Questions.md) VS-ANIM.
- A lock and brewing are not built yet; the key frames of their clips are measured for them.

The rule, with `Δ = (attribute + skill) / 2 − difficulty` as in §4; each use names its attribute, skill and difficulty:

- **Good zone:** `20 % × 1.25^Δ` of the bar, held between 5 % and 35 %. The width and its narrowest were owner-approved on 2026-11-03 with a widest of 60 %; the widest is 35 % since 2026-11-11, an authoring default under the owner's direction that the best case stay a challenge.
- **Marker:** it crosses the bar in `1.5 s × 1.15^Δ`, held between 0.6 s and 1.5 s. At even odds and above it keeps the old fixed pace of 1.5 s, the slowest; below them it runs faster. Authoring default.
- **Perfect zone,** where a use has one: a quarter of the good zone, in its middle. Authoring default; brewing keeps its own until S5b brings it to this rule (20 §3).
- **Placing.** The zone lies whole on the bar but never in its first fifth, so the player sees it before the marker reaches it; the seed places it (working reading of S5a.8).

So the player has 0.3 s to stop the marker at even odds, 0.53 s at the best, and 0.07 s at Δ −4.

# 9. Gathering attempt example

For gathering such as chopping a tree, each completed working-animation cycle is an independent attempt/check. Each cycle resolves into one of the three outcomes. The object's result table determines ordinary and exceptional materials; not every resource object needs rare materials. Critical chance is expected to be low and may depend on Luck, skill, attributes, tools, and effects.

Illustrative only: Strength around 15 and Woodcutting around 12 attempting a tree about six difficulty tiers higher was discussed as 95% failure, 4.8% normal success, and 0.2% critical success. This expresses the intended direction (extremely unlikely critical success at a large mismatch), not final values or the final curve.

Failure XP around half normal and critical XP around 125–150% were examples only, not approved numbers. Failure may cause significant tool wear; successful work usually causes much smaller wear. Figures such as 5–10% failure wear or tenths of a percent on success are illustrative only.

# 10. Combat relationship

The shared model informs combat but does not replace [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md). Since the combat rework (owner's concept and answers, 2026-11-13; [10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f):

- **No attribute adds damage.** Damage comes from the weapon, the ammunition, or the ability.
- **An attack aims by its profile attribute and its skill:** `(profile attribute + skill) / 2`. The profile attribute is Dexterity for light melee and light firearms and Strength for heavy melee and heavy firearms. A targeted ability aims by Intelligence and the Arcana Skill, never by Will.
- **Perception sets the critical chance** of an attack as of every check (§7), and aims nothing. Luck multiplies the hit chance and the critical chance (§6).
- **Perception as accuracy's secondary attribute** (owner's direction, 2026-11-13; adopted by measurement in S5b.3 the same day): Perception is every attack's secondary attribute by §5's model, a weapon's blow or shot and a targeted ability's hit alike, the target's Evasion standing for the task's difficulty, at most +10 % of the hit chance, relatively, while it stays the critical chance's own attribute; so it grows with every attack (§3). With it the slice with no player is at 21.4 % against 20.5 % without, and an allied fighter wins 74–77 % of duels at the same Limit, so both conditions hold ([10.2](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md) §5f *Accuracy*, `tools/balance/perception_sim.py`).
- **Five combat skills** (owner, Q3 (a)): **Light Melee**, **Heavy Melee**, **Light Firearms**, **Heavy Firearms**, and the **Arcana Skill**, one for the abilities of the character's own Arcana. Each is trained by its own use. Light Melee is the former Melee Skill and Light Firearms the former Ranged Skill, renamed, so a character keeps what it trained; the owner's direction of 2026-11-03 that a character trained in one kind is weaker with another holds for all five, and CC-41's question of a skill for each kind of weapon is answered by them.
- Dexterity and Perception still set evasion and initiative (working reading, COMBAT-6); Endurance sets HP and negative-state resilience; there is no universal Attack Speed attribute.

The exact reconciliation between the shared primary-attribute/skill check and combat formulas remains open.

# 11. Intelligence and information disclosure

At lower Intelligence, the player may receive outcome notifications without exact progress bars, XP, or probabilities. As Intelligence grows, the interface may progressively reveal progress, XP, action suitability, chances, damage, enemy condition, item properties, yields, fishing odds, and crafting outcomes. This is progressive information disclosure, not an obligation to reveal every formula. Intelligence does not create knowledge or grant undiscovered recipes.

**Screen Messages** (owner, 2026-11-12): an outcome or a hint is a Screen Message, a short plain line on the screen for 2–3 s, such as "Провал — цель далеко", never a note on the thing interacted with, and it never keeps the next try back: no detailed reasons. The slice shows each for 2.5 s (authoring default). A Screen Message that tells something Intelligence or Perception gates, a reason, a chance or a reading of the world, shows only when the character's attribute reaches that gate; the outcome itself always shows. The gates are §12's open *Intelligence-based UI disclosure thresholds*; none of the slice's present Screen Messages (theft, a carcass, gathering, First Aid) tells anything gated. Readings that follow the cursor, such as the hit chance in a fight or a Social Check's chance, are not Screen Messages and stay while the cursor rests on them.
