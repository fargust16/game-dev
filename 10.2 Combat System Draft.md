# Purpose and authority

This document records the combat design decisions explicitly approved by the project owner during the design pass dated 2026-10-30.

It is a design draft, not a complete combat specification and not implementation authorisation. Approved principles below are treated as decisions; formulas, numeric values, edge cases, and unresolved mechanics remain open.

Where this document conflicts with older draft assumptions, the decisions recorded here are the newer owner-approved direction. In particular, the action-point model described here replaces the earlier provisional Move + Action + Bonus structure in [10 Turn Action Economy](10%20Turn%20Action%20Economy.md). That document must be reconciled in a later documentation pass.

# 1. Attack accuracy and hit resolution

## Approved principles

- Hit probability is based on the relationship between the attacker's offensive capability and the target's defensive capability, together with a base accuracy value and relevant situational modifiers.
- Attributes do not act in isolation: the relevant weapon/ability skill, equipment, effects, and circumstances also contribute.
- **Perception contributes to the accuracy of every attack explicitly targeted at an opponent**, including melee, ranged, and targeted magical attacks.
- The target's ability to avoid an attack is represented primarily through Dexterity and Perception, with modifiers from items and effects.
- A target that cannot meaningfully evade (for example, because it is immobilised, stunned, or bound) may have its evasion reduced to zero.
- A ranged attack may still miss an immobile target because the attacker's ranged accuracy and weapon skill still matter.
- There is no separate universal Attack Speed attribute.

## Melee

The approved conceptual comparison is:

- Attacker: Strength + Dexterity + Perception + relevant weapon proficiency + applicable modifiers.
- Target: Dexterity + Perception + applicable evasion modifiers.

Strength is part of the attacker's melee accuracy contribution as well as being relevant to physical damage; accuracy and damage remain separate calculations.

## Ranged and magical attacks

Perception contributes to the accuracy of all targeted attacks. The remaining offensive attributes and skills depend on the attack type and its eventual rules. Do not assume that melee Strength applies to ranged or magical attacks.

## Base values and formula

A base accuracy / base evasion model is intended, but values such as 50% are illustrative only. The final probability function, caps/floors, scaling, and the exact contribution of each attribute/skill are not decided.

# 2. Defence

## Evasion

- Evasion applies against melee, ranged, and magical attacks.
- Evasion is derived from relevant attributes, principally Dexterity and Perception, and can be modified by equipment and effects.
- There is **no standalone Evasion skill**. The owner rejected it because there is no sufficiently clear, abuse-resistant, meaningful way to train it.
- Character states such as immobilisation, stun, or binding can reduce or remove the ability to evade.
- Ranged attacks retain an independent chance to miss even when the target cannot evade.

## Blocking and parrying

- Blocking and parrying apply by default only against melee attacks.
- They do not defend against ranged or magical attacks unless a specific item, artefact, ability, or other explicit effect grants that capability.
- Their precise resolution, action cost, mitigation, and distinction are still open.

# 3. Damage and armour

## Armour profiles

Armour has separate physical and magical defence values. The intended archetypes are:

- **Heavy armour:** stronger physical defence, weaker magical defence.
- **Light armour:** stronger magical defence, weaker physical defence.
- **Medium armour:** a more balanced distribution between physical and magical defence.

These are intended profiles, not final numerical ratios or a restriction that every individual item must follow them exactly.

## Defence conversion

- The total relevant defence values from equipped items are converted into a percentage reduction of the corresponding damage type.
- Physical and magical damage reduction are calculated separately.
- Each reduction has an upper cap so ordinary equipment cannot produce complete immunity.
- A non-linear conversion from rating to damage reduction is a candidate direction inspired by the general approach discussed for Path of Exile 2; no external game's formula is adopted as-is.
- Exact conversion curves, caps, penetration, and interactions with buffs/debuffs remain open.

# 4. Health, endurance, and negative states

## Health

Maximum health consists conceptually of:

- a base health value;
- a contribution from Endurance;
- additional bonuses from items, skills, abilities, and effects.

The formula and scale are open.

## Endurance in combat

- Endurance is **not** a combat action resource spent on attacks, blocks, parries, or evasion.
- Its combat role includes contributing to resistance against negative states.
- Higher Endurance makes negative states expire more quickly.
- This applies to both physical states (for example, stun, poison, sleep) and magical states (for example, paralysis, curses, magical bindings).
- The exact duration/resistance model and any state-specific exceptions remain open.

The previously agreed non-combat role of Endurance as a finite stamina pool for stamina-consuming physical activities remains unchanged.

## Critical outcomes

Critical outcomes may amplify damage or the magnitude/duration of an effect. A multiplier such as ×1.5 is illustrative, not approved numerically.

Critical chance, critical damage/effect multipliers, eligibility by attack type, and resistance to critical outcomes remain open.

# 5. Initiative and action-point economy

## Approved principles

- Combat is turn-based and takes place in the Combat Arena model already established by [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md) and [10 Turn Action Economy](10%20Turn%20Action%20Economy.md).
- There is no separate Attack Speed attribute.
- Turn order is determined by **Initiative**.
- Initiative depends on Dexterity, Perception, and applicable modifiers from items, skills, and effects.
- Each character receives a pool of **Action Points (AP)** for their turn.
- Every action consumes AP. Examples include movement, attacks, abilities, and other combat actions.
- Base AP is supplemented by bonuses from items and skills.
- Dexterity is the primary attribute for natural AP growth.

## Still open

- Base AP and its range.
- AP gained per Dexterity point and any limits/diminishing returns.
- Whether all actions use one common AP pool or some actions have special costs.
- Whether unused AP carries over (no decision yet).
- Whether any action can be interrupted, repeated, or performed outside the acting character's turn.
- Exact Initiative formula and tie-breaking.

The earlier Move + Action + Bonus structure in document 10 is a provisional older model and must not be treated as final alongside this AP model.

# 6. Range and targeting

- Weapon range is defined by the weapon's own properties.
- Magical range and area are defined by the description/rules of the relevant spell or ability.
- There is no general-purpose attribute or generic modifier that freely increases or decreases attack range.
- Any exception must be explicit in the weapon, ability, item, or effect itself.

# 7. Positioning and arena geometry

The desired tactical features are:

- flanking attacks;
- attacks from behind;
- attacks from higher elevation;
- cover.

Flanking, rear attacks, and attacks from elevation should increase hit chance and critical chance.

Cover obstructs aim and reduces the accuracy of ranged attacks against a protected target.

The exact geometry, facing rules, flanking detection, elevation bands, cover levels, and numerical modifiers remain open. Existing arena-grid constraints in [10.1 Grid World and Environment Actions](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) remain applicable.

# 8. Equipment durability

- Equipment has a durability value.
- Durability is consumed through use; more frequent use causes faster wear.
- Reduced durability does **not** progressively reduce the item's combat or functional effectiveness.
- The consequences of reaching zero durability, repair rules, and whether every equipment category uses durability remain open.

# 9. Defeat, death, surrender, and bargaining

## Player character

- Losing a fight can result in the death of the current character in the world.
- After death, the player can create a new character, consistent with the Character Life / Legacy framework.
- Some encounters may offer an alternative to death, such as paying money, surrendering part of the equipment, or being taken prisoner.
- These alternatives are contextual possibilities, not a guarantee that every defeat can be negotiated away.

## NPCs

- NPCs may ask for mercy or surrender when they judge defeat likely.
- The more one-sided the fight is, the greater the likelihood that an NPC seeks mercy.
- In a close fight with an uncertain outcome, an NPC may continue fighting rather than surrender.
- The player may initiate dialogue during combat and demand something in exchange for sparing an opponent, even if the NPC has not already asked for mercy.
- Possible terms include money, equipment, information, or other contextually meaningful concessions.

The intended interaction is inspired by the surrender/bargaining option in Space Rangers, not a requirement to reproduce that game's exact implementation.

This extends the existing Yield / Spare / Break Away principles in [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md) and [10 Turn Action Economy](10%20Turn%20Action%20Economy.md); those documents need a later consistency pass.

# 10. Explicitly not included / rejected

- No separate Attack Speed attribute.
- No standalone Evasion skill.
- No Endurance expenditure for ordinary combat actions.
- No default blocking/parrying of ranged or magical attacks.
- No progressive loss of item effectiveness as durability decreases.
- No general stat or effect that freely changes weapon/spell range.
- No combat XP or kill-based level progression; existing Confront constraints remain binding.

# 11. Remaining design questions

These are intentionally unresolved and should be addressed in focused sessions:

1. **Damage model:** What damage types exist beyond the broad physical/magical split? Are there subtypes with distinct armour/resistance interactions?
2. **Weapon damage:** How are base weapon damage, Strength, weapon proficiency, abilities, and critical outcomes combined?
3. **Magical attack resolution:** Which attributes besides Perception affect accuracy, and how do spell/ability skill and power affect damage?
4. **Blocking and parrying:** What is the functional difference? Do they fully prevent damage, mitigate it, or create counterattack opportunities?
5. **Action costs:** What AP costs do movement, attacks, abilities, defence, dialogue, and item use have? Can a character spend AP on reactions?
6. **Initiative:** What is the exact ordering model, and how are ties handled?
7. **Positioning:** How are facing, flanking, rear attacks, elevation, line of sight, and cover represented on the arena grid?
8. **Critical outcomes:** Are critical hits and critical status effects one shared roll or separate possibilities?
9. **Negative states:** Does Endurance shorten all durations uniformly, or does each state have its own resistance/duration rules? Can a state be resisted entirely?
10. **Death alternatives:** Which surrender/mercy outcomes are possible for players and NPCs, and what determines whether negotiation is available?
11. **Durability:** What happens at zero durability, and which item categories can break?
12. **Healing and recovery:** How are health, wounds, and negative states recovered outside and after combat?

# 12. Design constraints for the next pass

- Keep accuracy, evasion, blocking/parrying, armour mitigation, and negative-state resistance as distinct layers.
- Do not let one attribute dominate every combat outcome.
- Preserve meaningful differences between weapon skills, character attributes, equipment, and positioning.
- Do not introduce a new skill unless it has a clear gameplay purpose and a credible progression path.
- Keep Confront costly and consequential; it must not become the default best solution over the other Approaches.
- Design the mathematical model only after the remaining combat rules and desired combat experience are sufficiently specified.
- Validate the eventual formulas through automated simulations and edge-case testing, then confirm feel through playtesting.
