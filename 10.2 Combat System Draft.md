# Purpose and authority

This document records the combat design decisions explicitly approved by the project owner during the design pass dated 2026-10-30.

It is a design draft, not a complete combat specification and not implementation authorisation. Approved principles below are treated as decisions; formulas, numeric values, edge cases, and unresolved mechanics remain open.

This document is the current combat action-economy and combat-system authority. The older Move + Action + Bonus draft has been retired; do not use or reintroduce that structure.

# 1. Attack accuracy and hit resolution

## Approved principles

- Hit probability is based on the relationship between the attacker's offensive capability and the target's defensive capability, together with a base accuracy value and relevant situational modifiers.
- Attributes do not act in isolation: the relevant weapon/ability skill, equipment, effects, and circumstances also contribute.
- **Perception contributes to the accuracy of every attack explicitly targeted at an opponent**, including melee, ranged, and targeted magical attacks.
- The target's ability to avoid an attack is represented primarily through Dexterity and Perception, with modifiers from items and effects.
- A target that cannot meaningfully evade (for example, because it is immobilised, stunned, or bound) may have its evasion reduced to zero.
- A ranged attack may still miss an immobile target because the attacker's ranged accuracy and weapon skill still matter.
- There is no separate universal Attack Speed attribute.
- Attribute and skill progression, secondary-attribute bonuses, and Luck's relative probability modifier follow the shared foundations in [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md). Combat-specific attack and defence formulas remain governed by this document and must be reconciled with that shared model.

## Melee

The approved conceptual comparison is:

- Attacker: Strength + Dexterity + Perception + relevant weapon proficiency + applicable modifiers.
- Target: Dexterity + Perception + applicable evasion modifiers.

Strength is part of the attacker's melee accuracy contribution as well as being relevant to physical damage; accuracy and damage remain separate calculations.

Cover gives no advantage in melee.

An attack from Stealth Mode strikes before Initiative is rolled and rolls to hit as usual. On a hit it adds 50 percentage points to the calculated critical chance. It never stuns ([10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md)).

## Ranged and magical attacks

Perception contributes to the accuracy of all targeted attacks. The remaining offensive attributes and skills depend on the attack type and its eventual rules. Do not assume that melee Strength applies to ranged or magical attacks.

Cover hinders ranged and magical attacks, because it is a problem of aiming ([10.1](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md)). How much it hinders is open.

## Base values and formula

A base accuracy / base evasion model is intended. Working values are in §5b. The final probability function, caps/floors, scaling, and the exact contribution of each attribute/skill are not decided.

# 2. Defence

## Evasion

- Evasion applies against melee, ranged, and magical attacks.
- Evasion is derived from relevant attributes, principally Dexterity and Perception, and can be modified by equipment and effects.
- There is **no standalone Evasion skill**. The owner rejected it because there is no sufficiently clear, abuse-resistant, meaningful way to train it.
- Character states such as immobilisation, stun, or binding can reduce or remove the ability to evade.
- Ranged attacks retain an independent chance to miss even when the target cannot evade.

## Blocking and parrying

- Blocking and parrying apply by default only against melee attacks.
- They do not defend against ranged or magical attacks unless a specific item, artefact, ability, or other explicit effect grants that capability.
- Blocking is available only when the character has an eligible equipped item: a shield or a two-handed weapon.
- Parrying is available only with eligible weapon types; the eligible types must be defined in the equipment/weapon rules.
- Block/parry chance and effectiveness come from the relevant equipment and bonuses granted by the corresponding ability or effect, not from a universal attribute-derived efficiency value.
- The amount of damage absorbed by a block is determined by the equipped item. The item may define this as a fixed numeric value or as a percentage of the incoming attack; the exact representation can vary by item.
- The exact distinction between block and parry, the parry outcome, and the precise chance/mitigation calculations remain open.

# 3. Damage and armour

## Armour profiles

## Damage types

Combat uses exactly two damage types:

- **Physical damage:** damage caused by weapons, physical attacks, and abilities explicitly defined as physical.
- **Magical damage:** damage caused by spells and magical abilities. Magical damage is one unified type; it is not subdivided into fire, cold, lightning, chaos, or other elemental categories.

An attack's delivery method and damage type are separate concepts. Weapon attacks and spells may be defined independently of whether their damage is physical or magical. For example, a physical ability may deal physical damage, while a spell may deal magical damage. The rules of a specific ability determine its damage type.

There are no separate fire, cold, lightning, or chaos damage types, and no separate resistances for those categories.

## Armour and damage resistance

- Equipment provides physical defence and/or magical defence.
- Owner's direction, 2026-11-03: the armour family decides the split. Cloth gives very high magical defence and very low physical defence. Leather gives half and half. Plate gives physical defence only, with no magical defence. So a full set of cloth guards well against magic, and a full set of plate guards badly against it. Chain, as metal, stands near plate (working reading). Values are in §5b.
- The total relevant defence values from equipped items are converted into percentage damage reduction, calculated separately for physical and magical damage.
- Each reduction has an upper cap so ordinary equipment cannot provide complete immunity.
- A non-linear conversion from defence rating to damage reduction may be considered, taking inspiration from the general defensive approach in Path of Exile 2. No external game's formula is adopted as-is.
- Working conversion and cap are in §5b. Final curves, caps, penetration, and interactions with buffs/debuffs remain open.
- Owner's direction, 2026-11-02: arrows and bullets do not pierce physical armour. Physical defence counts double against a ranged weapon's physical damage (§5b).

The adopted inspiration from Path of Exile 2 is limited to the general idea of distinct damage types and corresponding defensive mitigation. The project's actual combat model has only physical and magical damage, each with its own defence value.

# 4. Health, endurance, and negative states

## Health

Maximum health consists conceptually of:

- a base health value;
- a contribution from Endurance;
- additional bonuses from items, skills, abilities, and effects.

The formula and scale are open. The shared resource and derived-statistics structure is recorded in [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md).

Health is an important, deliberately scarce resource. Natural recovery during ordinary activity is very slow: **1% of maximum HP per 10 world ticks** under the owner-approved ordinary recovery rule (2026-10-31), unless a condition or effect blocks recovery. Sleep advances 6–8 in-game hours and fully restores ordinary HP only if no active condition or effect blocks recovery. Poison blocks HP and stamina recovery while active; Bleeding continues to remove HP and can kill the character during sleep. First Aid is a profession for treating specific conditions and injuries (such as stopping bleeding, applying dressings, antidotes, and splints), not a universal HP-restoration system. No HP-restoring potions are part of the current design. Doctors and appropriate medical specialists provide more complete treatment. See [18 World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md). Exact rates, treatment requirements, and item rules remain open.

## Will Power resource

Arcana and supernatural abilities spend a shared visible Will Power resource. Will is the governing 1–100 attribute; Will Power is the spendable pool. Arcana determine ability access, while Will determines capacity. Maximum capacity is \((20+0.8\times Will)\times S\), where S is the current Limit multiplier (defined in [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md)). Ordinary regeneration is approximately 1% of maximum per 10 world ticks; sleep normally restores the pool fully unless an active effect blocks recovery.

Reaching zero Will Power through ability use triggers full exhaustion: one modest Will-progression reward per continuous exhaustion episode, plus Sanity damage scaled by Limit and mitigated by Will. Ability costs, exact exhaustion XP/eligibility, and the base Sanity-damage value remain balance parameters. The complete rules and Limit multipliers are defined in [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md). A character can also pour Will Power into a weapon through Infusion (§5b).

## Endurance in combat

- Endurance is **not** a combat action resource spent on attacks, blocks, parries, or evasion.
- Its combat role includes contributing to resistance against negative states.
- Higher Endurance makes negative states expire more quickly.
- This applies to both physical states (for example, stun, poison, sleep) and magical states (for example, curses and magical bindings).
- The exact duration/resistance model and any state-specific exceptions remain open.

The non-combat Stamina rules are defined in [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md): ordinary walking and ordinary running are free; accelerated running, climbing, gathering/physical work, and other designated exertion consume Stamina. Carrying high or overloaded loads increases exertion cost. Stamina regenerates at 5% of maximum per world tick while no Stamina-consuming exertion is being performed, and regeneration pauses during such exertion.

## Combat conditions and damage-over-time effects

Combat uses a compact catalogue of conditions rather than a large elemental ailment system. Conditions are consequences of attacks, abilities, poisons, wounds, and other events; they are not additional damage types.

Conditions may be physical or magical in origin. The origin describes how a condition is inflicted, not a separate version of its gameplay effect. For example, a physical trap and a magical binding may both apply Immobilisation. Each condition must have a distinct, understandable gameplay consequence. Do not create multiple condition names for effects that are functionally the same.

### Approved core conditions

- **Poison:** does not directly reduce health. While poisoned, the character cannot recover health or stamina. It has no guaranteed expiry: at each world tick it may be removed by a probability check influenced by Endurance, treatment, and relevant items. If the check fails, Poison persists until a later successful check or active treatment. Sleep does not guarantee removal.
- **Bleeding:** causes continuing health loss at world-time updates while active. If left untreated, it can reduce health to zero and cause death, including during sleep. It has no guaranteed expiry: each world tick may remove it through a probability check influenced by Endurance, treatment, and relevant items. Bleeding does not by itself prevent movement, attacks, or dialogue.
- **Stun:** completely disables the character for the duration of the condition. The character cannot move, attack, use abilities, perform defensive reactions, or initiate/participate in dialogue. This replaces the previously considered separate Paralysis condition: Paralysis is not retained as a separate condition because its proposed action restriction overlapped with Stun.
- **Immobilisation:** prevents use of the character's legs for movement. The character cannot walk, run, or perform other actions that require changing position, but may still use their hands, eyes, and speech. They may use ranged weapons and abilities that do not require movement, provided their other requirements and AP are met.
- **Disarm:** prevents the character from using weapons or other held combat equipment that the condition has made unavailable. It does not disable the hands themselves: the character may still move, speak, use unarmed actions, and cast or use abilities that do not require the unavailable weapon or item. A staff-dependent spell, for example, cannot be used while the required staff is unavailable; a spell that requires no such implement remains usable.
- **Blindness:** reduces Perception for the purpose of detecting targets and other things to approximately zero while the condition lasts. It also reduces the accuracy of attacks that rely on the character's perception of a target. This is a gameplay/stat effect; it does not require a separate world-visibility or fog-of-war simulation. Exact numerical handling remains open.
- **Disorientation:** reduces attack accuracy and Initiative while active. It does not prevent the character from acting, moving, or speaking. Exact penalties and sources remain open.
- **Fear:** imposes restrictions or penalties in relation to the source of fear, rather than universally disabling the character. Possible consequences include reduced effectiveness against the source or an inability/unwillingness to approach it. The exact behaviour must be defined by the specific fear effect and encounter context; it should not be conflated with the separate sanity/chaos system.

### Deferred candidate

- **Weakness** is not included in the approved core catalogue for now. A generic reduction to physical or magical damage can be represented as a direct modifier/effect where needed. A separate Weakness condition should be introduced only if it gains a distinct gameplay role that cannot be expressed by such a modifier.

### Base durations and shared game time

The current owner-approved starting proposal for condition durations is defined in [18 World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md):

- Stun and Fear: 2 world ticks.
- Immobilisation, Disarm, Blindness, and Disorientation: 3 world ticks.
- Poison and Bleeding: no fixed expiry; they persist until actively removed or naturally overcome.
- One complete tactical round advances the shared world clock by exactly one in-game minute; the world does not advance during an individual character's turn. The wider simulation updates at the end of the round.

These are initial design/balance values, not final tuned durations. Endurance, equipment, abilities, and temporary effects influence resistance to initial application and the chance of early removal. The exact per-condition probability curves remain open. Poison and Bleeding have no natural-removal chance at zero Endurance under the current proposal.

### Condition design rules

- Immobilisation and Disarm are independent restrictions: one limits the use of legs/movement, the other limits use of held weapons/equipment. Neither implies the other.
- Conditions should restrict only the relevant capability. Immobilisation does not prohibit hand use; Disarm does not prohibit movement or all hand use.
- Conditions resolve on shared world ticks. During tactical combat, one complete round produces one world tick; outside combat, ticks follow the shared world clock. Individual player deliberation during a turn does not advance time.
- Exact duration, application chance, treatment/removal methods, stacking, refresh, and interactions remain open unless explicitly stated above.
- Health recovery is deliberately slow and important. Poison blocks health and stamina recovery; Bleeding actively removes health. These effects must remain mechanically distinct.
- Do not add separate chill, freeze, shock, electrocute, ignite, or similar elemental ailment systems. Fire, cold, and lightning are not damage types or condition families in this game's combat model.

## Sanity

Sanity is a separate 0–100 measure of mental integrity, not a combat damage type, resistance value, or synonym for Will Power. Combat and supernatural encounters may cause Sanity damage through fear, curses, mental attacks, entities, or other explicitly defined effects. A source declares its base Sanity impact and whether the shared Will mitigation applies.

Full exhaustion of Will Power also causes Sanity damage. Its amount scales with the current Limit multiplier and is reduced by Will, as defined in [07.2 Derived Character Statistics](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md).

At Sanity 0, the character loses their human personality and becomes a game-controlled creature in the world; this is not ordinary death. The character may retain abilities, equipment, and artefacts and may be encountered by other characters. Sanity bands, mitigation formulas, exhaustion consequences, and recovery principles are specified in [07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md); exact symptoms, penalties, and balance values remain open.

Chaos is not a combat damage type and has no separate combat resistance value. Do not create a second “chaos influence” meter alongside Sanity.

## Critical outcomes

Critical outcomes may amplify damage or the magnitude/duration of an effect. The working multiplier is ×2 (§5b).

Conditional critical chance follows the shared Perception + Luck model and 95% cap in [07.1 Character Attributes, Skills, and Action Checks](07.1%20Character%20Attributes,%20Skills,%20and%20Action%20Checks.md). Excess chance above the cap adds to the critical damage multiplier one to one, so an excess of 0.50 adds 0.5 ([10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md)). Whether it also strengthens critical status effects remains open. Eligibility by attack type and resistance to critical outcomes also remain open.

# 5. Initiative and action-point economy

## Approved principles

- Combat is turn-based and takes place in place on the Combat Grid established by [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md) and [16 Architecture Pivot](16%20Architecture%20Pivot.md).
- There is no separate Attack Speed attribute.
- Turn order is determined by **Initiative**.
- Initiative depends on Dexterity, Perception, and applicable modifiers from items, skills, and effects.
- Each character receives a pool of **Action Points (AP)** for their turn.
- The base AP value is fixed and identical for all characters.
- Dexterity gradually increases a character's AP beyond the common base value; this is gradual growth rather than a threshold-based step system. The exact rate and limits are open.
- Items and skills may provide additional AP bonuses.
- Every action consumes AP. Examples include movement, attacks, abilities, and other combat actions.
- Action costs are variable and may differ by action, weapon, or ability. The exact cost table and any cost modifiers are open.
- AP remaining when a character's turn ends is retained only for defensive reactions outside that character's turn. It cannot be spent on ordinary actions after the turn has ended.
- At the beginning of the character's next turn, any AP remaining from the previous turn expires and the character receives a fresh full AP pool.
- Defensive reactions outside the character's own turn consume AP from the retained defensive remainder. Once that remainder is exhausted, the character cannot pay AP for further reactions until their next turn refreshes the pool.
- A character can Wait, moving its turn to the end of the round, or end its turn in a Defensive Stance instead of attacking, and answer an attacker with a Counterattack. Owner-approved on 2026-11-02; the rules are in §5b.

## Still open

Working values for base AP, its gain from Dexterity, action costs, and Initiative are in §5b.

- Any cap or diminishing returns on the AP gain from Dexterity.
- Cost modifiers for actions, weapons, and abilities.
- Which defensive reactions are available in each situation, their individual AP costs, and whether some reactions require particular equipment or abilities.
- Whether defensive AP can be reserved or otherwise restricted during the character's own turn. The currently approved rule is that any AP left at the end of the turn is available for defensive reactions; no separate reservation step has been established.
- Whether any action other than a reaction or a Counterattack can be interrupted, repeated, or performed outside the acting character's turn.

The retired Move + Action + Bonus structure is not part of the current design.

# 5a. Battle scale and NPC turns

Owner-approved:

- Battles should last about as long as an army battle in Heroes of Might and Magic III or Olden Era, with cells, Initiative, and a number of rounds.
- NPCs take their turns quickly, by fixed rules such as choosing the nearest target and how to move and attack. There is no machine-learning AI in combat.
- A group's Force Estimate decides whether it fights, flees, or stays out ([21](21%20Cult%20Campaign%20and%20City%20Control.md)).
- The same target rules decide whether an enemy follows the player through a door: it does so if the player is still its priority and the door is the nearest valid way.
- Owner-approved on 2026-11-02: fighters find the nearest targets and close in. A melee fighter that sees shooters tries to reach them. If it cannot reach the shooter on its way but reaches a melee fighter, it attacks that fighter first and still tries for the shooter next time. Supports are treated the same way. Each side goes first for whoever brings the most to the fight, considering how far it can actually harm that target.

## Target Rules V0

Working readings of that direction, tried in `tools/balance/tactical_sim.py`. They draw on Heroes III as VCMI rebuilds it, on Larian's published combat AI, and on Space Rangers 2, and they stay fixed rules.

- **Target Impact.** An NPC scores each enemy as the harm that enemy does to its side in a turn, plus the worth of its abilities, multiplied by the share of that enemy's HP the NPC can take off in a turn. A target it can barely harm therefore scores low, however dangerous it is.
- Each full turn of walking needed multiplies the score by 0.7. A target it can kill now counts ×2.5, a target asleep or losing its turn ×0.2, and a target with three of the NPC's allies already beside it ×0.5.
- A melee fighter goes for its best target. If it will not reach that target this turn and an enemy stands next to it, it attacks that enemy, and it chooses again next turn.
- A shooter shoots its best target in range, at −20 to hit while an enemy stands next to it.
- A control ability goes to the enemy with the highest Target Impact in reach.
- **Held.** A step that leaves every awake enemy next to a fighter costs 2 AP more. There is no opportunity attack.
- **Waiting.** Owner-approved on 2026-11-02: a melee NPC that can strike nobody this turn waits if a melee enemy still to act will walk into its reach. It then strikes at the end of the round, and again at the start of the next if it goes first there.
- **Hold Back.** A working reading added on 2026-11-02, because of what Waiting did alone: a melee NPC that can strike nobody this turn stops one cell short of the reach of any melee enemy still to act, waiters included, and stands in a Defensive Stance with the AP it has left. Without this rule a side with three Initiative modifiers won 90 % of fights, because the slower side walked under a double blow.

# 5b. Combat Numbers V0

Authoring defaults calculated on 2026-11-02, because the owner left balance calculations to the agent. They are starting values for the slice, checked by `tools/balance/combat_sim.py`, and they are meant to change in playtest. They give working values for the questions in §11 and do not close them.

## Attack

- **Melee accuracy:** `((Strength + Dexterity + Perception) / 3 + weapon skill) / 2`.
- **Ranged accuracy:** `((Dexterity + Perception) / 2 + weapon skill) / 2`.
- **Magical accuracy:** `(Intelligence + Will + Perception) / 3`. Not simulated yet.
- **Evasion:** `(Dexterity + Perception) / 2`, or zero for a target that cannot evade.
- **Hit chance:** `(70 % + Accuracy − Evasion + situational modifiers) × (1 + Luck / 100)`, in percentage points, from 5 % to 95 %. Peers hit about 71 % of the time at every Limit.

## Damage

- **Stage Factor:** `1 + 0.35 × (9 − item stage)`. It multiplies weapon base damage and armour values.
- **Physical damage:** `weapon base × Stage Factor × (1 + Strength / 50)`, ±15 %. Ranged weapons take no Strength.
- **Magical damage:** `ability base × (1 + (Intelligence + Will) / 100)`, ±15 %. Not simulated yet.
- **Damage reduction:** `defence / (defence + 50)`, up to 75 %, separately for physical and magical defence.
- **Physical defence against ranged attacks:** counts double against a ranged weapon's physical damage, by the owner's direction of 2026-11-02. Plate at 24 therefore stops 32 % of a blow and 49 % of a bullet, and leather at 8 stops 14 % and 24 %.
- **Critical hit:** ×2, plus any excess over the 95 % cap. Owner-approved on 2026-11-02, up from ×1.5. A critical melee blow also halves the target's physical defence for that blow, because it finds the gap in the armour. A shot does not.

## Firearms

Owner's direction, 2026-11-02: the style is Victorian. Staffs and wands are cancelled, so there is no standard fireball, and the crossbow is removed. Gunpowder rifles and pistols join swords, daggers, and hand weapons. Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: the bow and the mini-crossbow are removed too. Every weapon is iron: a melee weapon or a firearm, in one hand or two. The values are authoring defaults.

- **Shot and Reload.** A firearm holds one shot, and it starts a fight loaded. A shot costs its attack AP, and **Reload** costs its reload AP before the next shot. A Reload can be spread over turns, so a fighter short of AP still gets there.
- **A rifle** fills a turn of 10 AP with one shot and one Reload, so its owner fires once a round. It takes both hands.
- **A pistol** has a short range, takes one hand, and needs 8 AP for a shot and a Reload.
- **Next to an enemy,** a rifle shoots at −10 to hit. A pistol has no such penalty.
- **A pistol with something else.** Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: a pistol can be held with a one-handed weapon, a shield, or a second pistol, and every firearm starts a fight loaded. Reload needs a free hand. Putting an item away or drawing it costs 2 AP. So a sword and a pistol give one shot at the start of a fight, and two pistols give two shots and then one pistol, once the other is put away.
- **The off hand.** Authoring defaults: a shot from the off hand is at −20 to hit and does half damage, and a fighter with a pistol in the off hand cannot parry. A pistol held with a shield is the main weapon and pays none of this. Without this price the opening shot raised a critical build from 46 % to 74 % and a mage with two pistols from 36 % to 61 %, measured before the armour split, so every fighter would have carried one. With it, a pistol in the off hand is a trade: it helps against mages and shooters and costs against melee fighters (table of *Kinds of fighter*). In `tactical_sim.py` a fighter never puts the off-hand pistol away, either to reload or to parry again.
- **No ammunition in V1.** Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: V1 counts neither cartridges nor arrows, and Reload is the price of shooting. Ammunition as an item may come in V2, once shop prices (CC-48) exist.

## Infusion

Owner-approved on 2026-11-02: a character can fill a weapon with magic through Will Power, with no spell. Besides its physical damage the blow then deals magical damage. Characters who play through Intelligence use the same weapons as everyone else, and this repays their weak Strength and Dexterity. Their Will lets them do it more often, and their blow goes through heavy armour better, because plate has little magical defence. The values below are authoring defaults, and the rest of the wording is a working reading.

- **Cost:** 5 × S Will Power a blow, paid before the roll, so a miss spends it too. Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: Infusion is paid for each blow, never once for a whole turn, so that a weapon with many blows a turn gains no cheaper Infusion. The value 5 × S is an authoring default. The cost is flat and not a share of the maximum, so that more Will means more blows. A Limit 8 fighter with Will 10 has 32 Will Power, about five blows.
- **Damage:** `weapon base × Stage Factor × Intelligence / 24` of magical damage. Strength adds nothing to it, a critical hit multiplies it, and magical defence reduces it. Intelligence 14 adds 58 % of the weapon's base, and the Intelligence 3 of a cult fighter adds 12 %.
- **Accuracy** stays the weapon's own.
- **Iron stands in the way.** Chain halves Infusion, and plate stops it. Without this rule a mage in chain had a mean of 65 in the table of *Kinds of fighter*, because chain took the rifle's edge away.
- **Exhaustion.** An NPC stops with two blows' worth left. A player can go on, and zero Will Power means full exhaustion as usual. Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: it pays the usual Will reward of 07.2, once a continuous episode, with the usual Sanity damage. Farming it is hard, because Infusion works only in a real fight and there is no voluntary Turn-Based Mode.
- **Magical weapons.** The Magical Damage modifier of 19 §2.4 gives the same kind of damage with no Will Power, for anyone. That is what a magic pistol or a magic rifle is.
- **Who can infuse.** Owner-approved, 2026-11-03: any character who has entered an Arcana, with no training; the power comes with initiation. An Uninitiated character has no such power and gets magical damage only from a weapon with Magical Damage. In the slice the allied fighters of Жрица infuse, and the cult's fighters of Император do not, because their Intelligence is too low to be worth the Will Power.

## Blocks and parries

A character reacts to a melee hit by spending 2 retained AP. The chance comes from the item, as §2 requires.

| Item | Reaction | Chance | Damage stopped |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dagger | Parry | 30 % | All |
| One-handed weapon | Parry | 40 % | All |
| Shield | Block | 60 % | 80 % |
| Two-handed weapon | Block | 40 % | 60 % |

A two-handed attack leaves 3 AP, so it keeps one free reaction. A one-handed fighter who strikes twice keeps none, and giving up a blow for two reactions is a real choice: with a shield it nearly breaks even in a duel, and it pays when holding a place matters more than winning quickly.

## Action Points and Initiative

- **AP in a turn:** `10 + Dexterity / 20`. The fraction carries into the next turn, so the growth is gradual.
- **Initiative:** `(Dexterity + Perception) / 2 + 1d20`. A tie goes to the higher Dexterity, then at random.

## Wait and Defensive Stance

Owner-approved on 2026-11-02. The values are authoring defaults, and the rest of the wording is a working reading.

- **Wait.** As in Heroes III, a fighter can hand its turn to the end of the round, once a round. Those who waited act after everyone else, the lowest Initiative first, so if two fighters both wait, the slower one goes first. The next round runs in the usual order again. AP stay in the fighter's pool while it waits, and a reaction in that time spends them.
- **Defensive Stance.** A fighter that has not attacked this turn can end the turn in a stance, after moving if it likes. Until its next turn, each AP it still holds adds 1.5 to Evasion and 3 % to its chance to block or parry. With all 10 AP that is +15 Evasion, a parry of 52 %, and a shield block of 78 %. The bonus falls as reactions spend the AP.
- **Counterattack.** A fighter in a stance answers each melee attack on it, hit or miss, with a blow, for its weapon's attack AP plus 2. A Counterattack cannot itself be answered. A one-handed fighter with 10 AP pays one parry and one Counterattack, so it answers one attack a round.

Ranged attacks, abilities, and a ritual going on elsewhere all pass a stance by. A fighter that only stands in a stance against one that attacks, in a duel, on `tools/balance/item_sim.py`:

| Weapon | Wins |
|---|---:|
| One-handed | 45 % |
| Shield | 54 % |
| Two-handed | 45 % |
| Dagger | 16 % |

So a stance gives real protection and is not a best move: it buys time, and with a shield it holds a little better than even. Tried without the +2 on a Counterattack, and even with a smaller bonus, a shield won 64 % and a one-handed weapon 52 %, which made standing still the better answer.

## Will Contest

An ability that a target resists by Will uses `(50 % + the caster's higher of Intelligence and Will − the target's Will) × (1 + Luck / 100)`, from 5 % to 95 %. The base is lower than a hit's because losing a turn costs more than taking a blow.

Abilities that do not name a cost use 15 % of maximum Will Power for an ordinary ability and 33 % for a powerful one.

## Why one fighter decided the fight

Measured on `combat_sim.py` at 3,000 runs a line, because the owner asked why one man with a club moved the result so far.

- With no positions the fight is a race of total damage against total HP. Everyone reaches anyone, random targets spread the damage, and nobody falls until late: the winners of the main battle keep 4.3 of 5 fighters. One more fighter therefore adds blows and HP at full value. This is Lanchester's square law, where strength is fighters squared times quality, so going from 5 to 6 adds 44 %.
- Many small dice leave little luck. Limit 9 fighters with no items go 52 % at 5 against 5, 3 % at 5 against 6, and 97 % at 6 against 5.
- An uninitiated man is close to Limit 9 by design: 24 HP against 25, a blow of 4.5 against 5.6, and a 70 % base hit that dwarfs small differences in attributes. That fighter's damage alone adds 25 points and the body alone 12.
- Flanking is not the cause. With no flank bonus one club still takes the chain from 32 % to 62 %.

- On 2026-11-03, by the owner's direction, cloth went to 2 and 20, leather to 8 and 8, and plate to 24 and 0. The tables above are measured with these values.
- A shot from the off hand is at −20 and does half damage, and a pistol in the off hand takes away parrying, because with neither the opening shot of a sword and pistol raised a critical build to 74 %.

# 6. Range and targeting

- Weapon range is defined by the weapon's own properties.
- Magical range and area are defined by the description/rules of the relevant spell or ability.
- There is no general-purpose attribute or generic modifier that freely increases or decreases attack range.
- Any exception must be explicit in the weapon, ability, item, or effect itself.

# 7. Positioning and battlefield geometry

The desired tactical features are:

- flanking attacks;
- attacks from behind;
- attacks from higher elevation;
- cover.

Flanking, rear attacks, and attacks from elevation should increase hit chance and critical chance.

Cover obstructs aim and reduces the accuracy of ranged attacks against a protected target.

Working modifiers are in §5b. The exact geometry, facing rules, flanking detection, and elevation bands remain open. Existing Combat Grid constraints in [10.1 Grid World and Environment Actions](10.1%20Grid%20World%20and%20Environment%20Actions.md) remain applicable.

# 8. Equipment durability

- Equipment with durability loses it through use and damage; partial wear does **not** progressively reduce item effectiveness.
- At zero durability, an item is broken and unusable. It remains present and does not disappear; it can be repaired.
- Item Creation/repair boundaries and the current durability rule are in [19 Items, Crafting, Inventory, Equipment, and Enchanting](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md).
- Durability applies to hand-slot equipment, all pieces in the worn armour set, and resource-gathering tools; jewellery and belts have none. Repair uses a portion of ordinary/basic ingredients required for the item's creation, with no rare ingredient. A zero-durability item remains present and unusable. It cannot be enchanted, although extraction of an unlocked modifier is allowed even from a broken item. Exact wear rates, repair fraction, method/station, and duration remain open; see [19](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md).

# 9. Defeat, death, surrender, and bargaining

## Player character

- Losing a fight can result in the death of the current character in the world.
- After death, the player can create a new character, consistent with the Character Life / Legacy framework.
- Some encounters may offer an alternative to death, such as paying money, surrendering part of the equipment, or being taken prisoner.
- These alternatives are contextual possibilities, not a guarantee that every defeat can be negotiated away.

## NPCs

- NPCs may ask for mercy or surrender when they judge defeat likely.
- The more one-sided the fight is, the greater the likelihood that an NPC seeks mercy.
- In a close fight with an uncertain outcome, an NPC may continue fighting rather than surrender.
- The player may initiate dialogue during combat and demand something in exchange for sparing an opponent, even if the NPC has not already asked for mercy.
- Possible terms include money, equipment, information, or other contextually meaningful concessions.

The intended interaction is inspired by the surrender/bargaining option in Space Rangers, not a requirement to reproduce that game's exact implementation.

This extends the existing Yield / Spare / Break Away principles in [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md) and [10.2 Combat System Draft](10.2%20Combat%20System%20Draft.md); the current combat and confrontation rules are consolidated in this document and [08 Confront Constraints](08%20Confront%20Constraints.md).

# 10. Explicitly not included / rejected

- No separate Attack Speed attribute.
- No standalone Evasion skill.
- No Endurance expenditure for ordinary combat actions.
- No default blocking/parrying of ranged or magical attacks.
- No fire, cold, lightning, or chaos damage types; combat damage is physical or magical only.
- No staffs, wands, or crossbows, by the owner's direction of 2026-11-02.
- No separate elemental resistance values or elemental ailment families such as chill, freeze, shock, electrocute, or ignite.
- Chaos influence and loss of sanity are handled by a separate sanity/chaos system, not as combat damage.
- No progressive loss of item effectiveness as durability decreases.
- No general stat or effect that freely changes weapon/spell range.
- No combat XP or kill-based level progression; existing Confront constraints remain binding.

# 11. Remaining design questions

These are intentionally unresolved and should be addressed in focused sessions. §5b gives working values for 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6, which playtest may still change:

1. **Damage model:** What are the final physical and magical damage formulas, and how do armour/physical defence and magical defence convert into damage reduction?
2. **Weapon damage:** How are base weapon damage, Strength, weapon proficiency, abilities, and critical outcomes combined?
3. **Magical attack resolution:** Which attributes besides Perception affect accuracy, and how do spell/ability skill and power affect damage?
4. **Blocking and parrying:** What is the functional difference? Do they fully prevent damage, mitigate it, or create counterattack opportunities?
5. **Action costs and reactions:** What are the AP costs of movement, attacks, abilities, defence, dialogue, and item use? Which defensive reactions can be triggered, and under what conditions?
6. **Initiative:** What is the exact ordering model, and how are ties handled?
7. **Positioning:** NPCs need a facing for the Vision Cones of [10.3 Stealth and Detection](10.3%20Stealth%20and%20Detection.md). How are facing, flanking, rear attacks, elevation, line of sight, and cover represented on the Combat Grid?
8. **Critical outcomes:** Are critical hits and critical status effects one shared roll or separate possibilities?
9. **Combat conditions:** What are the application, treatment/removal, stacking, refresh, and recovery rules for Poison, Bleeding, Stun, Immobilisation, Disarm, Blindness, Disorientation, and Fear? What are the final Endurance curves and per-condition early-removal probabilities? See [18 World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md) for the current provisional duration model.
10. **Sanity:** What exact symptoms and penalties apply at each nonzero Sanity band? Which sources use shared Will mitigation, and what are their base damage values? What are the exact recovery methods/rates and the base Sanity damage from full Will Power exhaustion?
11. **Death alternatives:** Which surrender/mercy outcomes are possible for players and NPCs, and what determines whether negotiation is available?
12. **Durability:** What are the wear rates and exact repair method/station, ordinary-ingredient fraction, and timing? Durability categories, zero-durability behavior, repair without rare ingredients, and extraction from a broken item are specified in [19 Items, Crafting, Inventory, Equipment, and Enchanting](19%20Items%2C%20Crafting%2C%20Inventory%2C%20Equipment%2C%20and%20Enchanting.md).
13. **Healing and recovery:** What are the sleep comfort bonuses, First Aid recipes and treatment actions, injury categories and penalties, and doctor/specialist services? Ordinary HP recovery is fixed at 1% of maximum per 10 world ticks ([07.2](07.2%20Derived%20Character%20Statistics.md)). Stamina recovery is fixed at 5% of maximum per world tick outside Stamina-consuming exertion; creature-specific recovery modifiers remain to be balanced. First Aid is not universal healing; no HP-restoring potions are planned. See [18 World Time and Living Simulation](18%20World%20Time%20and%20Living%20Simulation.md).

# 12. Design constraints for the next pass

- Keep accuracy, evasion, blocking/parrying, armour mitigation, and negative-state resistance as distinct layers.
- Do not let one attribute dominate every combat outcome.
- Preserve meaningful differences between weapon skills, character attributes, equipment, and positioning.
- Do not introduce a new skill unless it has a clear gameplay purpose and a credible progression path.
- Keep Confront costly and consequential; it must not become the default best solution over the other Approaches.
- Design the mathematical model only after the remaining combat rules and desired combat experience are sufficiently specified.
- Validate the eventual formulas through automated simulations and edge-case testing, then confirm feel through playtesting.
